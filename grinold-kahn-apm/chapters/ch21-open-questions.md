# Chapter 21: Open Questions

> **Source**: Grinold & Kahn, *Active Portfolio Management*, 2nd ed. (McGraw-Hill, 1999) | Chapter 21, pp. 573–576

## Core Idea

The 1999 book's final substantive chapter identifies **open questions** that the authors believed would shape the future of active management. The chapter is short and reads as a research agenda; many of the questions have been answered in the 25+ years since, but several remain open.

## The 1999 Open Questions (and 2026 Status)

1. **Factor persistence**: do factor premia persist? *Status (2026)*: yes for value, momentum, and quality; weakly for size; no for many other published factors (post-publication decay).
2. **Transaction cost dynamics**: how do costs scale with AUM and frequency? *Status*: Almgren–Chriss 2001 has become the standard.
3. **The optimal combination of fundamental and quantitative signals**: how to blend value-style and momentum-style signals? *Status*: stacking and meta-learning are the modern approaches (LdP ch06).
4. **The role of alternative data**: how to use news, social media, satellite imagery? *Status*: the alternative data industry is now $10B+ in annual spend; the academic evidence is mixed.
5. **The fund-flows feedback loop**: does the flow into a fund affect its alpha? *Status*: yes, the capacity constraint is real and binding.
6. **The integration of ESG considerations**: how to incorporate ESG into the consensus? *Status*: ESG factors are now standard in institutional consensus; the question has shifted to "how to measure the ESG premium".

## Key Takeaways

1. **The 1999 open questions have been mostly answered**, with the answers often more nuanced than the 1999 expectations.
2. **Factor persistence is the key open question for the future**, particularly under the multiple-testing haircut.
3. **The agent's research agenda** should be guided by the 2026 versions of these questions, not the 1999 ones.

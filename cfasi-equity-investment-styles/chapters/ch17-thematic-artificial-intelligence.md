# Chapter 17: Artificial Intelligence as an Investment Theme
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, *Thematic Investments* guide (2021), Ch. 5 "Artificial intelligence: il futuro è già qui" | Alessandro Stella, CFA, CESGA; plus CPR AM insert "La crescita del contactless" (Wesley Lebeau, sponsor content, attributed)

## Core Idea
AI is not one technology but a combination (sensing, understanding, acting, learning) whose core engine today is **machine learning**, itself fuelled by exploding data volumes, cheaper compute and rising R&D. For investors the theme is **cross-sectoral**: security selection should be bottom-up and look for firms where AI is a measurable earnings driver (a moat), not only the IT giants. Correlation to tech indices is positive but below one and varies widely across funds. The chapter also frames AI in asset management (four use areas, robo-advisors) and ethical/social risks.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **EU High-Level Expert Group capability framework (2018)**: AI = Sense (perceive environment, acquire data), Understand (logic rules or learned patterns), Act (physical or digital, adapting to effects), Learn (optimise from successes and failures). Use to test whether a product is "real AI" or plain automation: the learning loop is the discriminator.
- **Ladder of AI**: narrow/specific AI (today, one task or a defined set) → general AI via deep learning (many tasks, heterogeneous contexts) → superintelligence (conscious, beyond human). Investable today = narrow AI.
- **Machine-learning capabilities** (Accenture): predictive systems, search and optimisation, natural-language understanding, expert systems, knowledge representation, information retrieval, machine vision, robotics.
- **History (summer/winter cycles)**: Babbage analytical machine design 1837; Turing paper 1950; term "Artificial Intelligence" coined 1955 (Dartmouth proposal, $13,500 request to Rockefeller Foundation; workshop summer 1956); first industrial robot Unimate 1961; Hinton deep learning 2006; Second Winter (1987-2011) ended with IBM Watson winning Jeopardy! (2011) and AlexNet in the ImageNet competition (2012); AlphaGo beats Lee Sedol 2016; AlphaGo Zero learns by self-play. Investor lesson: AI funding is cyclical; hype followed by disappointment is part of the pattern.
- **Three accelerators of the theme**: (1) digitalisation boosted by the pandemic; (2) data explosion ("food of AI", quoted growth of 900% in two years; Italian Big Data market EUR 1,815m in 2020; 96% of large firms kept analytics investment during Covid); (3) more powerful algorithms, hardware, neural networks and scalable infrastructure. IDC: global AI spending $50.1bn (2020) to over $110bn (2024), CAGR above 20% for 2019-2024.
- **Macro view (PwC/DWS, 2018)**: AI could add about $15.8tn to GDP by 2030 (regional pieces: China $7.0tn = 26.1% of GDP; North America $3.7tn = 14.5%; Northern Europe $1.8tn = 9.9%; Developed Asia $0.9tn = 10.4%; Southern Europe $0.7tn = 11.5%; Latin America $0.5tn = 5.4%; rest of the world $1.2tn = 5.6%); headline in the text: about $15tn, GDP +14%, process efficiency +40%. China took about 28bn of private AI investment in 2017 (70% of global); Asia (China and India) tipped to lead on talent pool, supportive regulation and data.
- **AI in asset management (four areas)**: (1) portfolio management: faster fundamental analysis, richer scenario analysis, new strategies; (2) trading: algorithmic execution, lower transaction cost, shorter news-to-execution gap; (3) risk management: tail-risk and early-warning modelling; (4) advisory: robo-advisors. Robo-advisor pros: cheap, accessible, no human bias, handles big data; cons: limited view of risk tolerance, ignores tax and inflation, weak in crises, shifts responsibility to retail investors (CFA Institute Research Foundation 2020). Example: China Life AMC's CreditMaster screens opaque Chinese credit but leaves the decision to humans.
- **Security selection framework** (three clusters): Data collection, Computing power, Use cases (visible EPS uplift and integration across divisions). Bottom-up to find AI as a driver of growth/innovation and economic moat; avoid concentrating only on traditional IT giants; add mid and small caps from other sectors.
- **BlueStar AI Index (BAINTR)**: rule-based global total-return index; six themes (big data analytics, ML chips and hardware, internet and e-commerce services, semiconductor components and process-automation software, sensors/processors for machine vision and voice recognition, proprietary AI software or services); liquidity screens: float at least 10%, float-adjusted market cap at least $1bn, 20-day average bid-ask spread below 1%, 6-month average daily traded value at least 5M; equal-weighted, semi-annual rebalance. **Solactive Big Data Index (SOLDATAU)** is narrower (data management, analysis, collection). Italian market then: 6 ETFs on AI or Big Data (one on ML) and about ten active funds.
- **Ethics checklist** (against responsibility gap and bias): ethically informed design tied to corporate values; keep human override (e.g. take control of autonomous vehicle); privacy and security from prototype; organisation-wide understanding; avoid discrimination; explainability. Cases: Facebook ad-delivery accused of discrimination (2019); facial recognition for policing leading IBM and Amazon to pause use (2020). WEF Global AI Action Alliance launched at Davos (January 2021).
- **CPR AM insert: contactless / edge AI**: edge computing lets devices run AI locally via NPUs. Claims: Edge AI chip sales over 600M in 2020 and over 1bn in 2021; share of data processed in centralised data centres 91% in 2020, with 74% analysed on devices by 2022 (Gartner); contactless payments 65% of transactions in June 2020 vs 30% nine months earlier (BIS); South Korea "untact" plan about EUR 55bn. Edge benefits: lower bandwidth and energy, real-time decisions (autonomous cars), better privacy.

## Key Concepts
- **Narrow vs general AI**: single-task versus multi-task systems.
- **Machine learning**: systems learn from data without explicit programming; tolerant of messy data.
- **Deep learning**: layered neural networks (face recognition, vision).
- **Reinforcement/self-play learning**: AlphaGo Zero.
- **AI winter**: funding collapse after failed expectations (second winter 1987-2011).
- **Big Data**: valuable information extracted through analytics.
- **Edge computing / NPU**: local on-device AI inference.
- **Robo-advisor**: algorithmic advice platform.
- **Responsibility gap**: unclear accountability when autonomous systems err.
- **Explainability**: ability to justify an AI decision.
- **Multi-use technology**: AI affects automotive, healthcare, retail, finance, not only IT.
- **Economic moat via AI**: AI-driven earnings that competitors cannot copy.

## Mental Models
- Use **"is there a learning loop?"** to separate AI from automation.
- Use **three-cluster look-through** (data, compute, use cases) to see a fund's true AI exposure: chip makers and cloud giants versus adopters.
- Use **correlation dispersion** as a due-diligence tool: if an "AI fund" has 0.95 correlation to the tech index, you are buying tech beta with an AI label.
- Use **hype-cycle discipline**: AI has had winters; size positions assuming eventual disappointment episodes.
- Use **ethics and regulation as risk factor**: facial recognition and bias can impair business lines.

## Worked Example
Compare risk-adjusted 5-year results, Table 1 (return per unit of volatility using the cumulative-to-annualised conversion, no risk-free).
- BlueStar AI: annualised `(1 + 2.531)^(1/5) - 1 = 28.7%`; vol 19.6%; ratio **1.46**.
- NASDAQ 100: `(1 + 1.963)^(1/5) - 1 = 24.3%`; vol 19.7%; ratio **1.23**.
- MSCI World IT: 26.6% / 18.9% = **1.41**.
- Solactive Big Data: 20.1% / 25.3% = **0.79**.
- Interpretation: the broad AI index slightly beat tech on return per unit of risk over five years, but the narrower Big Data index lagged on 5Y despite leading on 1Y and 3Y; results depend on window, so check several horizons.

## Anti-patterns
- Buying an AI fund without checking its correlation and top holdings versus a tech ETF.
- Reading one-year returns of +60% as normal.
- Ignoring the overlap among AI, robotics, cloud, Big Data and digitalisation themes (thresholds are not clean).
- Holding only mega-cap IT because "they do AI": the guide recommends adding mid/small caps from other sectors.
- Assuming robo-advice solves behavioural or crisis-time suitability.
- Disregarding ethics and legal exposure.

## Practitioner Notes
- Sponsor claim (CPR AM) that NPUs double processing power monthly is rhetorical: 50x in a year implies about 5.6 doublings a year (a doubling roughly every two months), and the guide elsewhere cites Moore's Law as 18 months and as 2 years in another chapter. Use order of magnitude only.
- Guide's own caveat: AI usage in investment management is so far for repetitive support tasks; final decisions remain with humans.
- Vendor/forecast figures (PwC, IDC, MMC Ventures, Accenture) are 2017-2020 projections.

## Key Takeaways
1. AI = sense, understand, act, learn; ML is the core.
2. Three accelerators: digitalisation, data explosion, compute/algorithm progress.
3. Macro upside estimates: about 14% of global GDP by 2030, Asia and China leading.
4. Thematic index (BlueStar) is equal-weighted, liquidity-screened, semi-annual rebalance.
5. Fund correlations to tech range from about 0 to 0.97; choose by holdings.
6. Selection should be bottom-up with exposure beyond the tech giants.
7. Ethics, bias and accountability are material risks.

# Chapter 22: Definition and Composition of Emerging Markets
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, *Emerging Markets* guide (2021), Ch. 1 "La definizione e composizione di mercati emergenti" | Federico Reggianini, CFA; preface by Capital Group (sponsor, attributed)

## Core Idea
"Emerging market" is an index-provider classification, not a natural category. MSCI places each country in Frontier, Emerging or Developed using three tests (economic development, size and liquidity, market accessibility). EM equities are 13.3% of MSCI ACWI by market cap but about 39% of world GDP and about 42% of the revenues of ACWI companies, so the index weight understates real economic exposure. On the bond side, the key split is **currency of issue** (hard vs local) and **issuer type** (sovereign vs corporate); currency risk dominates, and EM debt diversifies a developed-market bond portfolio.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **MSCI market classification framework** (the three tests; a country must meet the minimum on each point)
  | Criterion | Frontier | Emerging | Developed |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | A. Economic development (GNI per capita) | none | none | 25% above World Bank high-income threshold for 3 consecutive years (USD 12,376 for 2019, Atlas method) |
  | B. Companies meeting Standard Index criteria (minimum number) | 2 | 3 | 5 |
  | Company full market cap | USD 700m | USD 1,400m | USD 2,801m |
  | Security float market cap | USD 53m | USD 700m | USD 1,400m |
  | Security liquidity (ATVR, annual traded value ratio) | 2.5% | 15% | 20% |
  | C.1 Openness to foreign ownership | at least some | significant | very high |
  | C.2 Ease of capital flows | at least partial | significant | very high |
  | C.3 Efficiency of operational framework | modest | good and tested | very high |
  | C.4 Availability of investment instruments | high | high | unrestricted |
  | C.5 Stability of institutional framework | modest | modest | very high |
  (thresholds in use for the May 2020 index review)
- **Country lists (MSCI, 2021)**: 27 emerging markets: Americas: Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Mexico, Peru; EMEA: Czech Republic, Egypt, Greece, Hungary, Kuwait, Poland, Qatar, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Turkey, UAE; Pacific/Asia: China, India, Indonesia, Korea, Malaysia, Pakistan, Philippines, Taiwan, Thailand. Developed (23): Americas 2 (Canada, US); EMEA 16 (Austria, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Ireland, Israel, Italy, Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, UK); Pacific 5 (Australia, Hong Kong, Japan, New Zealand, Singapore). (Classification is dated; MSCI reviews it annually.)
- **History**: IFC (World Bank) launched the "emerging markets" campaign in late 1986; institutional investors put about USD 50m into the first strategy; MSCI created its first EM index at end-1987. Early barriers: local-language communication, settlement, currency controls, no internet.
- **Two structural reasons EM weight rises**: (1) faster GDP growth than developed markets; (2) deepening of markets: free movement of capital, currencies and instruments, and "democratisation" (listings, distribution to wider population). Interruptions: Asian crisis 1997 and the 2014-15 slump.
- **Weight vs economy vs revenue (end-2020)**
  | Measure | EM share |
  |---|---|
  | MSCI ACWI market-cap weight | 13.3% (vs US 57.3%, Japan 6.8%, UK 3.8%, France 3.0%, Canada 2.7%, Switzerland 2.6%, Germany 2.5%, Australia 1.9%, Netherlands 1.0%, other developed 5.2%) |
  | Share of world GDP (IMF) | 39.1% (about double 20 years earlier) |
  | Revenue exposure of ACWI companies | 42.4% from EM |
  | Revenue exposure of MSCI USA | 100% US-listed but about 14.9% of revenue from EM (68.5% North America) |
  Use: listing-based geography understates EM exposure; look through to revenue.
- **MSCI EM composition (end-2020, pie labels partly garbled; see ch23 for text-confirmed weights)**: China about 38%, Taiwan about 14%, Korea about 13%, India about 10%, Brazil about 5%, others about 20%. Sector mix: Information Technology about 20%, Financials about 18%, Consumer Discretionary about 17% (the three largest). Energy and Materials, once dominant, are now small (see ch23).
- **EM debt taxonomy**: currency (hard currency vs local currency) x issuer (government vs corporate). Key point: EM states often keep large direct or indirect roles in the economy (stakes, guarantees), so analysis of corporate bonds must include state links.

- **Sovereign default rates by rating, 1975-2020 (S&P; Table 7)**: AAA and AA 0% (both currencies); A 1.62% foreign / 1.57% local; BBB 2.13% / 1.45%; BB 4.25% / 2.90%; B 14.34% / 5.87%; CCC/CC 54.23% / 30.53%. Local-currency default rates are lower because a sovereign can print its own money, but foreign-currency repayment depends on balance of payments, growth and inflation; some states default for political reasons although able to pay.

GBI-EM currency exposure: Chinese yuan 14.81%, the rest of the top currencies about 4.5% each (as reported in the guide's figure).

## Key Concepts
- **Emerging market (MSCI definition)**: country classed by MSCI between frontier and developed on size/liquidity, accessibility; development test not required.
- **ATVR**: annualised traded value ratio, a liquidity measure.
- **Frontier market**: smaller, less accessible than EM.
- **Hard-currency debt**: issued in USD/EUR; risk is default, not FX.
- **Local-currency debt**: issued in domestic currency; FX is the main risk.
- **EMBI**: JPM index for hard-currency sovereign/quasi-sovereign EM bonds.
- **GBI-EM**: JPM local-currency government bond index.
- **Market cap vs revenue geography**: listing-based vs sales-based exposure.
- **Democratisation of the economy**: retail/lower-income access to capital markets.
- **Currency risk**: largest EM bond risk driver.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- Return/vol ratio above uses annualised return divided by annualised volatility (not Sharpe; no risk-free deducted).
- Currency-risk contribution (approx., assuming additive variances): `σ_FX ≈ sqrt(11.86² - 4.26²)` = **11.07%**; the difference between unhedged and hedged EM local debt volatility is almost entirely FX.
- Hedging cost / carry effect: unhedged return exceeds hedged by `6.72 - 4.79` = **1.93 pts** a year.
- Look-through weight: `EM revenue weight (ACWI) = 42.4%` vs `EM market-cap weight = 13.3%`; ratio 3.2x.

## Mental Models
- Use **index definitions as a risk map**: knowing MSCI criteria explains why Korea and Taiwan sit in EM and Hong Kong and Singapore sit in DM.
- Use **revenue look-through** to avoid under-estimating EM exposure in a "developed" equity core.
- Use **FX first** for EM bonds: decide hard vs local before choosing countries.
- Use **hedge-or-not test**: if you want EM carry and diversification, accept FX; if you want credit-like exposure, hedge or use hard currency.
- Use **concentration check**: EM equities are 1/3 China and heavy in IT and Financials.

## Worked Example
Revenue look-through for a global investor holding MSCI ACWI (100).
- Market-cap weight in EM = 13.3 points; economic revenue exposure in EM = 42.4% of company sales, so holding ACWI already gives substantial EM revenue exposure via developed-market multinationals.
- A US-only investor (MSCI USA) has 68.5% North America revenue and about 14.9% from EM, ie. EM sales exposure roughly equal to the EM weight in ACWI.
- Interpretation: adding a pure EM sleeve raises *listing* exposure; a 10% EM allocation on top of ACWI takes EM market-cap exposure to roughly 21% (`0.9 x 13.3 + 10 = 22.0%`), but the *operational* exposure already exceeds that.

## Anti-patterns
- Using EM equities and EM local-currency bonds as substitutes: different risks (equity/credit vs FX/rates).
- Judging EM bond risk from hedged index volatility (4.26%) when your mandate is unhedged (11.86%).
- Treating "EM" as homogeneous: single-country shocks (political, policy) are large; country limits matter.
- Reading old classification lists as current (Pakistan, Greece, Argentina have shifted status in later reviews; check annual MSCI review).
- Citing correlation of "EM bonds" generically; hard vs local differ.

## Practitioner Notes
- The preface by Capital Group (sponsor) argues for active selection; treat it as advocacy.
- Series 2002-2020 includes the 2008 and 2020 crises; results depend on start date (local-currency EM bond index started in 2002).
- Check index versions: GBI-EM Global Diversified caps single countries; the guide's yuan weight of 14.81% suggests a different or earlier version.

## Key Takeaways
1. MSCI classifies by development, size/liquidity and accessibility.
2. EM = 13.3% of ACWI market cap, 39.1% of GDP, 42.4% of ACWI revenues.
3. EM equity index is concentrated: China about 38%, IT and Financials about 38% together.
4. EM bonds split by currency and issuer; FX dominates risk.
5. Local-currency unhedged EM debt: 6.72% return and 11.86% vol; hedged: 4.79% and 4.26%.
6. EM debt has near-zero correlation to Treasuries; hard and local EM debt correlate 0.80.
7. Sovereign local-currency default rates are lower than foreign-currency ones at every rating from A to CCC.

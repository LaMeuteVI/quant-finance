# Chapter 15: Healthcare Innovation
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, *Thematic Investments* guide (2021), Ch. 3 "I tematici sull'innovazione sanitaria - Healthcare innovation" | Giancarlo Sandrin, CFA; plus CPR AM insert "Tecnologie mediche, le fondamenta di un nuovo mondo" (Nicolas Picard, sponsor content, attributed)

## Core Idea
Healthcare is entering a technology-driven revolution where robotics, AI and life sciences converge. Demand rises on two drivers, the **Covid-19 pandemic** (which accelerated remote care) and **population ageing**; costs rise too, so technology is the lever for productivity and system-cost reduction. The thematic universe is broad, and managers select different segments, so funds labelled "Healthcare Innovation" are not interchangeable.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Healthcare-innovation segment map** (use as a checklist to classify a fund's exposure)
  | Segment | What is changing | Data points from the source |
  |---|---|---|
  | AI diagnostics and genomics | Earlier, more precise diagnosis; AI mines genomic and imaging data for predictive models; targeted therapies on gene-level drivers | Verily (Google) reads cardiovascular risk from a retinal photo |
  | Minimally invasive robotic surgery | Remote-connected specialists, less post-operative risk | IEO (Milan): over 1,000 robotic interventions a year of over 14,000 globally cited, +25% annual growth; robotic procedures 7,188 over 2006-2016 (cited figure); Da Vinci: 4,500+ systems installed, Intuitive Surgical about $65bn market cap (May 2020), 2,750+ patents |
  | DNA sequencing and gene therapies | Sequencing cost falling; faster virus sequencing speeds vaccine discovery; CRISPR/Cas9 genome editing (2020 Chemistry Nobel to Charpentier and Doudna) | First genome (2001) about $100m; today about $1,000 |
  | 3D-printed implants and bioprinting | Personalised anatomical models and prostheses; prototyping; Covid-era adaptations; tissue and organ bioprinting as frontier | n.a. |
  | Wearables | Health monitoring, secure data sharing with doctors, fitness | n.a. |
  | Telemedicine | Remote consultation, online records, drug delivery | Ping An Good Doctor: 346M registered, 67M+ active users (June 2020); share of remote visits at Mayo Clinic from 4% pre-pandemic to 85% at the peak; telemedicine market estimate about EUR 27bn by 2026 (CPR AM) |
  | Digital health platforms | Digital health market over $200bn end-2020 vs $144bn in 2018 | Big-tech partnerships: Verily partnered with Sanofi, Novartis, Pfizer, Otsuka; Apple health revenue estimate $90bn by 2027 (Morgan Stanley, 2019) |
- **Medicine of the future (Picard / CEA view)**: precise, preventive, personalised, reactive, participative, digital. R&D focus: (1) multidisciplinary diagnostic/therapeutic devices, (2) personalised biotechnology (e.g. organs-on-chips), (3) a self-learning digital health system.
- **Data-privacy risk lens**: health data centralisation (e.g. France's Health Data Hub on Microsoft Azure) raises sovereignty and hacking-concentration concerns; experts argued for self-managed cloud. Treat privacy regulation as the main non-financial risk to platform business models.

## Key Concepts
- **Healthcare innovation theme**: technological advances changing the boundary of what medicine can do.
- **Medtech**: medical technology devices and software.
- **Genomics / CRISPR-Cas9**: reading and editing DNA; targeted therapies.
- **Robotic surgery**: tele-operated minimally invasive systems (Da Vinci with four arms and miniature instruments).
- **Telemedicine (B2B and B2C)**: B2B teleradiology/telepathology/telecardiology; B2C remote visits.
- **Digital health**: apps, platforms, records and AI-based services.
- **Wearables**: consumer devices that monitor health.
- **Bioprinting**: 3D printing of tissue or organs from biological material.
- **GAFA involvement**: Google, Amazon, Facebook, Apple using data scale to enter healthcare (Amazon via a JP Morgan/Berkshire cost-focused venture).
- **Ageing + pandemic demand drivers**: structural plus cyclical push.
- **Data protection**: precondition for personalised medicine.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- Cost decline of genome sequencing: `$100m (2001) / $1,000 = 100,000x`, about 17 doublings of cost-performance in 19 years.
- Ping An active-to-registered ratio: `67 / 346 = 19.4%`.
- Digital health market growth: `($200bn / $144bn)^(1/2) - 1` = about **18% a year** from 2018 to 2020 (inconsistent vendor estimates, order-of-magnitude only).
- Index stats (from ch13, 5Y): Healthcare Innovation return 36.88%, vol 18.89%, Sharpe 1.98, beta 1.01, Sortino 4.44, VaR95 -7.37%, max drawdown -16.02% (Q4 2018, not Q1 2020), rebound +129%; correlation with MSCI World lower than beta suggests.

## Mental Models
- Use **segment decomposition** to avoid false diversification: two healthcare-innovation funds may hold surgical robotics versus genomics.
- Use **"technology deflation in medicine"**: when sequencing, diagnostics or devices fall in cost, adoption can accelerate (as in the genome example).
- Use **pandemic as accelerant, not creator**: telemedicine adoption (4% to 85% at Mayo peak) will not hold at peak; the lasting level is the question.
- Use **regulation and privacy as valuation discount** for data-driven healthcare platforms.
- Use **big-tech partnerships** as a signal of where data and distribution will concentrate.

## Worked Example
Genome cost curve. Cost fell from $100,000,000 (2001) to about $1,000 (c. 2020), 19 years.
- Reduction factor = 100,000x; `log2(100,000) = 16.6` halvings.
- Average halving time = 19 / 16.6 = **1.14 years**, faster than Moore's Law (about 2 years per doubling).
- Interpretation: sequencing cost deflation outpaced compute deflation, which is why diagnostics/genomics models can scale; but cost per genome is not revenue per share, and price competition squeezes tool vendors.

## Anti-patterns
- Buying "healthcare innovation" through one segment (e.g. robotics) while believing you own the theme.
- Extrapolating pandemic-peak telehealth usage.
- Ignoring clinical, regulatory and reimbursement risk: tech narrative does not guarantee payer acceptance.
- Using beta alone: the index shows beta about 1 but low correlation and different drawdown dates (ch13).
- Valuing platforms without data-protection risk.

## Practitioner Notes
- The guide treats this as a long-term theme with continuing investment need (time and capex before returns).
- Check overlap with traditional healthcare funds: innovation indices skew to medtech, tools, digital health and large-cap biotech.
- Sponsor figures (CPR AM) are marketing; the segment list is the useful content.

## Key Takeaways
1. Demand: pandemic plus ageing; supply: technology raising productivity.
2. Seven segments, each with different drivers: AI diagnostics, robotic surgery, sequencing/gene therapy, 3D printing, wearables, telemedicine, digital platforms.
3. Genome sequencing cost fell about 100,000x in under 20 years.
4. Big tech and pharma partnerships shape the ecosystem.
5. Data privacy is the key structural risk.
6. Historical index performance (Sharpe near 2) reflects a rare boom period.

# Chapter 20: The Potential of Hydrogen
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, *Thematic Investments* guide (2021), Ch. 8 "La potenza dell'idrogeno" | Giancarlo Sandrin, CFA (data: BloombergNEF Hydrogen Economy Outlook, March 2020; Hydrogen Council 2017 per ch13)

## Core Idea
Hydrogen is a versatile energy carrier that returned to investor attention because it can decarbonise sectors batteries cannot reach and can store surplus renewable power. Today about **99% of hydrogen comes from fossil sources**, so the thesis depends on shifting to **green hydrogen** (electrolysis powered by renewables), which in turn depends on electrolyser costs and electricity prices. Investable exposure spans production, distribution and end-use.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Colours of hydrogen**
  | Type | Production | Cost / issue |
  |---|---|---|
  | Grey (fossil-based) | Mostly from methane; emits greenhouse gases | About $1-1.5 per kg, driven by gas price |
  | Blue | Fossil fuels with carbon capture and storage | Higher than grey because capture and storage cost |
  | Green | Electrolysis of water with an electrolyser | Cost depends on electrolyser cost and electricity price; only green if power is renewable (not coal) |
- **Hydrogen-renewables flywheel**: cheaper renewables lower green hydrogen cost; cheaper hydrogen improves the storage and utilisation of intermittent wind and solar (seasonal output swings), giving renewables a second storage channel beyond batteries. Carbon taxes are a key lever.
- **Uses (Hydrogen Economy Outlook)**: fuel (fuel-cell vehicles turn hydrogen to water and electricity, the reverse of electrolysis; power-plant fuel burning to water vapour), heat source (metallurgy, cement, aluminium, heating), feedstock (green steel using hydrogen to remove oxygen from iron ore with water as by-product; ammonia and fertilisers).
- **Policy**: Next Generation EU worth EUR 750bn funds decarbonisation projects; the source states 2021-2027 allocations of 500bn grants and 250bn concessional loans. **⚠ Correction**: official split is about EUR 390bn in grants and EUR 360bn in loans; check before quoting. ch13 cites a 2017 Hydrogen Council study: hydrogen could be 18% of final energy demand by 2050, abating 6 Gt CO2, a $2.5tn market and 30 million jobs (industry advocacy).
- **Investable value-chain map**
  1. Production: hydrogen producers/utilities, electrolyser makers, electrolyser component suppliers.
  2. Distribution: storage, transport and distribution, infrastructure providers.
  3. Users: fuel-cell makers and component suppliers, mobility providers (with or without own fuel-cell technology), key industrial-technology owners.
  4. Materials: platinum and related companies (platinum underpins water electrolysis), analogous to lithium for batteries; technology advances may cut the precious-metal need.

## Key Concepts
- **Electrolyser**: device splitting water into hydrogen and oxygen using electricity.
- **Fuel cell**: converts hydrogen and oxygen into electricity and water.
- **Grey / blue / green hydrogen**: by production route and emissions.
- **Carbon capture and storage (CCS)**: captures CO2 from production.
- **Intermittency**: variable wind and solar output, solved partly by hydrogen storage.
- **Green steel / ammonia**: hard-to-abate industrial uses.
- **Carbon tax**: lever improving green-hydrogen competitiveness.
- **Next Generation EU**: EU recovery fund with climate focus.
- **Platinum group metals**: catalysts in electrolysers and fuel cells.
- **Value-chain exposure**: production, distribution, end use.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- Cost logic: `LCOH_green ≈ (electrolyser capex annualised + O&M)/(kg produced) + (electricity price x kWh per kg)`. The source states the two drivers (electrolyser cost and electricity cost) but gives no numbers; typical electrolyser consumption is roughly 50-55 kWh per kg of hydrogen (outside the source, flagged as general knowledge).
- Grey hydrogen cost range: `$1.0-1.5/kg`.
- Index statistics (ch13): Hydrogen index (launched Nov 2018): annualised return 73.59%, volatility 36.25%, Sharpe 2.04, beta 1.46, downside deviation 13.73%, VaR95 -15.03%, max drawdown -20.08% (Jan-Mar 2020), rebound +222%; correlation to MSCI World oscillates with a 1-year window.

## Mental Models
- Use **"colour first"** to judge claims: ask what share of a company's hydrogen is green and what electricity it uses.
- Use **cost-curve intersection**: hydrogen becomes competitive when electrolyser cost and renewable power price fall below grey hydrogen plus a carbon price.
- Use **picks-and-shovels**: electrolysers, fuel cells, infrastructure, catalysts, rather than a single technology bet.
- Use **policy dependence discount**: current valuations lean on subsidies and carbon pricing.
- Use **short-history rule**: Hydrogen has less than two years of index data in the guide; treat returns as a bubble-risk signal rather than a forecast.

## Worked Example
Carbon-price needed for blue hydrogen to match grey (illustrative; source gives only the cost ranking).
- Grey cost $1.25/kg; assume CCS adds $0.50/kg (assumption) and grey emits about 10 kg CO2 per kg H2 (typical steam-methane reforming, external to source).
- Blue cost = $1.75/kg. A carbon price `P` makes grey cost `1.25 + 0.010 t/kg x P`. Break-even `1.25 + 0.010P = 1.75` gives **P = $50/t CO2** (if blue captures nearly all CO2).
- Interpretation: a carbon price near the level of EU allowances in 2021 would align blue with grey, which shows why carbon taxes are the lever the source highlights. Numbers other than the cost range are illustrative assumptions.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating all hydrogen as clean: 99% still comes from fossil routes.
- Extrapolating the Hydrogen index return (73.6% annualised from a late-2018 start).
- Ignoring electricity sourcing: green only with renewable power.
- Confusing hydrogen with batteries as competitors; they complement (long-duration storage, heavy transport).
- Investing in an unproven technology without a policy plan.

## Practitioner Notes
- At publication (early 2021) no UCITS hydrogen product existed, though providers were preparing launches (ch21).
- Chapter is brief; the numbers are market-outlook level. Use with BloombergNEF and Hydrogen Council primary data for sizing.
- Key diligence: electrolyser order backlog, margins, technology (alkaline vs PEM), platinum exposure, state subsidies.

## Key Takeaways
1. 99% of hydrogen is still produced from fossil sources.
2. Green hydrogen cost = electrolyser + renewable electricity.
3. Hydrogen can store renewable surplus and decarbonise steel, ammonia, heavy transport.
4. Value chain: production, distribution, end-use, plus materials such as platinum.
5. Policy (Next Generation EU, carbon taxes) is the catalyst.
6. Index history is short and extreme (high return, high volatility, beta 1.46).

# Chapter 23: The Equity Approach to Emerging Markets and Differences from Developed Markets
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, *Emerging Markets* guide (2021), Ch. 2 "L'approccio equity ai mercati emergenti e le differenze con mercati sviluppati" | Luca Castoldi, CFA; plus Capital Group insert "Equity emergente pronto ad accelerare" (Victor Kohn, Chapman Taylor, Kent Chan; sponsor content, attributed)

## Core Idea
The MSCI Emerging Markets index is a **market-cap-weighted mirror of the real economy**, so its composition has changed radically: from 10 countries led by Malaysia and Brazil (1988) to 27 countries led by China (about 38%), and from energy, telecoms and utilities to technology and consumer discretionary. The investment case rests on **five advantages** (growth, new trends, diversification, inefficiency, foreign capital flows) weighed against **four disadvantages** (volatility, higher-than-expected correlation, political/legal/operational risk, liquidity). Evidence is that EM delivered higher return and a better Sharpe ratio than ACWI in the last 15 years but with deeper drawdowns, and that investors, notably Italian households, are barely invested in it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Index evolution as a regime map (MSCI EM)**
  | Year | Countries in index | Top 5 (weight) | Event |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1988 | 10 | Malaysia 29.5%, Brazil 25.5%, Mexico 10.0%, Thailand 9.9%, Chile 7.8% = 82.7% | Index born |
  | 1997 | 27 | Brazil 16.8%, Mexico 13.2%, South Africa 10.3%, Taiwan 9.3%, India 6.5% (sum of components = 56.1%; source total says 55.9%) | Asian crisis |
  | 2007 | 24 | China 15.9%, Korea 14.3%, Brazil 13.4%, Russia 10.1%, Taiwan 9.9% = 63.6% | China becomes largest |
  | 2017 | 24 | China 29.7%, Korea 15.4%, Taiwan 11.3%, India 9.9%, South Africa 7.1% (components sum 73.4%; source total says 72.3%) | Asia predominant |
  | 2021 | 27 | China about 38%, Taiwan 14%, Korea 13%; Brazil 5.3% (peak above 16% in Feb 2010) | A-share inclusion |
  Key facts: weight of EM in ACWI from 0.9% to about 13% (June 2021); market cap from about USD 15bn to USD 8.6tn; more than 1,400 companies. Malaysia lost about 69% in USD in 1997 under currency devaluation and capital controls and left the index in 1998; Russian crisis added a drawdown of about 60% (1997-98).
- **Sector rotation**: 2007 energy about 18% (now about 5%), materials about 14% (now 8.6%), technology about 10% (now about 27% including communication-type names), consumer discretionary about 5% (now about 18%).
- **Top-10 turnover (Table 1)**: 1998: 7 of 10 in Latin America (Telebras, Telefonos de Mexico first); 2008: 3/3/4 split Latin America/EMEA/Asia (Petrobras, Gazprom, Vale); 2021: 8 of 10 in Asia (TSMC, Tencent, Alibaba, Samsung, Meituan...), no telecom. Top-10 weight rises 16.7% (1998) to 21.3% (2008) to about 27% (2021); number of stocks 958 → 790 → over 1,400.
- **China vs Brazil lesson**: June 2008 to mid-2021 MSCI China about +7% a year (USD), about 10 points a year ahead of Brazil, explained by the commodity boom to 2011 and bust, versus China's ability to innovate into new segments; top two MSCI China names (Tencent, Alibaba) are over 25% of the index.
- **A-shares inclusion**: domestic Chinese shares added to MSCI EM and ACWI in 2017 thanks to Stock Connect (Shanghai-Hong Kong); today about 5% of the index, heading to about 15% as the inclusion factor rises. Index changes: UAE and Qatar added 2014; Pakistan 2017; Saudi Arabia and Argentina 2019; Kuwait 2020; Argentina removed in 2021 (reclassified standalone due to default risk; Frontier in 2009); Israel upgraded to developed in 2010. Candidates: Vietnam inclusion; possible upgrade of Korea and/or Taiwan.
- **Five advantages**
  1. **Economic growth**: China second-largest GDP, projected to overtake the US in 2028 (five years earlier than pre-Covid forecasts, being the only major economy to grow in 2020); rebalancing from investment to consumption; goal to double per-capita income by 2035; households earning above USD 50,000 could quadruple in 20 years.
  2. **New trends**: domestic China sectors (consumption, technology, automation, education, health); working-age population peaked about 2010 (about 1bn) and is expected to fall at least 10% in 25 years, pushing automation, 5G, AI, robotics, e-commerce, telemedicine, mobility.
  3. **Diversification**: Markowitz logic of adding EM to a developed-market portfolio.
  4. **Market inefficiency**: about 70% of China A-share investors are retail, which creates volatility and opportunities.
  5. **Foreign capital flows**: EM's GDP weight (39%) is not reflected in index weights; capital-market opening (Stock Connect) should lift index weight and foreign allocation.
- **Four disadvantages**
  1. **Volatility**: 15-year annualised net return MSCI EM 9.75% vs ACWI 6.52%, Sharpe 0.47 vs 0.38, but maximum drawdown -65% vs -58% (both 2008); currency volatility adds to it.
  2. **Higher-than-expected correlation** with developed markets: due to globalisation, DM companies' EM revenues, and correlation spikes in stress.
  3. **Political, legal, counterparty, operational risk**: erratic policy, inflation/deflation, unorthodox monetary moves.
  4. **Liquidity**: wider spreads, higher transaction costs, hence higher fund fees.
- **Portfolio positioning**: Italian household financial wealth about EUR 4.4tn (2018; EUR 4.777tn end-2020), 31% in deposits, 7% debt securities, 24% equity/participations, 12% mutual funds, 23% insurance/pension; real assets (mostly property) about EUR 6.3tn. Foreign funds were only about 6% of wealth and about 40% of those in debt; geography 47% Europe, 25% US. Two-thirds of Italian assets sit in low-risk areas and about 1% in riskier ones. Globally, Morgan Stanley (EPFR data) estimates EM equities at about 7% of a global 60/40 portfolio. Conclusion: EM is under-held versus its 13% index weight and 39% GDP weight.
- **Capital Group view (sponsor, 2021)**: five reasons for acceleration in 2021: faster recovery than many developed economies (IMF Oct 2020 real GDP growth projections: India 8.8%, China 8.2%, Indonesia 6.1%, EM average 6.0%, Eurozone 5.2%, US 3.1%, Brazil 2.8%); broadening rally (financials, travel, real estate, commodities); new stimulus; better virus control and geopolitics; compression of risk premium. Valuation chart: projected 2021 EPS growth Europe 37.1%, EM 33.6%, Japan 28.4%, US 23.3% vs forward P/E (the chart assigns 17.4 to EM and 20.4/18.4/15.1 to others; labels garbled). Claims EM currencies undervalued and EM no longer a high-beta asset even excluding China. Advocacy, not evidence.

## Key Concepts
- **MSCI EM / FTSE EM**: main providers (MSCI EM from 1988; FTSE incorporated Barings EM index in 2000).
- **A-shares**: yuan-denominated mainland-listed Chinese shares.
- **Inclusion factor**: percentage of A-share free float counted in the index.
- **Stock Connect**: link between mainland and Hong Kong exchanges.
- **Cap-weighted concentration**: top-10 weight of 27%.
- **Retail-dominated market**: speculative, policy-following flows.
- **Drawdown**: maximum peak-to-trough loss.
- **Currency volatility** as part of equity return for USD investors.
- **Home bias**: low allocation to EM relative to market weight.
- **Standalone market (MSCI)**: country removed from EM index but not frontier (Argentina 2021).
- **Revenue vs listing**: DM companies' EM sales already create EM exposure.
- **Risk premium compression**: re-rating thesis.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- Sharpe: `(R - Rf)/σ`. Over 15 years: EM 0.47 vs ACWI 0.38 (reported). Return difference 9.75% - 6.52% = **3.23 pts** a year.
- Concentration measure: top-5 weights 82.7% (1988), 56.1% (1997), 63.6% (2007), 73.4% (2017); top-10 stocks 16.7% / 21.3% / 26.6%.
- Weight-gap metric: EM GDP share 39.1% − index weight 13.3% = 25.8 pts under-representation.
- Compound growth: ACWI EM weight 0.9% → 13% in 33 years is `(13/0.9)^(1/33) - 1` = **8.5%** a year relative growth.

## Mental Models
- Use **index = economy snapshot**: re-check each decade what the index actually holds; a 1998 view of EM (telecom, utilities, LatAm) is obsolete.
- Use **country-sector decomposition**: EM today is China-Taiwan-Korea tech/consumer; commodity exporters are a minority.
- Use **retail-flow lens** for China A-shares: volatility and inefficiency are two sides of one coin.
- Use **drawdown budgeting**: EM's -65% in 2008 vs -58% for ACWI means higher tail pain for higher return.
- Use **gap analysis for allocation**: compare client holdings with market weight (13%), GDP weight (39%) and revenue weight (42%).

## Worked Example
Return per unit of risk, EM vs ACWI over 15 years (reported data).
- Excess return gap (EM minus ACWI) = 9.75 − 6.52 = 3.23 pts.
- Sharpe 0.47 vs 0.38. If ACWI had volatility `σ` and Rf `r`, then `(6.52 − r)/σ = 0.38`; EM volatility is only "slightly higher", so the Sharpe gap is mainly the return gap.
- Try Rf = 1.0% (assumption): ACWI `σ = (6.52 − 1.0)/0.38 = 14.5%`; EM `σ = (9.75 − 1.0)/0.47 = 18.6%` (so "slightly higher" means about 4 points).
- Interpretation: higher Sharpe does not remove drawdown risk (-65%); size EM against tail loss, not only Sharpe. Values depend on assumed Rf; the source gives only the Sharpe ratios.

## Anti-patterns
- Describing EM as commodity/LatAm exposure today.
- Counting on EM to diversify equity risk without checking stress correlation.
- Ignoring China concentration (about 38%) when using an EM index fund as a "diversified" holding.
- Assuming retail-driven inefficiency is easy alpha (it raises volatility).
- Treating one favourable 15-year window as a forecast; the previous decade underperformed US markets, as the sponsor itself admits.
- Using index top-5 totals from the source without recomputing (two sums disagree).

## Key Takeaways
1. MSCI EM grew from 10 countries (1988) to 27 and from 0.9% to about 13% of ACWI.
2. Leadership rotated from Malaysia/Brazil to China, with Asia now dominant and technology the largest sector.
3. 15-year EM return 9.75% vs ACWI 6.52%; Sharpe 0.47 vs 0.38; drawdown -65% vs -58%.
4. Advantages: growth, new trends, diversification, inefficiency, capital inflows. Disadvantages: volatility, correlation, political/operational risk, liquidity.
5. A-share inclusion will raise China's index weight.
6. Italian households (and global 60/40 portfolios at 7%) are under-allocated to EM.
7. Single-country outcomes diverge strongly (China vs Brazil).

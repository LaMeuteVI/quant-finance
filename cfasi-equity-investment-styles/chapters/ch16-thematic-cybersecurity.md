# Chapter 16: Cybersecurity Investing
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, *Thematic Investments* guide (2021), Ch. 4 "Perché guardare agli investimenti sulla Cyber security" | Giancarlo Sandrin, CFA

## Core Idea
Covid-19 moved work and consumption online, accelerating cloud adoption and enlarging the attack surface at the same time as a recession raised cybercrime. Cybersecurity spending is therefore driven by **short-term shocks** (remote work, digital consumption, recession-related crime) and **medium/long-term structural drivers** (cloud-delivered security, 5G and IoT connections, data-protection regulation). The market is large (about $124bn in 2020) but grew only 2.4% in 2020, with sharp differences by segment.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Driver map for security demand**
  | Horizon | Driver | Mechanism |
  |---|---|---|
  | Short | Prolonged remote working | Corporate networks must be protected outside the perimeter |
  | Short | Recession | Cybercrime statistically rises when economic output declines |
  | Short | Peak digital consumption (streaming, gaming) | More activity online needs more security |
  | Medium-long | Cloud-based providers | Recessions push firms to outsource and seek efficiency; security vendors that are themselves cloud-based benefit |
  | Medium-long | 5G rollout and IoT | 41.6bn connected devices by 2025 generating 79.4 ZB of data (IDC); every device is a vulnerable node |
  | Medium-long | Data-protection regulation | Applies to small and large operators; forces spending |
- **Discontinuity framing**: attacks rose in severity and frequency (CLUSIT: serious public attacks about 450 in 2014 and 850 in H1 2020 alone, an average of about 141 a month, +39% vs the prior five-year monthly average). The author calls it a regime change, not a trend.
- **Spending by segment (Gartner, June 2020, $M)**

## Key Concepts
- **Cybersecurity theme**: companies earning at least half of revenue from digital security (index rule in ch13, with a liquidity filter).
- **Cloud security**: protection delivered for or by cloud services; fastest segment (+33%).
- **IAM (Identity Access Management)**: controls who can access what; mid-size, steady.
- **Infrastructure protection / network security equipment**: hardware-centred; network equipment contracted in 2020.
- **Security services**: largest segment (about half the market), consulting, managed services.
- **Remote working effect**: shift of corporate perimeter to home networks.
- **Cybercrime-recession link**: crime rises with output decline (Portsmouth Centre for Counter Fraud Studies, 2020).
- **IoT attack surface**: more connected devices means more entry points.
- **Regulatory driver**: data-protection law compliance spend.
- **Cybersecurity Ventures forecasts**: market 3.5bn (2004) to over 120bn (2017), about 35x in 13 years; more than $1tn cumulative spending 2017-2021 (forecast).

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- Growth `g = V_2020/V_2019 - 1`: total `123,818/120,934 - 1 = 2.4%`.
- Market CAGR 2004-2017: `(120/3.5)^(1/13) - 1` = about **31%** a year (forecasts, not audited).
- Mix 2020: security services 64,270 / 123,818 = **51.9%** of the market; cloud security 585 / 123,818 = **0.47%** (still tiny).
- Index stats (ch13, 5Y): Digital Security return 15.04%, vol 14.97%, Sharpe 1.04, beta 0.98, Sortino 1.76, VaR95 -8.75%, max drawdown -17.18% (Jan-Mar 2020), rebound +44.5%; the lowest return among the twelve technology themes.

## Mental Models
- Use **demand = threat x connectivity x regulation** to frame the theme.
- Use **segment mix over headline growth**: a 2.4% market hides +33% cloud security and -12.6% network hardware.
- Use **services vs product split**: services are half the market and more cyclical to IT budgets; product vendors depend on innovation cycles.
- Use **"decisamente non siamo più nel Kansas"** (Dorothy quote the author uses): treat threat evolution as a discontinuity, so past breach statistics understate the future.

## Worked Example
Segment contribution to 2020 growth (million $).
- Total change = 123,818 - 120,934 = **+2,884**.
- Cloud security +146 (5.1% of the change), IAM +572, infrastructure +963, services +2,291, network equipment -1,693, consumer -19, others (app, data, risk, other software) +192+190+176+67 = +625.
- Sum check: 146 + 572 + 963 + 2,291 - 1,693 - 19 + 625 = **2,885** (rounding 1).
- Interpretation: almost all net growth came from services and infrastructure; cloud security is the fastest but contributes little in absolute dollars, so a pure-cloud play is a growth-from-small-base thesis, not a market-beta play.

## Anti-patterns
- Extrapolating the 2004-2017 growth rate or the $1tn cumulative forecast.
- Assuming breach headlines imply earnings: vendor consolidation, price competition and platform bundling by large cloud firms matter.
- Ignoring that incident counts are partly reporting-driven (companies often hide attacks; CLUSIT counts only serious public ones).
- Treating "digital security" as defensive: index beta about 1 and the theme sold off in March 2020.
- Overlooking hardware exposure when buying a broad security fund.

## Practitioner Notes
- Pair with cloud and AI themes: they share data-centre and software drivers, so correlation inside a thematic basket is high.
- Watch: share of revenue from subscription/cloud, net retention, platform consolidation, regulatory deadlines.
- Statistics here come from vendors and industry associations (Gartner, CLUSIT, IDC, Cybersecurity Ventures); forecasts are dated 2017-2020.

## Key Takeaways
1. Pandemic accelerated cloud and remote work, widening attack surface.
2. Demand drivers split into short-term (remote work, crime, digital consumption) and structural (cloud vendors, 5G/IoT, regulation).
3. 2020 spending: $123.8bn, +2.4%; cloud security +33%, network hardware -12.6%.
4. Serious public attacks went from about 450 in 2014 to an 850-in-six-months pace in 2020 (roughly 3.8x on an annualised basis).
5. Services are half the market.
6. The index has moderate return and Sharpe versus other tech themes, with market-like drawdown.

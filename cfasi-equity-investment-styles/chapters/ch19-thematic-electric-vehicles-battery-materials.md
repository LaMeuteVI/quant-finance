# Chapter 19: Electric Vehicle Growth and the Battery Raw-Materials Chain
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, *Thematic Investments* guide (2021), Ch. 7 "La crescita dei veicoli elettrici e l'impatto sulle materie prime delle batterie" | Giacomo Fumagalli, CFA (data: BloombergNEF, Robeco, IEA, ACEA, UBS, Roskill)

## Core Idea
EV growth is **policy-led** (CO2 fleet limits with fines in the EU, subsidies and quotas in China, post-pandemic incentives), then supported by falling battery prices. Among battery inputs, **lithium** is the only element present in every lithium-ion chemistry and is the least substitutable, with demand forecast to surge while supply is still small, immature and uncertain. The chapter links the auto-electrification thesis to the investable raw-materials chain (lithium, nickel, cobalt) and explains the cathode-chemistry trade-off between cost and range.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Regulatory pull**: EU fleet CO2 target of 95 g/km (phase-in 2020, fully applicable 2021); fine EUR 95 per g/km excess per vehicle sold (Regs 443/2009 and 2019/631); dieselgate (2015) accelerated city diesel bans and national sale bans (Norway 2025); 26 of 27 EU states subsidise EVs (ACEA). China: subsidies and manufacturer quotas. Figure 1 of the source compares fleet CO2 standards in g/km for the US (CAFE), Japan, Europe and China across 2014, 2025E and 2030E; values are garbled in the extraction, so only the EU 95 g/km figure is used here.
- **Automaker electrification plans** (company data): BMW 15-25% EVs by 2025; Daimler electrify full lineup by 2022; Ford over 50% of European sales electrified by 2022; GM 1m EV sales in 2026; Hyundai-Kia 1m in 2025; PSA electrify all lines by 2025; Renault 20 EV models by 2022; Tesla 500k BEV in 2020; Toyota 500k BEV in 2025; Volvo 50% BEV by 2025; VW Group 1m BEV in 2023 and 1.5m in 2025.
- **Cost driver**: battery pack price $1,191/kWh (2010) to $137/kWh (2020) per BloombergNEF, a fall of about 89% (compound about -19% a year). Cells are 70-75% of battery cost and the pack 25-30%; raw materials are 15-30% of cell cost, depending on cathode mix and prices.
- **Cathode chemistry trade-off**: Hi-Nickel NMC/NCA = highest energy density (kWh/kg) and range (300 miles, about 480 km, seen as the minimum to replace combustion cars) but higher cost; LFP (lithium iron phosphate) = lower density and range, lower raw-material cost, mature Chinese capacity, strong safety (about 40% of China's EV battery market at end-2020 per JPMorgan); cobalt-free LFP used by Tesla in some markets. Trend: toward Hi-Nickel while LFP persists in China. Solid-state batteries (lithium metal anode, more than 2x energy density) are expected to reach mass adoption next decade, with cost the main hurdle.
- **Cobalt problem**: price $30-110/kg over five years; over 70% of supply from the Democratic Republic of Congo, partly artisanal mining, so human-rights, political and supply risk. Cobalt reduction (via high-nickel or LFP) is a key R&D target.
- **Lithium market structure**: demand 70,000 t LCE (lithium carbonate equivalent) in 2000; about 300,000 t in 2020; forecast above 2,000,000 t in 2030 (about 21% a year). Batteries for EVs about 30% of demand today, to exceed 80% within ten years. Supply: about 355,000 t LCE at end-2020 (UBS) far from the needed 5-10 year capacity; resource is abundant (17-20 ppm in the crust; 41m t of resources per USGS) but conversion to battery-grade carbonate or hydroxide is the bottleneck. Two compounds (carbonate, hydroxide) from two deposit types (brines, hard rock). Not a standardised commodity: no liquid futures market. Roskill: lithium intensity per Wh could double. Recycling will contribute long term; marginal supply from higher-cost sites supports long-run price floors; past price spikes in China.
- **Stationary storage**: wind and solar share of power generation 9% (2019) to 56% (2050) (BloombergNEF) raises storage demand; Li-ion competitive but not the only technology.
- **EV penetration facts**: BEV and PHEV sales kept growing in 2020 despite the recession and a sharp fall in the overall car market; EVs are still a minority of global sales but their share is rising quickly (Figures 2-3, BloombergNEF).

## Key Concepts
- **BEV / PHEV**: pure battery electric vehicle / plug-in hybrid.
- **Range anxiety**: fear of insufficient range per charge.
- **NMC / NCA / LFP / LMO**: nickel-manganese-cobalt, nickel-cobalt-aluminium, lithium iron phosphate, lithium manganese oxide cathodes.
- **LCE**: lithium carbonate equivalent, unit of lithium demand.
- **Brine vs hard-rock lithium**: two deposit types.
- **Battery-grade**: product meeting customer chemistry specs; lithium is not yet a standard commodity.
- **Pack vs cell cost**: cell about 70-75%, pack 25-30%.
- **Solid-state battery**: solid electrodes allowing lithium metal anode.
- **Fleet CO2 target**: 95 g/km EU.
- **Upstream execution risk**: mining/expansion projects delayed or cost-overrun.
- **Downstream conversion**: refining to battery-grade compounds.
- **Cobalt-free**: LFP or high-nickel designs.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- Battery price decline: `1 - 137/1191 = 88.5%`; CAGR `(137/1191)^(1/10) - 1 = -19.4%`.
- Lithium demand CAGR 2020-2030: `(2,000,000/300,000)^(1/10) - 1 = 20.9%`; 2000-2020: `(300,000/70,000)^(1/20) - 1 = 7.5%`.
- Raw materials share of pack cost: cells 70-75% x raw materials 15-30% of cells = about **10.5-22.5%** of pack cost.
- EU penalty: `fine = EUR 95 x (fleet average g/km - 95) x vehicles sold`.
- EV penetration scenario (ch18 data): 10% (2025), 28% (2030), 58% (2040) of passenger car sales.
- Theme-index stats (ch13, 5Y): Battery return 22.01%, vol 19.78%, Sharpe 1.14, beta 1.24, downside deviation 10.87%, VaR95 -11.17%, max drawdown -21.21%, rebound +111%.

## Mental Models
- Use **policy first, cost second, demand third** to time the EV theme.
- Use **chemistry scenario tree**: whichever cathode wins, lithium is in all; nickel and cobalt carry chemistry risk; this isolates a low-regret exposure (lithium) from chemistry bets.
- Use **supply-curve thinking**: price must rise to the marginal cost of the least-attractive new project; expect cyclical spikes.
- Use **execution-risk discount** for junior miners and new refiners.
- Use **cell-cost sensitivity**: raw materials are 10-22% of pack cost, so a doubling of a raw-material price moves pack cost by only 10-22%.

## Worked Example
Sensitivity of pack cost to a lithium-price shock (illustrative). Pack $137/kWh. Take raw materials at 22.5% of pack (upper range) = $30.8/kWh, with lithium as, say, one-fifth of that (illustrative) = $6.2/kWh.
- Lithium price +100%: pack cost + $6.2 = **+4.5%** to $143.2/kWh.
- Cobalt price doubling with cobalt at 1/5 of raw material cost has the same effect.
- Interpretation: even a large raw-material shock moves EV cost modestly, so the miners' earnings volatility is higher than automakers' margin impact; the investment case for lithium rests on volume growth, not on automaker pricing power. (The lithium and cobalt shares used are illustrative assumptions, not from the source.)

## Anti-patterns
- Buying lithium miners as a pure EV beta without checking execution, conversion capacity and price-realisation (no futures hedge).
- Assuming one dominant chemistry for raw-material forecasts.
- Treating subsidy-driven demand as permanent.
- Ignoring cobalt ESG and supply concentration (Congo).
- Extrapolating 2010-2020 cost declines linearly (the decline rate has slowed: yearly changes in Figure 4 fall from -35% to -13%).

## Practitioner Notes
- Figures in the Robeco/BloombergNEF charts are partly garbled in extraction; verify chart-based percentages (cathode mix, growth rates) before external use.
- The thesis has policy and technology risk both ways: faster solid-state adoption or cobalt-free chemistries change the materials mix; lithium remains robust.
- Index overlap: Battery theme holds both cell makers and miners.

## Key Takeaways
1. Regulation, then subsidies, drove the EV inflection; costs followed.
2. Battery pack price fell about 89% in a decade to $137/kWh.
3. Hi-Nickel for range, LFP for cost and safety; solid-state is next decade.
4. Cobalt is costly and geopolitically concentrated; reduction is a priority.
5. Lithium is the common denominator; demand could be about 7x by 2030, supply response uncertain.
6. Lithium lacks standardisation and futures, so access is via equities.

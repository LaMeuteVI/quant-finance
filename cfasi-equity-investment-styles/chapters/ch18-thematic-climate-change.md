# Chapter 18: Climate Change: From Risk to Opportunity
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, *Thematic Investments* guide (2021), Ch. 6 "Il climate change: da rischio ad opportunità" | Alessandro Stella, CFA, CESGA (DWS, MSCI, ISS, Sustainalytics, Trucost, Trackinsight data as cited)

## Core Idea
Climate change is both a **financial risk** to be priced into every portfolio (physical, transition, liability) and an **investment opportunity** (low-carbon winners). Policy has shifted from symbolic to binding (EU Green Deal, US re-entry to the Paris Agreement, China 2060 neutrality), which makes transition risk and opportunity measurable. Investors can respond with three escalating tools: a **climate filter** on the whole universe (transition-risk scores, "leader vs laggard"), a **thematic or SDG 13-aligned strategy**, and **engagement/disclosure** pressure.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three forms of climate risk**
  | Risk | Meaning | Example in source |
  |---|---|---|
  | Physical | Damage from extreme events (acute: floods, storms, drought) and chronic change (degradation, pollution) to land, buildings, infrastructure | Sectors most exposed: agriculture/forestry/fishing, energy and extraction, infrastructure/transport, tourism |
  | Transition | Losses from adjusting to a low-carbon economy: regulation, technology, consumer preference; reprices assets up or down | Peabody Energy (largest private coal company) filed Chapter 11 on 13 April 2016 after four years of record losses and over $10bn debt; sectors most exposed: transport, agriculture, construction/manufacturing, chemicals, energy |
  | Legal liability | Lawsuits or compensation claims for negligence/harm | New York City 2018 suit against five fossil-fuel majors; pension fund divestment |
  Value at risk estimates: assets potentially affected by climate change about $2.5tn (LSE, 2016) to $4.2tn (Economist); for scale Japan GDP $5.1tn, Germany $3.9tn (2019).
- **Climate impact by warming scenario by 2100 (CRO Forum 2019)**: the source table compares four scenarios (about 1.5C, 2C, 3C, 5C; column headers are garbled in the extraction). Reading from the lowest to the highest scenario: sea-level rise 0.3-0.6 m up to 0.5-1.7 m; coastal assets to defend $10.2tn up to $27.5tn; probability of an ice-free Arctic summer from 1 in 30 to certain; frequency of extreme rainfall events +17% up to +150%; wildfire area x1.4 up to x2.6; people hit by heat waves x22 up to x300; area exposed to malaria +12% up to +46%. Use the shape (non-linear damage as warming rises), not the exact column mapping.
- **Policy catalysts (as of 2020-21)**: European Green Deal: at least 55% emissions cut by 2030 vs 1990, energy efficiency +32.5%, renewables share at least 32% (2030 targets as cited); EU recovery plan with 30% of the 2021-2027 funds (about EUR 550bn) for climate; US rejoined Paris Agreement, net zero by 2050 now covering 66% of global GDP; China (about 27% of global emissions, 1.4bn people) pledged neutrality by 2060, peak emissions possibly 2025. EV share forecast of passenger sales: 10% in 2025, 28% in 2030, 58% in 2040 (BloombergNEF).
- **Climate transition risk filter (from ESG filter to climate filter)**
  - ESG pillars: exclusions (tobacco, gambling, coal, nuclear, defence, pornography), controversial weapons, norms-based screening (human and labour rights), Best-in-Class.
  - **Carbon footprint (CFP)** is a narrow signal: it ignores avoided emissions and other environmental impacts, so it mostly identifies the biggest emitters.
  - **Transition-risk scoring** is better: MSCI estimates 20% of MSCI ACWI is significantly exposed to low-carbon transition.
  | Provider | Method |
  |---|---|
  | MSCI | Low Carbon Transition Score, 0-10, absolute multi-dimensional, sector-agnostic, includes secondary effects such as avoided emissions |
  | ISS-Oekom | Carbon Risk Rating, sector-relative, includes positive impact (cleantech), forward looking |
  | Sustainalytics | Carbon Pillar Risk Rating: exposure and management of transition risk across operations (Scope 1, 2 and partly 3) and products |
  | S&P Trucost | Carbon Earnings at Risk: simulated carbon-price scenarios in 130 regions, stress-tests ability to absorb cost, shows profit at risk (introduced 2019) |
  - Asset managers merge scores into proprietary "leader (score above 50) vs laggard (below 50)" ratings (DWS). Median sector scores: Energy 14, Utilities 19, Materials 25 (worst); Financials, Health Care, Communication and IT above 50 (leaders). Share of companies exposed to transition risk (score below 50): Energy 98%, Materials 95%, Real Estate 93%, Utilities 80%; exposed to opportunity (score above 75): Technology 12%, Utilities 10%, Industrials 8%.
  - Portfolio use: overweight transition promoters, underweight high-risk issuers; bottom-up at sub-sector and company level.
  - Obstacles: incomplete, non-comparable disclosure. Responses: TCFD (FSB, 2015), EU Action Plan on Sustainable Growth, SFDR (Regulation (EU) 2019/2088), and shareholder engagement.
- **Opportunity routes**: (1) climate tilt of the universe; (2) explicit thematic climate funds (Morningstar "Sector Equity Ecology": clean energy, resource scarcity, climate-adaptation innovators, climate technology, energy efficiency); (3) **SDG screening**: 17 UN goals, 169 targets (2015 Agenda 2030, 193 countries); revenue-based screen of alignment, SDG 13 (Climate action), also SDG 7 (clean energy) and SDG 14 (life below water). MSCI ACWI IMI screen (DWS, May 2020): 842 companies negative SDG contribution; 486 with 0-5% aligned revenue; 590 with 5-25%; 210 with 25-50%; 89 with 50-75%; 76 with 75-95%; 90 with 95-100%.
- **Passive products (Trackinsight, end 2020)**: 208 SDG-aligned ETFs ($71.8bn); 155 ETFs (74.5%) contribute to SDG 13, $49.1bn (68.4%); SDG 7: 18 ETFs, $20.7bn (28.8%); SDG 6: 2 ETFs; SDG 12: 2; SDG 14: 1. ESG low-carbon index ETFs use exclusion on actual or potential emissions (different from SRI/ESG-leader methods).
- **Opportunity size**: about $2tn a year green-energy investment to 2030 (then $3.3tn in the following decade); 500+ companies with over $11tn market cap have set ambitious climate goals; most sustainable power generators expected EPS CAGR 27% (2019-2022); Net Zero Asset Managers Initiative launched 11 Dec 2020 (30 founders, over $9tn AUM).

## Key Concepts
- **Climate transition risk**: financial effect of moving to low-carbon.
- **Physical risk (acute/chronic)**: weather events vs gradual change.
- **Liability risk**: litigation.
- **Carbon footprint (CFP)**: company CO2 emissions, for peer comparison.
- **Scope 1, 2, 3**: direct, purchased energy, value-chain emissions.
- **Carbon Earnings at Risk**: profit exposed to carbon pricing.
- **Climate leader / laggard**: above/below 50 on the transition score.
- **Best-in-Class**: choose top-rated firms in each sector.
- **SDG revenue alignment**: share of revenue contributing to a UN goal.
- **Net zero 2050**: target of zero net greenhouse-gas emissions.
- **TCFD / SFDR**: disclosure frameworks.
- **Engagement**: using shareholder rights to improve disclosure and mitigation.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- Share of ETFs on SDG 13: `155 / 208 = 74.5%`; AUM share `49.1 / 71.8 = 68.4%`.
- SDG alignment distribution total in MSCI ACWI IMI sample: 842 + 486 + 590 + 210 + 89 + 76 + 90 = **2,383** companies.
- Transition-risk score rule: `score < 50` laggard, `score > 75` strong opportunity.
- Theme index stats (ch13, 5Y): Climate Change return 12.40%, vol 13.56%, Sharpe 0.95, beta 1.00, max drawdown -18.61%; Clean Energy 25.97% / 23.16% / 1.14, drawdown -22.69%, rebound +180%.

## Mental Models
- Use **"risk lens first, theme second"**: apply a climate filter to the whole book before buying a climate theme.
- Use **forward-looking scores (Trucost, ISS-Oekom)** over backward-looking footprints when the question is repricing.
- Use **SDG revenue screening for impact**, ESG/exclusion for risk: they answer different questions.
- Use **sector medians**: expect Energy, Utilities and Materials to be laggards on average; look inside sectors for leaders.
- Use **policy as the catalyst clock**: EU, US and China targets define demand for clean energy and EVs.

## Worked Example
How concentrated is SDG alignment in MSCI ACWI IMI (sample of 2,383 firms)?
- Net negative or negligible (below 5%): 842 + 486 = 1,328 = **55.7%**.
- Meaningfully aligned (above 50% of revenue): 89 + 76 + 90 = 255 = **10.7%**.
- Interpretation: a strict SDG-13 revenue screen retains roughly a tenth of the universe, so such portfolios are concentrated and sector-tilted; this partly explains the high volatility of climate themes.

## Anti-patterns
- Relying on carbon footprint alone (ignores avoided emissions, other harms).
- Treating "ESG low carbon" ETFs as thematic climate solutions (different construction: exclusion vs revenue theme).
- Assuming climate themes are low risk: Clean Energy had the deepest drawdown among sustainable themes in ch13.
- Ignoring disclosure gaps: scores from different vendors disagree.
- Using one scenario (2C) without stress to 3C or 5C.

## Practitioner Notes

## Key Takeaways
1. Climate risk comes in three types: physical, transition, liability.
2. Policy now sets measurable targets, creating pricing for transition risk.
3. Move from carbon footprint to forward-looking transition scores.
4. Three implementation levels: filter, thematic/SDG strategy, engagement.
5. SDG 13 dominates ETF supply (155 of 208 SDG ETFs).
6. Climate themes combine growth with high volatility and boom-bust history.

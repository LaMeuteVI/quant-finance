# Chapter 14: Robotics and Automation, and the 5G Theme
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, *Thematic Investments* guide (2021), Ch. 2 "Una nuova era dorata per l'innovazione nella robotica e automazione" | Angus Muirhead, CFA; plus CPR AM insert "5G: aspettatevi altra disruption" (Andrea Mossetto, sponsor content, attributed)

## Core Idea
Robotics and automation moved from a niche, high-volume industrial market to a broad one because the **enabling stack** became cheap and widely available: abundant software developers, exponentially more computing power, collapsing component costs, billions of connected sensors, and open-source robot software. Low adoption rates plus costly human labour make automation a long-horizon theme for patient investors. 5G is the connectivity layer that extends the same thesis (smart factories, IoT, remote skills).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Enabler stack for the robotics thesis** (cause → effect chain)
  1. **Software talent**: developers 11M+ (2005) to 23.9M (2019, Evans Data), +5M expected over 5 years; India projected to pass the US by 2024; Asia-Pacific the fastest growth. Pipeline: coding education, toys (Lego Mindstorms 1998, Boost 2017), Raspberry Pi (30M+ units since 2012 at about $30).
  2. **Compute (Moore's Law)**: transistor count doubling about every two years. 1971 chip: 2,300 transistors; 1993: 3.3M (about 1,400x); 2019: 38.5bn (about 11,000x more than 1993).
  3. **Cost deflation** (Deloitte Shift Index, Credit Suisse): 1992 vs 2015 cost of 1M transistors $222 to $0.03; 1GB storage $569 to $0.02; 1,000 Mb/s network $1,197 to $11.
  4. **Virtuous circle**: cheaper parts expand use cases and customers, scale lowers cost further.
  5. **Platforms**: connected devices 16bn (2016) to 29bn (2022E, Ericsson); more than 90% of world data created in the previous two years (IBM, 2017); data centres and fast mobile networks; open-source robot tooling (ROS operating system, OpenCV/SLAM navigation libraries); chips such as Qualcomm RB3; crowdfunding capital.
  6. **Application space**: OT and IoT in the physical world is early (home automation, warehouse flows, crop pest detection) versus mature IT hardware.
- **Why automation is not optional (author's argument)**: innovation begets innovation; human labour is now too expensive or unavailable for many tasks; taxing or slowing automation protects jobs short-term but erodes competitiveness. Treat as the author's view, with social disruption an acknowledged risk.
- **5G vs 4G (CPR AM)**: up to 20x speed (film download about 6 minutes to under 20 seconds), many more simultaneously connected devices, latency about 10x lower (near real time). Use cases: smart factories (industry boost estimated at $400-650bn by 2030), IoT and smart cities/autonomous vehicles, remote expertise (robot-assisted remote surgery, remote diagnostics). IHS Markit estimate: 5G value chain output $3.6tn by 2035. Deployment as of Sept 2020: 397 operators investing in 5G networks, 118 announced live deployments, 96 announced service launches; about 1.8bn 5G connections by 2025, concentrated in developed Asia and North America; by 2035 seven countries expected to take 79% of 5G investment. Headwinds named: cyber-security, US-China tension and Huawei exclusion, environmental cost of replacing infrastructure, conspiracy rumours.

## Key Concepts
- **Moore's Law**: roughly biannual doubling of transistors per chip; compute cost deflation.
- **OT vs IT**: operational technology (machines, plants) versus information technology; OT/IoT penetration is low.
- **IoT**: sensors and devices connected to networks as data inputs and action outputs.
- **SLAM / ROS / OpenCV**: building blocks for autonomous navigation and robot development.
- **Technology deflation**: collapsing unit cost that widens demand (virtuous circle).
- **Penetration rate**: share of addressable tasks automated; the source argues it is low in most sectors.
- **Latency**: delay between command and response; the key 5G spec for industrial control.
- **Smart factory**: plant using robotics, cloud, big data, digital security, AR, 3D printing.
- **Robotics value chain**: chips/sensors/components, software, integrators, end users.
- **Beneficiary vs adopter**: suppliers of robots versus companies that raise productivity by deploying them.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- Doubling time from counts: `T_d = Δt / log2(N2/N1)`. 1971-1993 (22 years): 22 / log2(3.3M/2,300) = **2.1 years**; 1993-2019 (26 years): 26 / log2(38.5bn/3.3M) = **1.9 years**.
- Cost multiples 1992 to 2015: compute 7,400x cheaper, storage 28,450x, network about 109x.
- Connected-device CAGR 2016-2022: `(29/16)^(1/6) - 1` = **10.4%**.
- Theme-index stats from ch13 (5Y): Robotics return 21.82%, vol 19.47%, Sharpe 1.15, beta 1.26 (worst drawdown Q4 2018, -22.27%); Smart Factory 21.57% / 17.13% / 1.29; 5G since Jan 2017: 14.73% / 16.88% / 0.90 with negative returns until Dec 2018 and strong 2019-2020; drawdown -11.7% (smallest of all themes).

## Mental Models
- Use **"software eats hardware"** (Andreessen, 2011): value in robots increasingly sits in software, sensors and data, so look beyond robot makers.
- Use **cost-curve thinking**: buy the theme when the enabling-stack cost falls below the labour cost for the target task.
- Use **picks-and-shovels** for early adoption: chips, sensors, components, software platforms.
- Use **separate hype from deployment data** for 5G: operator capex and live networks, not headlines.
- Use **regional lens** for 5G: investment concentrated in a few countries.

## Worked Example
Estimate doubling time from the guide's data. Transistors rose from 2,300 (1971) to 3.3M (1993).
- Ratio = 3,300,000 / 2,300 = 1,434.8; `log2` = 10.49 doublings.
- Time per doubling = 22 / 10.49 = **2.10 years**, consistent with the "every two years" statement.
- Interpretation: Moore's Law held to within about 5%; the thesis relies on the cost curve continuing, which is a risk if physical limits slow it (not argued by the source).

## Anti-patterns
- Treating 5-year index returns for Robotics/Smart Factory as expected returns.
- Ignoring the beta of robotics (1.26) and the Q4 2018 drawdown: it is a cyclical, capex-driven theme.
- Assuming adoption speed from technology availability: regulation, wages and social acceptance matter (taxes on automation).
- Confusing a theme's story (5G speed) with investable profits (operator capex, equipment vendors, spectrum costs, geopolitical exclusion).
- Using sponsor revenue forecasts ($3.6tn by 2035) as base case.

## Practitioner Notes
- Source is a CFA charterholder's thematic note; the CPR AM piece is sponsor content, advocacy for 5G funds.
- Robotics is a "long-run, patient capital" theme per the author; size as a satellite.
- Diligence checklist: revenue share from robotics/automation, exposure by chain layer (components, software, integrators), customer concentration (autos, electronics), cyclicality of capex.

## Key Takeaways
1. Robotics' tipping point is driven by the cost and availability of the enablers, not one invention.
2. Moore's Law and deflation across compute, storage and network created a virtuous circle.
3. Software developer growth and open-source tools lower barriers to entering robotics.
4. OT/IoT penetration is low: large addressable market.
5. 5G adds speed, device density and low latency, enabling smart factories and remote services.
6. Both themes have strong index records but short, boom-period samples.

# Chapter 21: The Investable Universe of Thematic Products and How to Select Them
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, *Thematic Investments* guide (2021), Ch. 9 "L'universo investibile dei prodotti tematici" | Giovanni Semprini, CFA (data: Bloomberg, author's elaboration, early 2021; UCITS ETFs and funds only)

## Core Idea
Thematic products grew fast (EUR 31.6bn at end-2017 to EUR 123.9bn at end-2020, about +290%), but within each theme **products differ widely** in return, volatility, correlation, index design and holdings. Selecting the theme is only half the work: **product selection and due diligence** (index methodology, replication, sector/country/cap mix, concentration) matter as much. Thematic investing also differs from sector investing, and can serve as a **hedge against long-term "external" risks** (longevity, automation, energy transition) rather than a pure growth bet.

In Healthcare Innovation, lowest average excess-return correlation is Fondo3 at 0.62; best pair ETF2 + Fondo3 at 0.44.

- **Thematic vs sectoral**: sectors (GICS: 11 sectors, 24 industry groups, 69 industries, 158 sub-industries; ICB: 11 industries, 20 supersectors, 45 sectors, 173 subsectors) are horizontal classifications weighted by market cap, favouring "past winners"; themes cut across sectors (5G spans semiconductors, towers, social media; Robotics spans IT, Industrials, Consumer Discretionary, Health Care) and bet on "future winners" gaining share. ETFs publish daily holdings, active funds with a lag of at least 1-2 months.
- **Thematic as hedge against external risks (author's view)**: the typical view is growth factor exposure, but themes can offset long-term risks to income/wealth: a fossil-fuel worker holds renewables; a worker exposed to automation holds AI/robotics; a retiree exposed to longevity risk holds ageing/silver-economy; Norway's sovereign fund doubled sustainable-energy investment. It is a counterweight, not perfect protection.

## Key Concepts
- **Thematic fund / ETF**: product tracking or investing in a structural-trend basket.
- **Index provider effect**: each provider builds a different basket for the same theme.
- **Synthetic vs physical replication; full vs sampling**: how an ETF tracks.
- **Excess-return correlation**: correlation of returns net of MSCI World, better for diversification.
- **Dispersion within theme**: spread of returns and volatilities among products.
- **GICS / ICB**: sector taxonomies.
- **Past vs future winners**: market-cap weighting vs theme conviction.
- **Longevity risk / silver economy**: ageing-related income risk and its investment theme.
- **UCITS compliance**: regulatory scope of the mapping.
- **Efficient-frontier products**: ones that maximise return for given risk among peers.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- AUM growth `g = AUM_2020/AUM_2017 - 1`: total `123.9/31.6 - 1 = 292%` (source: about 290%); sustainable `59.5/14.6 - 1 = 308%`.
- Range = max - min (return range, vol range).
- Portfolio variance of two products: `σ_p^2 = w1^2 σ1^2 + w2^2 σ2^2 + 2 w1 w2 ρ σ1 σ2`; reducing `ρ` from 0.9 to 0.6 at equal weights and vol 30% reduces `σ_p` from 29.2% to 26.8%.
- Active-ETF flow: AUM growth conflates inflows and performance; split with `inflow = ΔAUM - AUM_{t-1} x R`.

## Mental Models
- Use **two-step selection**: theme conviction, then product methodology.
- Use **excess-return correlation vs the benchmark** to build a two-product sleeve inside a theme.
- Use **look-through to GICS, country and cap** before assuming a theme ETF diversifies your core.
- Use **past 2020 returns with suspicion**: dispersion that large (Clean Energy 38.7% to 167.3%) is mostly a holdings-and-timing effect.
- Use **sector fund for past winners, thematic for future winners**, and know which you hold.

## Worked Example
Diversification gain from picking a low-correlation Clean Energy pair (illustrative equal weights, both vols 32%).
- Raw correlation 0.95 (typical): `σ_p = 32% x sqrt((1 + 0.95)/2)` = **31.6%**.
- Excess-return correlation view: using 0.60 on the excess returns shows the fund-specific portion is substantial; at ρ = 0.88 (typical raw between fund and ETF): `σ_p = 32% x sqrt(0.94)` = **31.0%**.
- Interpretation: since the market factor dominates, mixing two products in the same theme trims less than one volatility point versus holding one (32.0% to 31.0-31.6%); the real benefit is lower index-methodology risk, not lower volatility.

## Anti-patterns
- Choosing an ETF by name without reading the index rulebook.
- Believing "AI ETF" or "Climate ETF" gives the same exposure across providers.
- Equating AUM growth with performance of theme (includes inflows and market movement).
- Buying a sector ETF thinking it is thematic or vice-versa.
- Using 2020 one-year product data (daily, EUR, short series) as evidence of manager skill.
- Overlapping themes (AI + Robotics + Digital Economy + Smart Factory) and overstating diversification.

## Key Takeaways
1. 130 thematic UCITS products, EUR 124bn at end-2020 (up about 290% in three years).
2. Sustainable products are older (3.6 years on average for funds) yet hold less AUM than non-sustainable.
3. AI, Healthcare Innovation and Robotics are about 53% of non-sustainable AUM.
4. Return dispersion within a theme can exceed 100 percentage points.
5. Index methodology, replication, cap and sector mix explain the differences.
6. Themes are cross-sector and forward-looking; sectors are classification-based.
7. Themes can hedge long-term external risks, but not perfectly.

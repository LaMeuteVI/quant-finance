# Chapter 6: International Trade and Capital Flows (Economics)

## Core Idea
Countries gain from trade by specializing according to **comparative advantage**, even when one country is better at producing everything. Trade barriers (tariffs, quotas, export subsidies, VERs) reduce global welfare and redistribute surplus. The balance of payments records how current-account imbalances are financed by the capital and financial accounts.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Terminology**:
  - **Terms of trade** = export price index / import price index. A rise is an improvement.
  - Net exports, trade surplus/deficit, autarky vs open economy.
  - **FDI** (long-term productive assets) vs **FPI** (portfolio holdings of securities).
- **World price vs autarkic price**:
  - World price < autarkic price: excess domestic demand, so the country imports.
  - World price > autarkic price: excess supply, so the country exports.
  - The further the world price is from the autarkic price, the larger the gains from trade.
- **Absolute vs comparative advantage**:
  - *Absolute*: producing with fewer resources.
  - *Comparative*: producing at a lower **opportunity cost**. This is what drives trade.
- **Ricardian model**: labor is the only factor. Comparative advantage comes from technology and productivity differences.
- **Heckscher–Ohlin (factor proportions)**:
  - Two factors (labor, capital) and identical technology.
  - Countries export goods that use their abundant factor intensively.
  - Trade **redistributes income** toward the abundant factor and exporting industries.
- **Trade restrictions**:
  - **Tariff**: consumers lose surplus; domestic producers and the government gain; there is a net deadweight loss. A *large* country can improve its terms of trade (if there is no retaliation).
  - **Quota**: similar effects, but the **quota rents** go to foreign exporters or license holders instead of the government.
  - **Export subsidy**: raises the domestic price, and the country loses welfare. It invites **countervailing duties**.
  - **VER (voluntary export restraint)**: a quota imposed by the *exporter*.
- **Regional integration, from least to most integrated**:
  1. **Free trade area**: no internal barriers, own external policies.
  2. **Customs union**: + a common external trade policy.
  3. **Common market**: + free movement of factors (labor, capital).
  4. **Economic union**: + common institutions and economic policy coordination.
  5. **Monetary union**: + a common currency.
- **Trade creation vs trade diversion**:
  - **Creation**: high-cost domestic production is replaced by lower-cost imports from members. This is good.
  - **Diversion**: lower-cost imports from non-members are replaced by higher-cost imports from members, because of the external tariff. This is bad.
  - The net effect is positive only if creation > diversion.
- **Balance of payments (BOP)**:
  - **Current account**: merchandise trade, services, income receipts, unilateral transfers.
  - **Capital account**: capital transfers (debt forgiveness, migrants' transfers), non-produced non-financial assets (patents, rights).
  - **Financial account**: claims on foreigners (reserves, FDI, securities) and foreign-owned assets at home.
  - CA + KA + FA ≈ 0, except for errors and omissions.
- **National accounts link**:
  - `CA = Y − (C + I + G) = X − M`, and `CA = S_private + (T − G) − I`.
  - A current-account deficit = importing present consumption and exporting future consumption (accumulating foreign liabilities).
- **International organizations**:
  - **IMF**: exchange-rate and balance-of-payments stability, temporary lending with conditionality.
  - **World Bank** (IBRD, which lends on market terms and is AAA-funded; IDA, which gives interest-free loans and grants): development and institutions.
  - **WTO** (1995, successor to GATT): trade rules, negotiations, dispute settlement.

## Key Concepts
- **Autarkic price**: the domestic equilibrium price without trade.
- **Intra-firm trade**: trade between subsidiaries of the same multinational.
- **Knowledge spillovers**: R&D benefits that extend beyond the firm doing it.
- **Quota rents**: excess profits earned by those who hold quota rights.

## Mental Models
- **Trade follows opportunity cost, not absolute productivity.**
- **Every current-account deficit has a mirror financial inflow.** Ask how it is being financed (FDI vs short-term portfolio money).
- **Barriers transfer surplus and destroy some of it.** Tariff revenue stays at home; quota rents leak abroad.

## Anti-patterns
- ⚠ **Correction vs the notes**: in the BOP, **credits** record exports, income received and financial inflows (sales of assets / new liabilities). **Debits** record imports, payments made and purchases of foreign assets. The notes invert parts of this.
- ⚠ **Correction**: the notes label the replacement of low-cost non-member imports by high-cost member imports as "trade creation". It is **trade diversion**.
- **Assuming tariffs protect overall welfare**: they create deadweight losses and invite retaliation.

## Worked Example
**Comparative advantage (from the notes).** A Brazilian worker makes 20 pens or 40 pencils. A Vietnamese worker makes 10 pens or 60 pencils.
- Opportunity cost of 1 pen: Brazil 40/20 = **2 pencils**; Vietnam 60/10 = **6 pencils**.
- So **Brazil has the comparative (and absolute) advantage in pens**, and **Vietnam in pencils** (1 pencil costs 1/6 pen in Vietnam vs 1/2 pen in Brazil).
- Any terms of trade between 2 and 6 pencils per pen benefit both countries.

## Key Takeaways
1. Comparative advantage (opportunity cost) drives the gains from trade.
2. Ricardian: technology differences. Heckscher–Ohlin: factor endowments, plus income redistribution.
3. Tariffs, quotas, subsidies and VERs all cut global welfare. They differ in who captures the rents.
4. Integration ladder: FTA → customs union → common market → economic union → monetary union.
5. BOP: CA + KA + FA = 0. A CA deficit means net foreign borrowing, and CA = S + (T − G) − I.

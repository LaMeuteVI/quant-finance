# Chapter 2: The Firm and Market Structures (Economics)

## Core Idea
Market structure (the number of sellers, product differentiation, barriers to entry) determines pricing power and long-run profits. Every firm maximizes profit at **MR = MC**. What differs across structures is the shape of the demand curve it faces and whether entry competes profits away.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four market structures**:

| Feature | Perfect competition | Monopolistic competition | Oligopoly | Monopoly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sellers | Many | Many | Few | One |
| Product | Identical | Differentiated | Similar/differentiated | Unique, no close substitutes |
| Entry barriers | Very low | Low | High | Very high |
| Pricing power | None (price taker) | Some | Substantial, interdependent | Considerable |
| Non-price competition | None | Advertising, quality | Marketing, features | Advertising |
| Long-run economic profit | Zero | Zero | Possible | Possible |

- **Monopoly**:
  - Profit-maximizing condition: MR = MC, with `MR = P(1 − 1/|E_p|)`.
  - So `P = MC/(1 − 1/|E_p|)`. The firm prices only in the elastic part of the demand curve.
  - Economic profit = (P − ATC)·Q. Barriers let it persist in the long run.
- **Regulating natural monopolies**:
  - **Marginal-cost pricing** (P = LRMC): efficient, but P < LRAC, so it needs a subsidy.
  - **Average-cost pricing** (P = LRAC): zero economic profit, larger output. This is the most common approach.
  - Deadweight loss = welfare under perfect competition − welfare under monopoly.
- **Price discrimination**:
  - **1st degree**: each buyer pays their reservation price, so the whole consumer surplus is captured.
  - **2nd degree**: price varies with quantity or quality (bulk pricing, economy vs business class).
  - **3rd degree**: prices differ by customer group (students, seniors), based on each group's elasticity.
- **Oligopoly models**:
  - **Kinked demand curve**: rivals match price cuts but not price increases. Demand is elastic above the kink and inelastic below it. The gap in MR explains sticky prices.
  - **Cournot (duopoly)**: each firm takes the rival's quantity as given. The equilibrium has equal quantities, and it is a **Nash equilibrium**.
  - **Stackelberg / dominant firm**: the low-cost leader sets the price and the fringe follows. In the long run the fringe shrinks.
  - **Cartels/collusion (OPEC)**: members restrict output to raise prices, but each has an incentive to cheat.
- **Conditions for a stable cartel**: few sellers with unequal shares, homogeneous products, similar cost structures, credible retaliation, weak antitrust enforcement.
- **Monopolistic competition**: short-run profit at MR = MC. Entry shifts each firm's demand curve until P = ATC (zero long-run profit), with excess capacity.
- **Perfect competition**: demand is horizontal (perfectly elastic), so P = MR = MC. Long-run economic profit is zero.

## Key Concepts
- **Natural monopoly**: the average cost is lowest with one producer (utilities). Often regulated.
- **Nash equilibrium**: no player can improve by changing strategy alone, given the others' strategies.
- **Allocative inefficiency / deadweight loss (DWL)**: the lost surplus from output below the competitive level.
- **Antitrust laws**: prevent collusion and monopolization and preserve competition.
- **Market concentration measures** (curriculum): N-firm concentration ratio, Herfindahl–Hirschman index (HHI = Σ shares²).

## Mental Models
- **MR = MC everywhere**. Market structure only changes how MR relates to P: MR = P in perfect competition, MR < P elsewhere.
- **Pricing power ∝ 1/|E|**: the markup over MC is larger when demand is less elastic.
- **Entry kills profits** in low-barrier structures. Sustainable profits require barriers such as patents, scale or network effects.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming monopolies are always inefficient**: natural monopolies with economies of scale can produce more cheaply than fragmented competition.
- **Expecting cartels to hold**: the incentive to cheat grows with the number of members and differences in their costs.
- **Using only concentration ratios**: they ignore potential entry (contestable markets).

## Worked Example
**Monopoly markup.** A monopolist faces |E_p| = 2 at its optimal output, and MC = $30.
- P = MC/(1 − 1/|E|) = 30/(1 − 0.5) = **$60**.
- MR = 60 × (1 − 1/2) = $30 = MC. ✔
- If demand became more elastic (|E| = 4), then P = 30/0.75 = **$40**, a smaller markup.

## Key Takeaways
1. Profit maximization is always at MR = MC. The structure determines MR versus P.
2. Monopoly price = MC/(1 − 1/|E|), set in the elastic range of demand.
3. Regulators use average-cost pricing (zero profit) or marginal-cost pricing plus a subsidy.
4. Oligopoly models: kinked demand (sticky prices), Cournot (Nash), Stackelberg (leader), cartels (unstable).
5. Long-run economic profit is zero in perfect and monopolistic competition.

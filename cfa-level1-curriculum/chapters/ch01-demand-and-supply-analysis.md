# Chapter 1: Topics in Demand and Supply Analysis (Economics)

## Core Idea
Elasticities measure how quantity demanded responds to own price, income and other goods' prices. On the supply side, a firm's cost structure determines its breakeven, shutdown and optimal scale decisions.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Demand function**: `Q_x = f(P_x, I, P_y)`. The inverse demand function `P_x = f(Q_x)` is what the demand curve plots.
- **Own-price elasticity**: `E_p = %ΔQ/%ΔP = (∂Q/∂P)(P/Q)`. With linear demand, elasticity changes along the curve.
  - |E| > 1: elastic. |E| = 1: unit elastic. |E| < 1: inelastic.
  - |E| = ∞: perfectly elastic. |E| = 0: perfectly inelastic.
- **Drivers of elasticity**: more elastic with close substitutes, a larger budget share, discretionary goods and a longer time horizon.
- **Elasticity and total expenditure**:
  - Total expenditure P·Q is **maximized at unit elasticity**.
  - Elastic demand: a price cut raises expenditure. Inelastic demand: a price rise raises expenditure.
- **Cross-price elasticity**: `%ΔQ_x/%ΔP_y`. Positive for substitutes (rice/pasta), negative for complements.
- **Income elasticity**: `%ΔQ/%ΔI`. Positive for normal goods, negative for inferior goods.
- **Substitution vs income effect**:
  - When a price falls, the good becomes cheaper relative to others (substitution effect), and real income rises (income effect).
  - For normal goods both effects push consumption up. For inferior goods the income effect works against the substitution effect.
- **Exceptions to the law of demand**:
  - **Giffen goods**: inferior goods, a large budget share, no close substitutes. The income effect dominates the substitution effect.
  - **Veblen goods**: luxury goods whose status value rises with price.
- **Production and costs**:
  - Total cost: `TC = wL + rK`, where r is the rental rate (opportunity cost of capital).
  - Total product, average product (AP_L = Q/L) and marginal product (MP_L = ∂Q/∂L). **Diminishing marginal returns** set in as more labor is added to fixed capital.
- **Breakeven and shutdown**:
  - **Breakeven**: AR = ATC (TR = total economic cost).
  - **Short-run shutdown**: AR < AVC.
  - **Long-run exit**: AR < ATC.
- **Economies and diseconomies of scale**: the LRAC is the envelope of the SRAC curves.
  - Downward-sloping part: economies of scale (specialization, mass production, input discounts).
  - Flat part: constant returns.
  - Upward-sloping part: diseconomies (bureaucracy, coordination problems).
  - The **minimum efficient scale** is the lowest point of the LRAC.

## Key Concepts
- **Economic profit** = TR − (explicit + implicit costs, including normal profit). **Accounting profit** = TR − explicit costs only.
- **Normal profit**: the minimum return required by investors. Treat it as a fixed cost.
- **Marginal revenue (MR)** = ∂TR/∂Q. **Marginal cost (MC)** = ∂TC/∂Q.
- **AVC** = TVC/Q. **ATC** = TC/Q. **Quasi-fixed costs** (e.g., wages) are partly fixed and partly variable.

## Mental Models
- Think "**revenue rises when you move toward unit elasticity**": raise prices where demand is inelastic, cut them where it is elastic.
- Use the **AVC test for the short run** and the **ATC test for the long run**. Fixed costs are sunk in the short run.
- **Point elasticity with linear demand**: plug the partial derivative × P/Q at the evaluation point.

## Anti-patterns
- **Confusing the slope of the demand curve with elasticity**: a linear demand curve has a constant slope but varying elasticity.
- ⚠ **Correction vs the notes**: the notes label E = 1.1 as "inelastic" and E = 0.9 as "elastic". It is the reverse. |E| > 1 is elastic and |E| < 1 is inelastic. The conclusion (maximum expenditure at |E| = 1) is correct.
- **Operating below AVC in the short run**: every unit sold increases losses. Shut down.

## Worked Example
**Point elasticities from a demand function (from the notes).** `Q_x = 84,500 − 6,390P_x + 250I − 2,000P_y`.
- Own-price: E_p = −6,390 × P_x/Q_x.
- Income: E_I = 250 × I/Q_x. Positive, so X is a normal good.
- Cross-price: E_y = −2,000 × P_y/Q_x. Negative, so X and Y are complements.
- Illustration with P_x = 10, I = 100 and P_y = 5 (values chosen for the example): Q = 84,500 − 63,900 + 25,000 − 10,000 = 35,600.
  - E_p = −6,390 × 10/35,600 = **−1.79** (elastic).
  - E_I = 250 × 100/35,600 = **0.70**.
  - E_y = −2,000 × 5/35,600 = **−0.28**.

## Key Takeaways
1. Elasticity, not slope, is what drives pricing decisions and revenue.
2. The sign of cross elasticity classifies substitutes (+) and complements (−). The sign of income elasticity classifies normal (+) and inferior (−) goods.
3. Short run: shut down if P < AVC. Long run: exit if P < ATC. Breakeven at P = ATC.
4. The minimum efficient scale sits at the lowest point of the LRAC.
5. Giffen goods (the income effect dominates) and Veblen goods (status) are exceptions to the law of demand.

# Cheatsheet — Portfolio Construction (practitioner decision rules)

## Decision rules
| When | Do | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Contractual liabilities | ALM first, liability-driven SAA | Shortfall breaks a promise (ch01, ch02) |
| Client abandons rational portfolio | Moderate cognitive, adapt emotional biases | Behaviour gap costs points a year (ch04) |
| 60/40 sold as balanced | Show risk contribution | Equity about 92% of risk (ch03) |
| Efficient large-cap class | Passive core | Sharpe's arithmetic; SPIVA failure 77-98% over 5-10y (ch05, ch10) |
| Similar ETFs | Rank on 3-5y tracking difference, then TCO | TER omits swap, sampling, tax (ch06) |
| Low TEV ETF | Still read tracking difference | Constant drag keeps TEV low (ch12) |
| Italian taxable long horizon | Harmonised UCITS, accumulating class | 26% haircut on distributions (ch06) |
| US equity exposure | Irish domicile | 15% vs 30% US withholding (ch06, ch12) |
| Illiquid or high-turnover index | Synthetic, unfunded, many counterparties | No rebalancing trades (ch07) |
| Factor tilt wanted | Multi-factor, no timing | Timing gain lost to turnover (ch08) |
| Category success rate under 25% | Default passive | Odds of picking a winner too low (ch11) |
| Evaluating active fund | FAR and net alpha, portfolio level | Correlated bets keep fees, cut risk (ch10) |
| Cannot forecast a manager's alpha | Hold the index | Optimiser amplifies input error (ch13) |
| Alternative for diversification | Test SR_new > ρ × SR_p | Correlation drives benefit (ch09) |
| Retail client, non-UCITS | Check route: Consob authorisation or EUR 500,000 | AIFMD marketing regime (ch16) |
| Illiquid assets, daily-liquid wrapper | Refuse or disclose discount | Run risk (ch15, ch19) |
| Private programme | Stress denominator, overcommit 105-115% only with liquidity plan | Harvard 2008 (ch17) |
| Need private-fund exit | Managed auction, price as % of NAV with call adjustments | GP consent, bid-ask gap (ch18) |
| ESG fund screening | Label GSIA strategy first | Labels overlap (ch20) |
| Strict exclusions | Exclusion index, not over/underweight | Weights tilt only (ch23) |
| Single ESG rating | Add second agency and engagement evidence | Methodologies diverge (ch23, ch24) |
| Small company wants investors | One SASB-style material report | Cuts questionnaire load (ch26) |
| Family firm raising capital | Board of 3+, reserved matters, tag/drag-along | PE needs governance (ch28) |

## Tells and smells
- "Passive means no decisions": replication, lending, voting are active choices (ch07).
- Fund-of-funds with dozens of strategies: overdiversification (ch10).
- Top IRR on a one-month deal: read TVPI (ch17).
- Listed private-equity ETF mixes managers and vehicles (ch19).
- ESG rating rises with disclosure volume, not change (ch23).
- Sponsor inserts (Vanguard, Amundi, J.P. Morgan, Jupiter) are vendor views (ch02, ch05, ch10, ch12, ch17, ch22).
- Hedge fund "non-directional" yet equity-correlated (ch15).

## Decision tree: which vehicle for a sleeve
1. Efficient, liquid class: passive ETF or index fund (ch06, ch12).
2. Factor premium wanted: multi-factor ETF (ch08).
3. Niche or complex (hybrids, cat bonds, small cap): shortlisted active, check FAR (ch10).
4. Uncorrelated return, daily liquidity: UCITS liquid alternative (ch15).
5. Illiquidity premium, long horizon: closed private fund (ch18).

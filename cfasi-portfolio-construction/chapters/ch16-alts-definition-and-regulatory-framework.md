# Chapter 16: Alternative Investments - Definition and General Regulatory Framework
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private (AP Advisor Private n. 12, Jul-Aug 2018), *Alternative Investments: L'importanza nel Wealth Management*, Chapter 2 "Definizione e inquadramento normativo generale sugli investimenti alternativi" | Massimiliano Saccone, CFA

## Core Idea
In the EU an "alternative" is defined **legally by exclusion**: an **AIF** (Alternative Investment Fund, FIA in Italian) is any collective investment undertaking that is **not a UCITS**. The **AIFMD** (Alternative Investment Fund Managers Directive) therefore regulates a very heterogeneous set of strategies (hedge funds, private equity, venture capital, real estate, infrastructure, funds of funds, funds in exotic assets) through the **manager**, not the strategy. Its reach is global in practice: it applies to AIFs managed in the EU and to AIFs marketed in the EU wherever domiciled. For an advisor the regulatory perimeter (AIFMD, MiFID II product governance, UCITS, plus investor-side regimes such as Solvency II or Basel III) determines **who can sell what to whom** and often explains why an investor's alternatives allocation is small.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **UCITS vs AIF perimeter test**
  - When to use: first classification step for any fund brought to a client.
  - How: (1) Is it a retail-passported UCITS (eligible assets: transferable securities, money-market instruments, cash deposits, derivatives; diversification and leverage limits; daily liquidity)? If yes, it is outside AIFMD. (2) If not, it is an AIF whatever its domicile (Luxembourg SIF, Irish QIF, German Spezialfonds, Dutch FGR, UK non-UCITS retail schemes, authorized unit trusts and investment trusts, US '40 Act funds or bank collective trusts sold in the EU). (3) Check the exclusions: holding companies, joint ventures, securitization SPEs, pension funds and company savings schemes, family offices are **not** AIFs.
  - Why it works / failure mode: the test is about legal form, not risk. Some AIFs behave like plain bond funds, some UCITS use derivatives heavily; do not infer risk from the label.
- **ESMA six-category mapping (2012 exercise)**: ESMA grouped the funds that would fall under AIFMD into:
  1. non-UCITS hedge funds similar to UCITS but without diversification and leverage limits (traditional assets: equity, equity-linked, debt);
  2. non-UCITS hedge funds using instruments UCITS cannot hold;
  3. private equity (mostly unlisted, existing or mature companies);
  4. venture capital (unlisted start-ups and small companies);
  5. real estate (property or unlisted property companies, from construction to refurbishment, across sectors);
  6. funds investing in other assets (ships, art, wine, patents and rights, forestry and timber, coins, precious metals, endowment policies, carbon instruments, life sciences, commodities).
  - Use as a mental taxonomy; the reporting standard that followed is finer (see table).
- **AIFMD manager obligations (the "common minimum")**
  - Authorization by the home supervisor is required for managers above **EUR 100m** assets under management, or **EUR 500m** if the funds use no leverage and impose a lock-in of at least **5 years** on investors; smaller managers register.
  - Non-EU managers need authorization where the relevant authority has a cooperation agreement with the EU.
  - Duties: adequate capital, an **independent depositary** (prime brokers are excluded from acting as depositary to avoid conflicts), leverage limits and reporting, controlled delegation of portfolio or risk management (no "letter-box" entities), a valuation function (internal or delegated), remuneration policies consistent with effective risk control.
  - Purpose (in force 22 July 2013): investor protection and lower systemic risk through a harmonized regime.
- **Three admissible marketing routes in Italy (post-AIFMD, in force 3 April 2015)**
  1. EU manager, EU AIF, **professional investors and certain other categories**: notification to Consob via the home authority (passport); non-professional clients can be included if the non-divisible minimum investment is at least **EUR 500,000**.
  2. EU manager, EU AIF, **Italian retail investors**: prior Consob authorization with a prospectus in Italian and marketing, organizational and disclosure conditions.
  3. EU manager marketing **non-EU AIFs**, and non-EU managers: no marketing route available until the relevant EU rules are implemented.
  - **Reverse enquiry** (investor-initiated request) is not regulated at EU level and genuine professional requests are implicitly outside "marketing". Before AIFMD it was the only practical sales channel in Italy, which historically throttled non-UCITS distribution.
- **MiFID II product governance (in force 3 January 2018)**
  - Produce and distribute only products tested and supervised for a **target market**. ESMA lists five criteria for defining it: client type, knowledge and experience, financial situation, risk tolerance, objectives and needs. The more complex the product, the more granular the definition.
  - Producers set the target market; **distributors** check their client base matches it and test suitability client by client. Distribution partners should be chosen for alignment between the distributor's clients and the product's market.
  - Consob (December 2014 communication, following an ESMA opinion on structured retail products) already treated alternative AIFs as **complex financial products** but not as "particularly complex" ones for which recommendation to retail clients is expressly discouraged.
  - Rationale: harm to investors comes from poor product construction and poor advice.
- **Investor-side regulation checklist** (what constrains institutions buying alternatives): Basel III (loss absorbency, tight limits on illiquid assets for banks); Dodd-Frank **Volcker Rule** (bans banks from investing in hedge or private equity funds); **Solvency II** (insurers: risk management and liquidity of assets, heavier capital and administrative burden for alternatives); **IORP II** (EU pension schemes, effective 12 January 2019, extends the Solvency II risk-management philosophy); US **FATCA** (tax-evasion reporting that burdens non-US entities dealing with US counterparties) and non-resident US investment tax rules.

## Key Concepts
- **AIF / FIA**: any collective investment undertaking that is not a UCITS, regardless of domicile.
- **UCITS**: EU retail-passported fund directive family with restricted eligible assets, diversification and liquidity rules.
- **AIFM**: the manager of an AIF; the AIFMD regulates the manager, not individual strategies.
- **Depositary**: independent custodian-oversight function mandatory for AIFs; the prime broker cannot fill it.
- **Passporting**: right to market an authorized fund across EU states; incomplete for AIFs, so national rules still apply.
- **Reverse enquiry**: unsolicited investor request for a product; sits outside the marketing definition.
- **Lock-in**: contractual non-redeemability; at 5+ years and no leverage it raises the AIFM authorization threshold to EUR 500m.
- **Letter-box entity**: a manager that delegates everything; delegation is allowed only if substance remains.
- **Product governance**: policies and controls over design, target market definition, distribution and monitoring across the product life cycle.
- **Target market**: the group of investors for which a product is designed; must be defined and verified by producer and distributor.
- **Complex product (Consob)**: classification applied to alternative AIFs implying extra distribution caution toward retail.
- **KYC and KIID**: Know Your Client and Key Investor Information Document, usable as relationship tools beyond compliance.
- **Volcker Rule**: US restriction on bank proprietary investment in hedge and private equity funds.

## Mental Models
- Use the "not UCITS = AIF" rule when classifying a fund: ignore marketing names and check the legal regime first.
- Use "manager regulation, not product regulation" when judging safety: AIFMD gives governance minimums (depositary, valuation, leverage reporting), not return or risk guarantees.
- Use the **three-route marketing map** before promising a client access to a fund: professional (notification), retail (authorization), non-EU (generally closed).
- Use "producer defines, distributor verifies" under MiFID II: document both the target market and why this client fits.
- Use the investor-side lens for institutions: an insurer or bank may be unable to hold alternatives even when the strategy is attractive.

## Worked Example
No numerical example. Classification walk-through:
- Inputs: a Luxembourg SIF holding a long/short equity book with 3x gross leverage, offered to a Milan family with a EUR 300,000 ticket.
- Steps: (1) not a UCITS (leverage beyond UCITS limits) so it is an AIF. (2) EU AIFM, EU AIF: route 1 or 2. (3) The family is not professional and the ticket is below EUR 500,000, so route 1 does not extend to them; route 2 needs a Consob-authorized retail offer.
- Result: the sale is not permitted via notification alone; either the ticket is raised to at least EUR 500,000 (non-divisible) or a UCITS liquid-alternative equivalent is proposed.
- Interpretation: the regulatory perimeter often decides the product before suitability does.

## Anti-patterns
- Equating "AIF" with "hedge fund": the label also covers real estate, infrastructure, funds of funds and exotic-asset funds.
- Assuming passporting makes distribution uniform across the EU: national rules still decide.
- Relying on reverse enquiry as a standing sales channel.
- Treating KYC and KIID as pure paperwork instead of relationship and suitability tools.
- Ignoring client-side constraints (Solvency II, Basel III, Volcker, IORP II) when pitching alternatives to institutions.

## Practitioner Notes
- The guide frames regulation as an opportunity: a rigorous target-market and suitability process deepens client relationships and justifies the advice.
- **Dates to remember**: AIFMD in force 22 July 2013; Italian marketing regime 3 April 2015; MiFID II 3 January 2018; IORP II 12 January 2019.
- Check which route applies before sending any marketing material; "marketing" is defined broadly (any offer, invitation or promotional message, direct or indirect).
- ESMA product-governance guidance (target market) is expected to reshape both UCITS and AIFMD products; keep records of target-market checks.
- The text states passporting was due in 2015 but was not delivered; verify current status before relying on this.

## Key Takeaways
1. An AIF is any fund that is not a UCITS, wherever domiciled.
2. AIFMD regulates managers through authorization, depositary, valuation, leverage, delegation and remuneration rules.
3. The EUR 100m / EUR 500m thresholds decide the authorization level; unlevered 5-year lock-in vehicles get the higher threshold.
4. Italian retail access to non-UCITS funds requires Consob authorization or a EUR 500,000 minimum ticket.
5. MiFID II requires a defined target market and ongoing distribution monitoring; complexity raises the bar.
6. Institutional demand is shaped by Basel III, Volcker, Solvency II and IORP II.
7. Compliance work (KYC, KIID, target market) can be reused as a client-engagement tool.

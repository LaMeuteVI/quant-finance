# Chapter 26: Sustainable Investing and the Non-Financial Statement - The Case of a Small Italian Company
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private (AP Advisor Private n. 13, Sep-Oct 2018), *Investimenti ESG*, Chapter 7 "Investimento sostenibile e Dichiarazione non finanziaria, il caso di una piccola impresa italiana" (Luca Grassadonia, CFA) + sponsor insert "Finanziare una crescita sostenibile attraverso soluzioni di investimento responsabili" (Amundi) | the FIMER SpA case is an illustrative scenario by the author

## Core Idea
Half of European assets under management apply some sustainability criterion, so even **unlisted small and medium companies** are filtered by sustainable investors and by their large-customer supply chains. The EU **Non-Financial Reporting Directive 2014/95/EU** (Italian Legislative Decree 254/2016) made a **Non-Financial Statement (DNF, Dichiarazione Non Finanziaria)** mandatory for large public-interest entities and voluntary for others. The author argues that a small company should publish a short report using the **SASB** standard (material, sector-specific, investor-oriented) to be visible to sustainable investors, avoid the flood of ESG questionnaires, and improve operations and the cost of capital. Companion insert (Amundi) sets out a practical responsible-investment menu: best-in-class, exclusion, engagement, thematic, impact.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **DNF regime (Italy)**
  - Who: "public-interest entities" (banks, insurers, issuers on EU regulated markets) that exceed size thresholds: average over 500 employees during the year **and** at balance-sheet date either total assets of at least EUR 20m or net revenues of at least EUR 40m. About 6,000 companies in Europe, 250-300 in Italy. In force from financial year 2017.
  - What: a statement covering environmental and social topics, personnel, human-rights respect and anti-corruption (active and passive) that are relevant given the company's activity; prepared under a reporting standard chosen by the company among those issued by authoritative supranational, international or national bodies; EC guidelines (Communication 2017) list 21 non-binding standards.
  - Control: the statutory auditor verifies the statement was prepared and attests consistency with the decree and the chosen standards (Consob regulation allows an attestation limited to the truthfulness of data under international practice); Consob supervises.
  - Voluntary regime (Art. 7): other firms may publish a DNF with a compliance label; board approval, filing with the companies register within 30 days of the approval of the annual accounts by shareholders, notification to Consob and web publication.
  - Interpretation (ANDAF): the information is really "pre-financial" - linked to future performance and investment value.
- **SASB standards (why the author recommends them)**
  - Independent non-profit; standards designed for voluntary use in SEC filings (10-K, 20-F); one of the EC's references for the DNF.
  - Five sustainability dimensions: **Environment** (impact feeding back into operating performance), **Social capital** (licence to operate, contribution to society), **Human capital** (management of employees and contractors), **Innovation and business model** (integrating sustainability into value creation and product innovation), **Leadership and governance** (managing issues that conflict with wider stakeholders and risk liability or loss of licence).
  - Design principles: information that is (1) material, (2) decision-useful for firms and investors, (3) cost-effective for issuers. Materiality follows US securities law ("substantial likelihood" that disclosure would alter the total mix of information for a reasonable investor), identifying financial impact in three areas: costs/revenues, assets/liabilities, cost of capital/risk profile. Characteristics: evidence-based, market-informed, industry-specific. **SICS** (Sustainable Industry Classification System) regroups firms by business model, resource intensity, sustainability impacts and innovation potential.
  - Cost-saving: minimum set of topics per industry (most already partly in financial reporting) and alignment with existing metrics.
  - Reporting context: 15,580 organizations produced 94,312 reports (Corporate Register, March 2018) but reports are costly, often partial and not focused on investor-relevant issues; 71% of top 100 firms in 41 countries and 95% of the 250 largest firms report voluntarily.
- **FIMER case (small inverter manufacturer; illustrative)**: a Brianza electro-technical SME wanting to list on AIM Italia. Arguments: (1) sustainable funds are mostly foreign; ten renewable-energy funds hold EUR 5,387m in total (Handelsbanken, Swisscanto, Pictet, Vontobel, BlackRock New Energy EUR 951m, RobecoSAM, Deutsche, NN Global Sustainable Equity EUR 2,036m, DNB, Parvest; sums verified); (2) AIM is a high-risk market where many investors have size minimums, so **demonstrating sustainability can offset information asymmetry and can matter more than being listed**; (3) in the author's inverter-competitor table, 11 competitors publish sustainability reports (including unlisted Ingeteam and Fronius) and 13 do not (including listed SolarEdge, Enphase), which illustrates that reporting is a signalling choice rather than a listing consequence; (4) a Italian pension fund (Cometa, about EUR 10bn, 400,000 members) invests only after sustainability checks, an irony if the firm's own employees' pension fund cannot hold it.
- **"Avoid the questionnaire tsunami"**: over 100 organizations produce sustainability ratings, plus investors' own questionnaires; a firm that publishes a report under a recognized standard focused on material issues can politely decline additional requests.
- **Seven reasons it pays (for the firm)**: competitive advantage; better risk management; lower cost of capital; better reputation; efficiency and waste reduction; employee loyalty; discovery of new business opportunities. **Four reasons investors like it**: more transparency, better capital allocation, stabilizes the stock, higher liquidity.
- **Evidence cited**: Harvard Business School - Oxford: firms voluntarily disclosing more SASB-material sustainability information have more influence on their share price; non-material disclosure has none (HBS, 2017). Khan, Serafeim and Yoon (Accounting Review, 2016): firms with good ratings on material issues clearly outperform; good ratings on immaterial issues do not. Goldman Sachs (2018): integrating sustainability "when done well" complements financial analysis when focused on material factors. BlackRock CEO letter (2018): firms must show positive societal contribution. LSEG ESG reporting guide (Feb 2017/Jan 2018).
- **Conclusions of the author (to be read as opinion)**: (A) since 2016 half of European financial investments follow sustainability criteria; companies that provide no information are excluded from that half and may face a markedly higher cost of capital (the "doubled" claim in the source is unsupported by a cited estimate); (B) the EU reacts: voluntary DNF protects SMEs from being cut out of supply chains; the **EC Action Plan on Financing Sustainable Growth** (COM 97/2018, 8 March 2018) includes 10 actions - a sustainability taxonomy, standards and labels, sustainable infrastructure, ESG in investment advice, sustainability benchmarks, ESG in credit ratings, investors' duties and disclosure, prudential requirements, stronger corporate disclosure, sustainable governance - with a review of non-financial guidelines due in Q2 2019; (C) SASB is the best tool for the firm, covering the DNF's areas through the main sector's indicators and neighbouring sectors if needed.
- **Responsible investing menu (Amundi insert)**
  - Four drivers: **value** (ethics/impact), **risk** (reputational, operational, financial, regulatory), **return** (energy-transition opportunities), **duty** (fiduciary, legal, NGO beliefs).
  - **Best-in-class**: Amundi grid of 36 criteria - 15 generic (energy consumption, CO2, biodiversity, working conditions, board independence) and 21 sector-specific (green financing for banks, responsible marketing for pharma); over/underweight by ESG rating across all sectors.
  - **Exclusion**: norms-based and sector (fossil fuels, tar sands, weapons, tobacco, alcohol); powerful sanction but surrenders influence.
  - **Engagement**: about 2,600 shareholder meetings and over 32,000 resolutions a year; dialogue outside meetings benefits both sides.
  - **Thematic and impact funds**: climate/low-carbon strategies, green bonds, real-asset funds, social-impact funds with measurable results; UN SDGs steer capital to positive-impact firms; EU recommendations will translate into investor duties, a classification of sustainable assets, low-carbon benchmarks and sustainability advice for banks and insurers.

## Key Concepts
- **DNF**: Dichiarazione Non Finanziaria (non-financial statement).
- **Public-interest entity (ente di interesse pubblico)**: banks, insurers and issuers on regulated markets.
- **Directive 2014/95/EU**: EU disclosure of non-financial and diversity information.
- **Legislative Decree 254/2016**: Italian transposition.
- **SASB / SICS**: US standards body and its sector classification.
- **Materiality (legal definition)**: substantial likelihood of altering the total mix of information for a reasonable investor.
- **AIM Italia**: Borsa Italiana market for small, high-risk growth companies.
- **Questionnaire tsunami**: the load of overlapping ESG data requests from raters and investors.
- **EU Action Plan on sustainable growth**: 2018 European Commission plan with 10 actions.
- **Licence to operate**: social acceptance of a company's activity.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
No formulas. DNF scope test: `employees_avg > 500 AND (total_assets >= EUR 20m OR net_revenues >= EUR 40m)`, for public-interest entities.

| Dimension (SASB) | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Environment | How do impacts feed back into operations and finances? |
| Social capital | What does society expect in return for the licence to operate? |
| Human capital | How are employees and contractors managed as value drivers? |
| Innovation and business model | Is sustainability built into products and value creation? |
| Leadership and governance | Are industry-specific conflicts with stakeholders managed? |

## Mental Models
- Use "sustainability reporting is a market-access ticket" for SMEs that sell to large buyers or want investor attention.
- Use one recognized standard and say no to other questionnaires.
- Use SASB's minimum material topic set per sector to keep cost low.
- Use the DNF threshold test before assuming mandatory reporting.
- Use the engagement-versus-exclusion trade-off: exclusion sanctions, engagement changes behaviour.

## Worked Example
**DNF applicability.** Firm A: 650 employees, total assets EUR 18m, revenue EUR 52m, listed. Test: employees > 500 yes; assets < 20m but revenue >= 40m, so condition satisfied; public-interest entity (listed): **in scope**. Firm B: 650 employees, assets EUR 18m, revenue EUR 35m: fails the size condition, so **voluntary** (Art. 7) only. Firm C: unlisted manufacturer with 120 employees: not in scope; voluntary DNF or SASB-style report as a signalling choice.

## Anti-patterns
- Producing a long glossy report on immaterial topics (costly and ignored by investors).
- Answering every questionnaire separately.
- Treating unlisted status as an excuse not to disclose when customers and investors screen suppliers.
- Using disclosure volume as a score booster (see ch23, ch24).
- Quoting the "doubling of cost of capital" claim as established fact.

## Practitioner Notes
- The FIMER story is the author's illustration; the competitor table and fund list are the author's compilation.
- For SMEs: start with the SASB standard of the main sector, then add neighbouring sectors if needed to cover the DNF headings.
- Voluntary DNF formalities: board approval, registry filing within 30 days of the shareholders' approval of accounts, notification to Consob, web publication.
- Sponsor content (Amundi) describes one manager's practices; the framework (best-in-class, exclusion, engagement, thematic, impact) is generic.

## Key Takeaways
1. DNF is mandatory for public-interest entities with over 500 employees and EUR 20m assets or EUR 40m revenue; voluntary otherwise.
2. About 6,000 EU and 250-300 Italian companies are in scope.
3. SASB offers a cost-efficient, investor-focused, sector-specific basis for the statement.
4. Material sustainability disclosure moves share prices; non-material does not.
5. A recognized standard reduces the burden of questionnaires.
6. The EU Action Plan (March 2018) will tighten disclosure further from 2019.
7. Best-in-class, exclusion, engagement, thematic and impact are complementary tools.

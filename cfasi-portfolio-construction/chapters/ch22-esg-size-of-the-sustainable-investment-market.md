# Chapter 22: ESG Investing - Size of the Sustainable Investment Market (and Financing the Climate Fight)
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private (AP Advisor Private n. 13, Sep-Oct 2018), *Investimenti ESG*, Chapter 3 "Le dimensioni del mercato degli investimenti sostenibili" (Luca Grassadonia, CFA) + sponsor insert "Contribuire a finanziare la lotta contro il cambiamento climatico" (Thierry Bogaty and Julie Fardoux, Amundi); data from GSIA Global Sustainable Investment Review 2016

## Core Idea
Sustainable and responsible investment (SRI) reached **USD 22.9 trillion** at end-2016 (+25% over 2014; compound annual growth about 11.9%), about **26% of professionally managed assets** worldwide. Europe holds about half (52.6%), the US 38%. Negative screening is the largest strategy (USD 15tn), followed by ESG integration (10.4tn) and engagement (8.4tn). Institutions dominate but retail share doubled (13% to 25-26%). In Europe and Canada the asset mix flipped from equity-heavy to bond-heavy because of green bonds. The Amundi insert shows how the climate transition becomes an investable theme: green bonds, thematic equity, low-carbon indices, divestment rules and engagement.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Reading the GSIA market data (regional scoreboard)**: use three lenses together - size (USD), growth (period and CAGR) and penetration (SRI as % of total managed assets). Penetration can fall while size rises if definitions tighten (Europe) or the total pool grows faster.
- **Strategy by region**: negative screening is the leading strategy in Europe; ESG integration leads in the US, Canada and Australia/New Zealand.
- **Investor-type split**: institutional assets dominate in nearly every region (including Italy); retail's share rose from about 13% (2014) to about 25-26% (2016).
- **Asset-class mix (Europe and Canada, the regions with allocation data)**: 2016: bonds about 64%, equities about 33%, real estate about 1.1%, private equity/venture about 0.6%, other about 1.4%. In 2014 roughly 50% equities and 40% bonds. The reversal reflects green-bond issuance.
- **Climate-finance toolkit (Amundi view)**
  1. **Sector-focused ESG scoring**: in a best-in-class approach, start with environmental ratings for the most exposed sectors (utilities, oil and gas, chemicals, metals, autos, capital goods, real estate) and assess how well firms control direct and indirect impacts.
  2. **Green bonds**: USD 339bn outstanding at end of April 2018, quadrupled in three years; example of demand-supply design: IFC-Amundi USD 2bn emerging-market green-finance programme.
  3. **Thematic equity**: energy efficiency, alternative energy, clean technology, waste and water management.
  4. **Low-carbon / decarbonization strategies**: hold sector, style and country exposure close to a standard index while cutting exposure to carbon-intensive firms. MSCI Low Carbon Leaders (launched 2014 with Swedish AP4, French FRR and Amundi): reduces carbon intensity by at least 50% versus the parent index with low tracking error.
  5. **Divestment policy**: example rule from Amundi - exclude firms whose carbon-emission weight against revenue exceeds 30% or, after qualitative forward-looking review, producers of 100 million tonnes or more of CO2 a year (the source phrase is ambiguous; read as an exclusion on carbon-intensive revenue and absolute emissions).
  6. **Voting and engagement**: voting for climate-favourable resolutions, constant dialogue with issuers, voting alliances and collective statements; investors can join Decarbonisation Coalition, Montreal Carbon Pledge, IIGCC, CDP.
- **Scale of the investment need**: USD 53tn by 2035 to meet the 2 degrees C limit; EU goal (cut CO2 by at least 40% by 2030) needs over USD 1tn per year. Global temperature has risen about 1.1 degrees C since the late 19th century (NASA).

## Key Concepts
- **SRI (Sustainable and Responsible Investment)**: broad GSIA term covering all ESG-aware strategies.
- **Penetration rate**: SRI assets divided by total professionally managed assets.
- **CAGR**: compound annual growth rate over the two-year period 2014-2016.
- **Green bond**: bond whose proceeds finance environmental projects.
- **Low-carbon index**: index replicating a parent index while reducing carbon exposure.
- **Decarbonization strategy**: portfolio construction to cut footprint at low tracking error.
- **Divestment**: selling holdings that breach emission or sector thresholds.
- **COP21**: Paris climate conference, 2015.
- **Carbon Disclosure Project (CDP)**: investor-backed carbon-reporting initiative.

## Mental Models
- Use penetration, not size alone, to judge how mainstream SRI is in a market.
- Use "definition drift" when a region's penetration falls (Europe tightened its definition, not its commitment).
- Use the green-bond share to explain why SRI portfolios have drifted toward fixed income.
- Use a low-carbon index as a drop-in for a core equity sleeve when the client wants a footprint cut without a style or sector bet.

## Worked Example
**Implied total market.** Global SRI of USD 22.89tn is 26.3% of managed assets, so total professionally managed assets are `22.89 / 0.263 = about USD 87tn`. In 2014: `18.28 / 0.302 = about USD 60.5tn`. Total assets grew about 44% while SRI grew 25%, hence the fall in global penetration from 30.2% to 26.3% even as SRI size rose.

## Anti-patterns
- Quoting "ESG market size" without stating the GSIA definition (screening-heavy totals overlap across strategies, and the figure is adjusted for overlap).
- Comparing 2014 and 2016 European figures as like-for-like (definition narrowed).
- Treating the very high growth in Japan or Australia as organic: the base was tiny or definitions changed.
- Counting green-bond demand as proof of climate impact without checking use of proceeds.

## Practitioner Notes
- Climate risk is a risk-management theme for investors with long horizons; the sponsor positions it also as an opportunity (thematic and green bonds).
- Low-carbon index replication is the lowest-friction way to decarbonize a passive allocation.
- Voting alliances raise the weight of small holders; use them if a firm's engagement is otherwise ignored.
- Sponsor content: treat product claims (Amundi) as illustrative of approach, not as endorsements.

## Key Takeaways
1. SRI assets: USD 22.9tn, up 25% in two years, about 26% of managed assets.
2. Europe 52.6% and US 38.1% of SRI assets.
3. Negative screening is largest (USD 15tn); integration 10.4tn; engagement 8.4tn.
4. Institutions dominate; retail doubled to a quarter.
5. Europe/Canada allocation moved from equity-heavy to bond-heavy because of green bonds (USD 339bn in April 2018).
6. Low-carbon indices cut footprint by 50%+ with low tracking error.
7. Climate goals imply multi-trillion-dollar investment needs.

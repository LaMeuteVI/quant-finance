# Chapter 21: ESG Investing - The Growth of ESG Investments
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private (AP Advisor Private n. 13, Sep-Oct 2018), *Investimenti ESG*, Chapter 2 "La crescita degli Investimenti ESG" | Matt Orsagh, CFA and Antonella Puca, CFA (CFA Institute)

## Core Idea
ESG investing already represents over a quarter of global investments. It was institutionalized by the **UN Principles for Responsible Investing (PRI)**, sustained by large European asset owners (Norway's Government Pension Fund Global, Dutch ABP), and is spreading to alternatives, exchanges and households. The main obstacle to further growth is **data availability and quality**, together with the (still contested) link between ESG and financial performance: belief in a performance advantage is the factor most likely to convert non-adopters.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **PRI institutionalization path**: 2005 UN Secretary-General Kofi Annan invites 50+ chief executives of major institutions to a UN Global Compact project, with IFC and the Swiss government, to integrate ESG into capital markets; result: PRI, a UN-supported association with over 1,700 signatories and over USD 70 trillion assets under management. Definition used: responsible investment incorporates ESG factors into decisions to improve risk management and generate sustainable long-term returns.
- **Adoption signals**: Norway's sovereign fund and ABP lead institutions; households growing; AIMA/CAIS 2018 survey: alternative funds integrating ESG more, with at least USD 59bn allocated to responsible investments and rising demand; Borsa Italiana (LSEG) joined the UN Sustainable Stock Exchanges Initiative (over 70 exchanges) and published an ESG reporting guide for issuers in 2017.
- **CFA Institute ESG surveys (2015, 2017)**: over 73% of respondents consider ESG factors; interest in each of E, S and G rising: governance about 64% to 67%, environmental about 50% to 54%, social about 49% to 54% (reading of chart values). Ethics remains a driver, especially for institutions with mission statements; for many investors integration is mainly a way to improve medium/long-term **risk-adjusted** return regardless of ethics.
- **Data-quality problem and its fixes**: still large variance in the type and quality of corporate ESG data and calculation methods. Responses: **SASB** (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board, US) for standardized industry-specific disclosure; **EFFAS** KPIs for ESG (version 3.0, 2010) by sector; proprietary KPI systems by rating agencies, data vendors, index providers and managers; growing demand for independent verification and certification of data and processes.
- **Adoption lever**: the perceived performance advantage is the main determinant of adoption by non-integrators; CFA Institute and PRI are collaborating to track global integration.

## Key Concepts
- **PRI**: UN-backed signatory network promoting responsible investment principles.
- **UN Global Compact**: UN corporate sustainability initiative from which PRI emerged.
- **SSEI**: Sustainable Stock Exchanges Initiative, UN-promoted network of exchanges.
- **SASB**: US body setting industry-specific sustainability disclosure standards.
- **EFFAS KPIs**: European analyst-federation key performance indicators for ESG by sector.
- **ESG data variance**: differences in what companies disclose and how factors are calculated.
- **Independent assurance / certification**: third-party verification of ESG data and processes.
- **Risk-adjusted return**: return per unit of risk; the central performance argument for ESG integration.

## Mental Models
- Use "data quality is the bottleneck" when an ESG score looks decisive: ask what it is built on.
- Use SASB or EFFAS sector KPIs as the checklist of what is financially material in a sector.
- Use "ESG as risk management" to explain integration to clients who dislike value judgements.
- Use signatory status as a screen for process commitment, not as proof of performance.

## Anti-patterns
- Using different ESG scores for the same issuer without reconciling methodologies.
- Treating PRI signatory status as evidence of good ESG outcomes.
- Presuming performance benefits while the evidence is still being built.

## Practitioner Notes
- Prefer sector-specific materiality (SASB, EFFAS) over generic ESG checklists.
- Ask providers about verification: is the input data audited or self-reported?
- Hedge funds and alternatives are now part of the ESG story, not an exception.

## Key Takeaways
1. ESG is over one quarter of global investment.
2. PRI (2005) institutionalized it: 1,700+ signatories, USD 70tn+.
3. Large European asset owners and exchanges are pushing adoption.
4. Governance is the most considered factor; E and S are catching up.
5. Data quality and standardization remain the main hurdles.
6. Belief in a performance benefit drives adoption.

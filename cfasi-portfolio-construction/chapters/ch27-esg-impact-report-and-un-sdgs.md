# Chapter 27: The Impact Report - Extra-Financial Analysis Against the UN Sustainable Development Goals
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private (AP Advisor Private n. 13, Sep-Oct 2018), *Investimenti ESG*, Chapter 8 "Il Report di impatto dell'analisi extra-finanziaria sugli Obiettivi di Sviluppo Sostenibile delle Nazioni Unite" | Marina Pardini (Etica SGR case study)

## Core Idea
Asset owners and regulators increasingly ask what a portfolio *does*, not only what it returns. An **impact report** measures the ESG performance of a fund's holdings against a reference market using indicators mapped to the **UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)**. The chapter lays out the demand drivers (pension funds, French climate-reporting law, US DOL, fiduciary-duty trend, universal owners), the SDG framework and measurement tools, and a full **case study** of an Italian sustainable equity fund (118 issuers) versus the iShares MSCI ACWI ETF (1,322 issuers): a three-stage indicator selection (122 -> 57 indicators), a delta metric, SDG-by-SDG results, a climate section with TCFD metrics (weighted average carbon intensity, total attributable emissions, carbon footprint) and aggregate results (E 43%, S 28%, G 21%). This is a reusable template for building an impact report.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Why demand for impact reporting is rising**: institutional investors and regulators; pension funds that already measure and report impact to beneficiaries (PFZW, Cbus, AP2, Local Government Super); US Department of Labor guidance (2015) on ESG in plan decisions; France (2015) first country to require institutional investors to report on climate impact; spreading view that ESG integration is part of fiduciary duty (EU consideration); **universal owners** (very large, diversified investors) care because negative externalities (climate, resource depletion) raise systemic costs and hurt their returns.
- **Impact investing growth**: the fastest-growing strategy though smallest in assets: +146% from 2014 to 2016 (GSIA). Impact funds report results using metrics set at fund design with ad hoc data collection from usually unlisted investees. ESG-integrated listed funds are usually measured ex post on financial return and risk; the assumption that ESG integration yields higher ESG performance than non-ESG portfolios is testable with ESG metrics. Assogestioni defines a sustainable and responsible fund as one whose policy bans or favours securities based on analysis integrating ESG criteria.
- **SDGs as the common framework**: 17 goals agreed by 193 UN members (September 2015) for 2030, combining growth, social inclusion and environmental protection; unlike the Millennium Development Goals (2000) they involve the private sector. UNCTAD estimates the required private-sector contribution at USD 1.4 trillion a year. Examples: PME (Dutch pension) committed in March 2017 to invest 10% of its portfolio SDG-aligned; Union Investment launched an SDG equity fund (2018) for companies with over 50% of revenues exposed to SDG themes.
  - Tools: Investment Leaders Group (Cambridge CISL) methodology for measuring SDG impact; Dutch SDG Initiative indicator list per SDG; Bloomberg and Trucost SDG screening tools. Obstacle: availability, quality and granularity of input data (a KPMG study found only 4 in 10 of the world's largest firms reference SDGs in corporate reports); expect reporting to grow.
- **Impact-report method (seven steps)**
  1. Pick a reference market proxy: here the iShares MSCI ACWI ETF (no ESG integration) versus the fund.
  2. Source ESG indicators with SDG links **different from those used for ex-ante selection**, to avoid circularity (using the same indicators ex post as ex ante), with regular update (annual at least). Sources mainly Bloomberg and CDP, data at 31/12/2016.
  3. Map each indicator to E, S or G and to an SDG (e.g. "companies with at least 20% female board representation" -> G and SDG 5). Result: 122 indicators.
  4. Coverage filter: keep indicators covering at least 70% of holdings in both portfolios (67% for climate, given its importance): 57 indicators (20 E, 13 S, 24 G).
  5. Compute, for each indicator, the share of companies meeting the criterion in the fund (`a`) and in the market (`b`) (percentages avoid size distortions between 118 and 1,322 issuers).
  6. Compute the delta: `delta = (a - b) / b`. **Correction**: the source prints `(a - b) / a`, but its reported figures (e.g. 79% vs 70% -> 13%; 57% vs 44% -> 30%) reproduce only with `b` in the denominator.
  7. Focus on indirectly material SDGs identified by the asset manager's responsibility policy and engagement strategy; aggregate by simple average per E, S, G.
- **Case study results (indicator, fund vs market, delta)**
  - **SDG 5 Gender equality**: non-discrimination policies 79% vs 70% (+13%); women on boards 57% vs 44% (+30%); women in management delta +7% (management representation is about half the board level; the Golfo-Mosca law 120/2011 quotas may explain board levels).
  - **SDG 8 Decent work**: health and safety risk recognition and improvement commitment 79% vs 70% (+13%); child-labour prevention and human-rights commitments delta over +30%; UN Global Compact adherence confirms it; supply-chain human-rights initiatives delta +44%; whistleblowing 68% vs 69% (-1%, widely diffused); workforce growth delta +24%; fair-wage (living wage) commitment only 2% in both portfolios.
  - **SDG 12 Sustainable production and consumption**: waste reduction 74% vs 57% (+30%); eco-packaging and eco-friendly offices: fund 27% and 42%, deltas about 80% and 50% (sector tilt toward industrials may inflate the packaging delta); supplier environmental impact attention: both higher than direct impact, delta +41%.
  - **SDG 13 Climate action (CDP data)**: emission-reduction targets (Scope 1 and 2) 86% vs 57% (+51%); science-based targets 20% vs 17% (+18%); low-carbon products 66% vs 47% (+40%); CDP climate score: about 1 in 2 fund companies scores A versus 1 in 4 in the market (delta 80%); non-respondents 14% in the fund versus 36% in the market.
  - **SDG 15 Life on land**: biodiversity initiatives 42% vs 31% (+35%); environmental quality management systems (e.g. ISO 14001) delta 52%.
  - **Aggregate**: simple average of deltas E 43%, S 28%, G 21% (mean about 31%): the fund's ex-ante ESG selection delivers higher ESG performance, especially environmental.
- **Climate focus (TCFD-aligned)**: the manager joined the Montreal Carbon Pledge in 2015 (measure and report portfolio emissions annually). TCFD (Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures, set up by the FSB, 2017 recommendations) preferred metrics:
  - **Weighted Average Carbon Intensity (WACI)** `= sum_i w_i * (Scope1_i + Scope2_i) / Revenue_i`, in tCO2e per EUR million revenue. Scope 3 excluded because of data difficulty and double counting. Strengths: shows the effect of allocation and stock picking. Limits: outlier-sensitive; revenue normalization favours higher-margin firms. Fund 334.38 tCO2e/EUR mn, delta -85% versus market (a negative delta is favourable here). Fund beats market in every sector except consumer staples and industrials; part of the gap is caused by exclusion of controversial firms and sub-sector mix; about 80% of the market's under-performance comes from the materials sector.
  - **Total attributable emissions** `= sum_i (investor shares_i / shares outstanding_i) * Emissions_i`. Fund: 63,400 tCO2e versus market ETF 1,186,520 tCO2e; very different because of portfolio size (fund EUR 263.3m versus ETF EUR 5,559.1m at 31/12/2016). Sector shares of fund emissions: materials 65.4% (market 25.9%), utilities 14% (market 30.7%), industrials 10.9% (market 7.9%); energy and financials underweight owing to exclusion of oil companies and financials.
  - **Carbon footprint** `= attributable emissions / EUR million invested`: fund 271.69 tCO2e per EUR mn (about 27 kg per EUR 100 invested) versus ETF 213.54 (21.3 kg per EUR 100). The ETF's larger size gives economies of scale per unit invested. Check: ETF 1,186,520 / 5,559.1 = 213.4. For the fund, 63,400 / 263.3 = 240.8, so the printed 271.69 implies about EUR 233m of invested equity base (not stated in the source).
  - The most used metric among Montreal Carbon Pledge signatories is the carbon footprint.
- **Conclusion of the case**: ex-ante ESG selection plus engagement produces better ESG outcomes than a conventional financial-only market proxy, especially in environment (climate indicators).

## Key Concepts
- **Impact report**: structured comparison of a portfolio's ESG outcomes with a benchmark, indicator by indicator.
- **SDG**: UN Sustainable Development Goal; 17 goals to 2030.
- **Universal owner**: very large diversified investor exposed to systemic externalities.
- **Delta**: relative difference between fund and market share meeting a criterion.
- **Coverage threshold**: minimum share of holdings with data for an indicator to count.
- **Scope 1 / 2 / 3 emissions**: direct; purchased-energy indirect; all other value-chain emissions (GHG Protocol).
- **WACI**: weighted average carbon intensity.
- **Attributable emissions**: portfolio's pro-rata share of investees' emissions.
- **Carbon footprint**: attributable emissions per unit invested.
- **Science-based targets**: emission targets aligned with a 1.5 degree C pathway.
- **Montreal Carbon Pledge**: investor commitment to measure and publicly report portfolio carbon footprint.
- **TCFD**: FSB task force on climate-related financial disclosure.
- **Whistleblowing system**: channel to report violations anonymously.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- `delta = (a - b) / b` (corrected form); share meeting criterion: `a` fund, `b` market.
- `WACI = sum_i [ w_i * (E1_i + E2_i) / Rev_i ]` (tCO2e per EUR million revenue).
- `Attributable emissions = sum_i [ (Shares held_i / Shares outstanding_i) * E_i ]`.
- `Carbon footprint = Attributable emissions / Invested EUR mn`.
- Aggregate: `delta_E = mean of E deltas` (E 43%, S 28%, G 21%).

## Mental Models
- Use the SDG map as a common vocabulary when a client asks "what does my portfolio contribute to".
- Use percentage-of-companies indicators when portfolios differ greatly in size.
- Use separate indicator sets for ex-ante selection and ex-post reporting.
- Use WACI to compare allocation effects, footprint to compare cost per euro, attributable emissions for absolute size; never one alone.
- Use sector decomposition before concluding on carbon results (materials and utilities drive most).

## Worked Example
**Delta and footprint (recomputed).**
- Board gender: fund 57%, market 44% -> `delta = (57 - 44) / 44 = 29.5%` (source 30%). With the printed formula `(a - b)/a` it would be 22.8%.
- Carbon footprint (market ETF): `1,186,520 tCO2e / 5,559.1 EUR mn = 213.4 tCO2e per EUR mn`, i.e. 21.3 kg per EUR 100 (source 213.54).
- Check the fund: `63,400 / 263.29 = 240.8` against the printed 271.69; the gap suggests a smaller denominator (equity assets of about EUR 233m).
- Interpretation: lower absolute emissions (63,400 versus 1,186,520) mostly reflect size, whereas per-euro footprint is slightly higher in the fund (sector tilt to materials), a reminder to compare normalized metrics.

## Anti-patterns
- Comparing absolute emissions across portfolios of different size.
- Using the same indicators to select and to evaluate.
- Reporting a single aggregate ESG number without indicator detail.
- Over-reading small deltas on indicators with low prevalence (e.g. living wage at 2%).
- Ignoring coverage: indicators with thin data distort the average.

## Practitioner Notes
- Expect weaker data on S and G indicators than on climate (CDP is a strong single source).
- Sector tilts (industrials overweight, oil and financials excluded) change results; disclose them.
- Impact reports support fiduciary conversations; pair them with engagement priorities.
- The study is a single fund at one date (31 December 2016); not evidence of causation.

## Key Takeaways
1. Impact reporting answers what a portfolio contributes, not only what it earns.
2. SDGs offer a ready-made, global indicator framework.
3. Select indicators with at least 70% coverage and avoid circularity with the selection process.
4. Express results as percent-of-companies and a relative delta; use `(a - b)/b`.
5. Aggregate results (E 43%, S 28%, G 21%) show an ESG advantage mainly on environment.
6. TCFD metrics: WACI, attributable emissions and footprint each answer different questions.
7. Data availability remains the main obstacle, and is improving.

# Glossary — CFA Quantitative Methods (L1 · L2)

**Adjusted R²** — R² penalized for the number of regressors, `1 − [(n−1)/(n−k−1)](1−R²)`; can fall when a variable is added (ch 08)
**Annuity due / ordinary annuity** — level payments starting at t = 0 / t = 1; due = ordinary × (1+r) (ch 01)
**ARCH** — autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity; squared residuals correlate over time, `σ²ₜ₊₁ = a₀ + a₁ε²ₜ` (ch 09)
**Arithmetic vs geometric mean** — arithmetic for a one-period expected return; geometric (compound) for realized multi-period growth; harmonic ≤ geometric ≤ arithmetic (ch 02)
**AR(p) model** — regression of a series on its own p lags; valid only for covariance stationary series with clean residuals (ch 09)
**Bagging / random forest** — averaging many models fitted on bootstrap samples; forest adds random feature subsets per split (ch 10)
**Bag-of-words / document term matrix (DTM)** — order-free token counts / matrix of documents by tokens used as ML input (ch 11)
**Bayes' formula** — `P(E|I) = [P(I|E)/P(I)]·P(E)`; updates a prior with new information (ch 03)
**Bias-variance trade-off** — out-of-sample error = bias (underfit) + variance (overfit) + base noise (ch 10)
**Binomial distribution** — number of successes in n independent Bernoulli trials with constant p; mean np, variance np(1−p) (ch 04)
**Central limit theorem (CLT)** — the sample mean of large samples is approximately N(μ, σ²/n) for any finite-variance population (ch 05)
**Chebyshev's inequality** — at least `1 − 1/k²` of observations lie within k standard deviations, any distribution (ch 02)
**Chi-square test** — test statistic `(n−1)s²/σ₀²`, df n−1, for one variance; asymmetric, not robust to non-normality (ch 06)
**Coefficient of variation** — `s/X̄`, relative dispersion (ch 02)
**Cointegration** — two unit-root series share a stationary linear combination; tested by Engle-Granger (ch 09)
**Confidence interval** — point estimate ± reliability factor × standard error, with confidence 1−α (ch 05)
**Confusion matrix** — TP, FP, FN, TN counts behind precision, recall, accuracy, F1 (ch 11)
**Consistent / efficient / unbiased estimator** — converges as n grows / smallest variance among unbiased / expected value equals parameter (ch 05)
**Continuous compounding** — limit of compounding; `FV = PV·e^(rₛN)`, `EAR = e^rₛ − 1` (ch 01, ch 04)
**Correlation** — `Cov/(σσ)`, bounded in [−1, 1], linear only; zero correlation does not imply independence (ch 03)
**Covariance** — probability-weighted product of deviations from means; drives diversification (ch 03)
**Covariance stationary** — constant finite mean and variance, autocovariance depends only on lag (ch 09)
**Cross-validation (k-fold)** — rotate k−1 folds for training and 1 for validation; not valid for time series (ch 10)
**Degrees of freedom** — independent observations left after estimating parameters (n−1 for a mean-based variance, n−2 for simple regression, n−k−1 multiple) (ch 05, ch 06, ch 07, ch 08)
**Dickey-Fuller test** — H₀: unit root (`g₁ = b₁ − 1 = 0`); needs special critical values (ch 09)
**Durbin-Watson** — `DW ≈ 2(1−r)` test for first-order serial correlation; invalid with a lagged dependent variable (ch 08, ch 09)
**Effective annual rate (EAR)** — `(1 + rₛ/m)^m − 1`, annual growth including compounding (ch 01)
**Excess kurtosis / skewness** — tail weight relative to the normal (kurtosis − 3) / asymmetry of the distribution (ch 02)
**F1 score** — harmonic mean of precision and recall (ch 11)
**Feature engineering / selection** — create or transform features / choose a subset of existing ones (ch 11)
**F-test** — in regression, `MSR/MSE` for joint significance; in variances, `s₁²/s₂²` for equality of two variances (ch 06, ch 07, ch 08)
**Heteroskedasticity** — non-constant error variance; conditional (related to X) distorts standard errors; tested with Breusch-Pagan, fixed with robust SE (ch 08)
**Hypothesis testing** — framework to decide between H₀ and Hₐ using a test statistic and significance level (ch 06)
**Interest rate components** — real risk-free rate + inflation + default + liquidity + maturity premiums (ch 01)
**LASSO** — penalized regression minimizing squared errors plus `λΣ|b|`; performs feature selection (ch 10)
**Logit (logistic regression)** — models log odds linearly; `e^b` is the odds ratio; fitted by maximum likelihood (ch 08)
**Lognormal distribution** — Y with ln Y normal; floored at 0 and right-skewed; models prices (ch 04)
**Look-ahead bias** — test uses information not yet available on the test date (ch 05)
**Mean reversion level** — `b₀/(1−b₁)` in an AR(1) (ch 09)
**Monte Carlo simulation** — repeated random draws from specified distributions to estimate a distribution of outcomes (ch 04)
**Multicollinearity** — highly correlated regressors; inflates standard errors (high R², significant F, insignificant t's) (ch 08)
**Nonparametric test** — test using ranks or signs with few assumptions (Spearman, Wilcoxon, Mann-Whitney, sign) (ch 06)
**Normal distribution** — symmetric, fully described by mean and variance; basis of z-scores (ch 04)
**Odds** — `P/(1−P)`; "a to b" for an event means probability a/(a+b) (ch 03, ch 08)
**OLS** — ordinary least squares: chooses coefficients minimizing the sum of squared residuals (ch 07)
**Overfitting** — model fits noise, low training error but high test error (ch 10, ch 11)
**PCA** — principal component analysis; uncorrelated composites ordered by explained variance (ch 10)
**Power of a test** — probability of rejecting a false null, 1−β (ch 06)
**Precision / recall** — TP/(TP+FP) / TP/(TP+FN); choose by the cost of false positives vs false negatives (ch 11)
**Prediction interval** — forecast ± t × s_f; wider than a confidence interval for the mean (ch 07)
**Present value / future value** — `FV = PV(1+r)^N`; values of cash flows at different dates on a time line (ch 01)
**p-value** — smallest significance level at which H₀ can be rejected (ch 06)
**R²** — share of variance explained, `SSR/SST`; equals r² in simple regression (ch 07, ch 08)
**Random walk** — `xₜ = xₜ₋₁ + εₜ`; has a unit root and no mean-reverting level (ch 09)
**Roy's safety-first criterion** — maximize `[E(R)−R_L]/σ` to minimize P(R < R_L) (ch 04)
**Sample selection / survivorship bias** — non-random exclusion of members, e.g., failed funds dropped from databases (ch 05)
**Sampling distribution / standard error** — distribution of a statistic across samples / its standard deviation, `σ/√n` for the mean (ch 05)
**Serial correlation** — errors correlated over time; understates SE (positive); fixed with Newey-West (ch 08, ch 09)
**Significance level (α) / Type I error** — probability of rejecting a true H₀ (ch 06)
**Spearman rank correlation** — correlation of ranks, `1 − 6Σd²/[n(n²−1)]`; nonparametric (ch 06)
**Standard error of estimate (SEE)** — `√MSE`, typical regression residual size (ch 07)
**Stratified random sampling** — simple random draws within strata sized in proportion to the population (ch 05)
**Student's t-distribution** — symmetric, fatter-tailed than normal, df-indexed, approaches z as df grows (ch 05, ch 06)
**Supervised / unsupervised learning** — labeled target vs structure-finding without labels (ch 10)
**Time value of money** — cash flows are comparable only after moving them to the same date (ch 01)
**Time-series vs cross-sectional data** — one unit over time vs many units at one date (ch 05)
**Type II error (β)** — failing to reject a false H₀ (ch 06)
**Unit root** — lag coefficient of 1; series is nonstationary, difference it (ch 09)
**Value at Risk (VaR)** — loss exceeded with a small probability over a horizon (ch 04)
**Variance (population vs sample)** — divisor N vs n−1; the latter is unbiased (ch 02, ch 05)
**Winsorization / trimming** — replace vs remove outliers (ch 02, ch 11)
**z-test** — test statistic `(X̄−μ₀)/(σ/√n)` standard normal; known σ or large-sample alternative (ch 05, ch 06)

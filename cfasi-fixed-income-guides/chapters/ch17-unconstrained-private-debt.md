# Chapter 17: Private Debt
> **Source**: CFA Society Italy × Advisor Private, "Unconstrained Fixed Income" guide (Sept–Oct 2019), Chapter 6 (Private debt) | Matteo Soriani and Federica Colli Vignarelli (Anthilia Capital Partners SGR)

## Core Idea
Private debt is non-listed (or professional-segment-only) lending to mid-sized companies, through unlisted or minibond-style bonds and direct lending, complementing bank credit. Investors accept limited liquidity in exchange for a liquidity premium, bespoke covenants, stronger negotiating power, limited correlation to rates and liquid markets, and lower realized defaults than spread levels imply. Access is mostly through closed-end funds with a 6-10 year life; ELTIFs now open the segment to wealthy non-professional investors. The key barrier to entry is origination and credit selection skill.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Segments**: (1) **senior private debt**: unlisted (or professional-only) senior bonds issued by SMEs for general financing; (2) **structured private debt**: unlisted bonds for extraordinary or complex transactions (LBO, acquisitions, restructurings), often subordinated, e.g. **mezzanine**: fixed interest plus an equity kicker, possible conversion into equity; (3) **direct lending**: loans made directly by regulated specialist funds without issuing a financial instrument.
- **Use cases**: short-term, small amounts (cash and working capital) via direct lending or short senior deals; medium-long term for growth projects (organic or M&A), reorganisation, debt restructuring, generational transfer.
- **Market data (2019)**: global private debt AUM from slightly above USD 200 billion in 2007 to USD 770 billion (June 2018, Preqin); USD 110 billion raised in 2018; dry powder USD 307 billion; projected doubling to USD 1.4 trillion by 2023, surpassing real estate and third in AUM after hedge funds and private equity. Italy: market opened in 2012 (Decreto Sviluppo); from April 2013 to June 2019 about EUR 4.6 billion nominal in about 480 issues (Anthilia); fund raising EUR 297 million in 2018 and over EUR 2 billion cumulative since 2013 (banks, fund of funds, insurers, pension funds).
- **Why it grew**: regulation reduced bank lending capacity; compressed rates pushed investors toward new yield sources; SME access to bank credit tightened after the 2011 sovereign crisis; Italy removed legal limits on SME bond issuance and made interest deductible (up to 30% of EBITDA, as for bank loans, for listed bonds or those held by institutional investors).
- **Typical terms**: tenor 3-8 years (range under 18 months to 10 years); bullet or amortizing with grace period (interest-only) then amortization tied to the business plan; fixed coupon paid quarterly or semi-annually; listed on professional segments or placed privately.
- **Ranking**: senior (ordinary, lower yield) vs mezzanine (hybrid, higher yield, equity kicker, conversion). Typically unsecured; where needed, a security package (inventory pledge, bank guarantee, cash collateral). Public guarantee programs: Italy's Fondo Centrale di Garanzia (from April 2014; up to 80% of issue, max EUR 2.5 million per issuer, state as last guarantor) and EU InnovFin (EIF, Horizon 2020, EFSI).
- **Rating**: typically unrated; investor may commission an unsolicited, confidential rating.
- **Covenant architecture**
  | Type | Content | Mechanics |
  |---|---|---|
  | Financial | Thresholds on Net financial debt/EBITDA, Net debt/equity, total debt, interest charges/EBITDA tested on year-end accounts | Breach gives right to accelerate (after checking whether it stems from liquidity stress); waiver negotiable with a penalty |
  | Information | Notify tax/judicial notices, price-sensitive events, send audited accounts on time, share key financials sub-annually | Early monitoring |
  | General | Limits on extraordinary capital operations outside the plan, granting guarantees, pledging assets, distributing profits/reserves, selling assets or stakes, replacing key managers | Protect structure and credit quality |
  | Pricing ratchets | Step-up/step-down clauses: coupon rises if a ratio crosses a threshold (before the covenant), falls if the ratio is below another threshold; applies until the next approved accounts | Transparency, negotiation lever |
- **Comparison with traditional bonds**
  | | Private debt | Listed corporate/HY bonds |
  |---|---|---|
  | Liquidity | Limited, buy and hold | High |
  | Yield | Senior Italian issues 4%-6% at issue; liquidity premium over benchmark curves; low correlation to interbank/government rates (Anthilia data) | Spread vs benchmark |
  | Covenants | Tight, monitorable, early repayment right | Looser |
  | Negotiation | Few counterparties: more bargaining power at issue and in restructuring | Standardised |
  | Issuer benefits | Flexible schedule, mostly unsecured, reputation (over 90% of Italian issuers entered capital markets for the first time), media coverage | Market access for larger firms |
  | Target investor | Seeks higher coupons, low correlation, lower default rate than spread implies, good recovery | Liquid portfolio |
- **Fund structure**: closed-end, life 6-10 years, three phases (fundraising, investment period about half the life, divestment/repayment). Investment evaluation lasts 6-8 weeks in six steps: origination (pipeline, teasers) → screening (mandate fit, minimum info) → credit analysis (management meetings, site visits, sector and competitive position, governance, financials, business plan validation and stress tests, possible parallel rating) → rating and negotiation (covenants, guarantees, rate, tenor, grace period; independent opinions from risk and compliance, possibly binding investor committee) → subscription (legal documentation, possible listing, coordination) → monitoring (payments, covenant tests, management meetings, risk-return at name and portfolio level, reporting).
- **ELTIF**: European Long Term Investment Fund, closed-end, introduced by a 2015 EU regulation; for unlisted European SMEs; Italy's Decreto Crescita (June 2019) gives tax exemption on income from investments made in 2020 and held at least five years.
- **Fund investor mix (Italy)**: banks 26.2%, fund of funds 21.4%, insurers 15.2%, pension institutions 15.8%, public sector and sovereign funds 8.0%, foundations 3.7%, HNWI and family offices 4.3%, private fund of funds 1.8%, industrial and others 3.4% (AIFI, Anthilia).

## Key Concepts
- **Private debt** — non-listed lending to companies, outside public bond markets.
- **Direct lending** — fund-originated loans.
- **Mezzanine** — hybrid debt with equity kicker.
- **Minibond** — Italian small/medium issuer bond (see ch03).
- **Equity kicker** — profit-linked return component.
- **Dry powder** — committed but uninvested capital.
- **Grace period** — interest-only initial period.
- **Waiver** — lender's agreement not to accelerate on breach, usually for a fee.
- **Step-up/step-down** — pricing grid tied to ratios.
- **Liquidity premium** — extra yield for illiquidity.
- **Unsolicited rating** — private rating requested by investor.
- **ELTIF** — retail-accessible long-term fund wrapper.
- **Club deal** — loan or bond placed with few investors.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- Leverage covenant: `Net financial debt / EBITDA ≤ threshold`; interest cover: `EBITDA / Financial charges ≥ threshold`.
- Pricing ratchet: `Coupon = Base coupon + Step-up (if ratio > trigger) − Step-down (if ratio < lower trigger)`.
- Liquidity premium: `Private yield − yield of comparable listed bond with equal rating and duration`.
- Italian deduction rule: interest deductible up to 30% of gross operating result (EBITDA) for listed bonds or those held by institutional investors, plus issuance costs.

## Mental Models
- Use private debt as a buy-and-hold carry sleeve, sized so that illiquidity does not force sales.
- Use covenants as the early warning system: tight terms are the investor's compensation for lack of listing.
- Use the six-step process to evaluate any private debt fund's discipline; the quality of origination and monitoring matters more than headline yield.
- Use pricing ratchets to read how a deal was negotiated and how stress is handled.
- Use ELTIF or specialized funds instead of single-name exposure unless you have the credit team.

## Worked Example
Evaluate a senior private placement against a listed bond (writer's illustration, using the guide's range).
1. Private senior coupon 5.0%, 5-year bullet, quarterly pay; listed BB-/B+ 5-year bond yields 3.8% at similar credit assessment.
2. Liquidity premium `= 5.0% − 3.8% = 1.2%` (120 bp).
3. Expected-loss check: assume annual default 1.5% and recovery 60% (high-recovery in senior secured-like structures): expected loss `1.5% × 40% = 0.60%` a year, leaving `1.2% − 0.6% = 0.6%` net of expected loss; if listed bond expected loss is 1.0% a year, the private deal retains 0.2 pt more after loss plus tighter covenants.
4. Covenant: leverage ≤ 3.5x; ratchet +50 bp if leverage > 3.0x. If leverage goes from 2.8x to 3.2x, coupon rises to 5.5%, compensating the investor before the breach point.
Interpretation: the premium covers the illiquidity and expected loss; the value of covenants is the early lever for renegotiation.

## Anti-patterns
- Buying private debt as a liquid asset or planning to sell before maturity.
- Ranking deals by coupon without comparing covenants, security and ranking.
- Underestimating the origination and monitoring workload.
- Assuming unsecured means no protection; covenants act as protection.
- Putting a large share of a portfolio in a single closed-end fund with capital calls and long lock-up.
- Treating guarantees (Fondo Centrale) as removing all credit risk; limits apply (80%, EUR 2.5 million).

## Practitioner Notes
- Securitization of private debt portfolios might in future improve liquidity, according to the authors.
- A listed private debt instrument still trades little because holders buy and hold.
- Over 90% of Italian issuers used this as their first access to capital markets, and the issue brings reputational benefits and further extraordinary transactions.
- Barrier to entry: sophisticated analysis, tested procedures, and an experienced origination, selection and management team.

## Key Takeaways
1. Private debt = senior bonds, structured/mezzanine bonds and direct lending to non-listed or smaller issuers.
2. The global market grew from about USD 200 bn (2007) to USD 770 bn (2018); Italy's from 2012-13.
3. Terms are flexible: 3-8 years, bullet or amortizing, fixed coupons, mostly unsecured and unrated.
4. Covenants (financial, information, general) and ratchets are core to the risk framework.
5. Yields have been 4-6% for senior Italian issues with low rate correlation.
6. Liquidity is limited, so hold to maturity.
7. Funds are closed-end 6-10 years with a disciplined six-step process; ELTIFs open access to wealthy retail.

# Cheatsheet — CFA Wealth and Institutional Management (L3)

## Decision rules
| When | Do | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Client says "growth" | Quantify and rank goals, then test capital sufficiency | Vague objectives hide conflicts (ch01) |
| Tolerance and capacity differ | Use the lower | Anxious clients abandon the plan at the low (ch01) |
| Taxable client, active strategy | Compare TER and tax alpha, not pre-tax return | Tax drag can equal the fee (ch02) |
| Flat tax, two accounts | Spend taxable first | Sheltered account keeps compounding (ch02) |
| Concentrated, low-basis stock | Test sell-and-diversify against collar plus loan, exchange fund, CRT | Each alternative must beat the tax it avoids (ch02) |
| Gift or bequest | Compute RV | Donor return advantage favors bequest (ch02) |
| Young household | Count human capital as a bond; insure death and disability | Largest asset, no financial cushion (ch03, ch06) |
| Income tied to an industry | Underweight that industry in the portfolio, sell employer stock | Human and financial capital fall together (ch03, ch06) |
| Life cover | Run HLV and needs analysis, choose inside the range | Each rests on different assumptions (ch03, ch06) |
| Cover exceeds both methods | Reduce, unless dependants need lifetime support | Premium saved funds goals (ch06) |
| Buffer above need, pension subsidy unused | Redirect to pension | Tax-free growth and state top-up (ch06) |
| Goals with different horizons | Separate sub-portfolios | Success targets differ (ch06) |
| Worried about outliving assets | Annuitize the floor | Only annuities transfer longevity risk (ch06) |
| Inflation is a stated worry | Indexed annuity, accept about 10% less income | Fixed nominal income erodes (ch06) |
| DB plan | Manage surplus, hedge rate risk when funded | Assets alone mislead (ch04) |
| Endowment or foundation | Compare expected real return with spending rate first | Mix debates come second (ch04) |
| Illiquid exposure rising | Check cumulative liquidity budget under stress | Outflows rise while sources fall (ch05, ch07) |
| Private assets in an optimizer | Unsmooth or use a proxy, cap weights | Smoothed risk inflates the allocation (ch05, ch07) |
| Tactical tilt, unlevered | ETF if cheapest and flexible; recompute if levered | Futures overtake at about 1.16x in the case (ch05) |
| Rebalance small and temporary | Futures | Fast, reversible, managers stay (ch05) |
| Rebalance large and permanent | Cash managers | Tracking cost grows with horizon (ch05) |
| Manager has ties to staff or trustees | Disclose, recuse, verify | Standards I(B), VI(A), IV(A) (ch05) |
| ESG protest, integration policy | Engage and vote, divest last | Exclusion is a suboptimal fallback (ch05) |
| Small direct deal | Still scenario-test and screen reputation | Size does not cap reputational loss (ch07) |

## Decision tree: institutional model fit
1. Needs liabilities hedged first? LDI.
2. Cheap, passive, board-friendly? Norway.
3. Alternatives-heavy with outsourced managers and access? Endowment; with internal team and reference portfolio? Canada.
4. No staff or access? Fall back to Norway-style public portfolio.

## Trade-off matrix
| Choice | Gain | Give up |
|---|---|---|
| Annuity vs lump sum | Longevity cover, no timing risk | Flexibility, bequest; tax uncertainty (ch06) |
| Indexed vs fixed annuity | Inflation protection | About 10% starting income (ch06) |
| Direct vs fund private investing | Fees, exit control | Team, concentration, governance (ch07) |
| Futures vs ETF vs TRS | Cash efficiency | Roll, margin, counterparty (ch05) |
| Yale-type spending rule | Stable budget | Higher effective rate in downturn (ch05) |
| Goals-based vs total-portfolio MVO | Client comprehension | Aggregate efficiency (ch01) |
| Divest vs engage | Clean break | Influence and diversification (ch05) |

## Tells and smells
- Sponsor in love with the product, "it is small" used to dismiss risk.
- Private-asset Sharpe above public equity: smoothing.
- Reported tie "everyone knows" instead of written disclosure.
- Fixed annuity bought while inflation is the stated worry.
- Instant-access buffer growing while pension tax subsidy is unused.
- Insurance unchanged for a decade.
- Hedge advertised as removing risk in a concentrated stock (constructive sale).

## Exam traps
| Trap | Correct |
|---|---|
| Longer or shorter private holdings for premium | Longer illiquidity horizon, higher premium (ch05) |
| Low-liquidity public equity as a substitute | Partial, hard to isolate (ch05) |
| Wide band for cash, tight for PE | Reverse it (ch05) |
| 4x leverage means borrow | 75%; 3x means 66.7% (ch05) |
| Liquidity budget bucket reading | Cumulative minimums (ch05) |
| TRS tracking error | None, but counterparty risk (ch05) |
| Futures tracking error | Higher with multiple legs and longer horizon (ch05) |
| Growth in payments | Adjust the rate, annuity-due mode (ch06) |
| Cover after house purchase | Add debt and family loans to cash needs (ch06) |
| Disability cover | Net pay less state benefit (ch06) |
| DB funded ratio vs discount rate | Higher rate lowers liabilities (ch04) |
| Equity volatility correlation term | Subtracted (ch04) |
| Unsmoothed vs reported volatility | Unsmoothed is higher (ch07) |
| Denominator effect | Include uncalled commitments (ch07) |
| Liquidity risk for small direct deals | Not leading, but reputation is (ch07) |
| Dixon "member firm" claim | Misrepresentation: I(C), VII(A), VII(B) (ch05) |
| Benchmark net of hypothetical fee | Not allowed under GIPS (ch05) |

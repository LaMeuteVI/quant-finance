# Chapter 2: Topics in Private Wealth Management
> **Source**: CFA Program Curriculum 2023, Level III, Reading 22 · Portfolio Management | Paul Bouchey, CFA; Helena Eaton, PhD, CFA; Philip Marcovici (concentrated-position sections draw on Boczar & Pai 2013; estate sections on Horan & Robinson 2009)

## Core Idea
For a taxable client the objective is to maximize after-tax return for a given risk, and taxes are often a bigger drag than fees: a 50% tax on a 9.8% index return cuts $1m over ~30 years from $16m to about $4m. The reading gives three toolkits: (1) measure and manage tax drag (account type, asset location, vehicle, tax lots, loss harvesting), (2) de-risk concentrated single-asset positions without a punitive tax bill (hedge-and-monetize, exchange funds, charitable vehicles, loans), and (3) plan transfers across generations (gifts vs bequests, trusts, foundations, family governance) with specialists.

## Learning Outcomes Covered

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three foundations of investment taxation**: (1) taxation of the components of return (interest, dividends, realized gains, real estate income), (2) tax status of the account (taxable / tax-deferred / tax-exempt), (3) jurisdiction (tax haven / territorial / worldwide, residence or citizenship based).
  - When to use: first screen for any new private client or cross-border holding. Start with the investor's home-country rules, then layer source-country withholding, treaties, estate rules.
- **Taxes on what you earn, own, buy, transfer**: income and gains tax (earn), wealth/property tax (own), stamp duty (buy), gift/estate/inheritance/generation-skipping tax (transfer).
- **Tax-efficiency measurement set**: after-tax holding period return, after-tax post-liquidation return, after-tax excess return and tax alpha, tax-efficiency ratio (formulas below).
  - When to use: compare funds/managers for a taxable account; judge whether alpha covers its taxes (Jeffrey & Arnott: over 85% of active managers lagged an index fund after tax).
- **Asset location**: choose which account holds which asset class. Rule of thumb: tax-efficient assets (equities, tax-managed equities, tax-exempt bonds) in the taxable account; tax-inefficient assets (taxable bonds, high-turnover or high-yield strategies) in tax-deferred or tax-exempt accounts.
  - Failure mode: applied rigidly. Different goals/horizons per account (e.g. house purchase in 3 years from the taxable account) override it; with a long horizon or high-turnover equities, holding equities in the tax-exempt account can win.
- **Tax-aware decumulation**: with a flat tax rate, withdraw from the taxable account first so the tax-advantaged account keeps compounding at the pre-tax rate. In progressive regimes, first fill the lowest brackets with retirement-account withdrawals, then use the taxable account.
- **Tax-aware vs tax-indifferent planning (Exhibit 5)**: after-tax growth assumptions for taxable accounts; after-tax return and volatility in asset allocation; asset location rather than a single allocation across accounts; optimize withdrawals; gift highly appreciated stock rather than cash to charity.
- **Investment vehicle ranking for tax management**: partnership (taxes pass through), mutual fund (co-investors' redemptions can trigger your gains; losses stay inside the fund), ETF (creation/redemption in kind purges low-basis lots), separately managed account (SMA; most flexible, losses usable against any gain of that client).
- **Tax lot accounting**: FIFO (default, usually least efficient in rising markets), LIFO, HIFO (usually most efficient), specified lot (most flexible; must be instructed at trade time). Canada uses average cost.
- **Tax loss harvesting**: realize losses to offset gains/income; a tax deferral, not a permanent saving, because repurchase lowers basis. Wash sale rule (US): repurchase of the same or substantially identical security within 30 days before/after disallows the loss; applies across the taxpayer's accounts (including retirement, sometimes a spouse's). Hold a close substitute (Pepsi for Coca-Cola, sector or index ETF) for 31 days to stay invested.
- **Quantitative tax management**: optimizer minimizes tracking error, maximizes realized losses, minimizes realized gains and trading costs under sector/country/turnover/wash-sale constraints. Three uses: transitions (inherited legacy portfolio), continuous tax-optimized loss harvesting, gain-loss matching.
- **Concentrated position approach set (six)**: (1) sell and diversify, (2) staged diversification (with completion portfolio), (3) hedging and monetization, (4) tax-free exchange (exchange fund, 1031), (5) charitable strategies (CRT, private foundation, donor-advised fund), (6) tax-avoidance/deferral (step-up at death, harvesting losses to pair with staged sales).
  - Selection factors: degree of concentration, volatility/downside of the position, tax basis, liquidity, investor tax rate, horizon, sale restrictions, emotional attachment/control wishes.
- **Equity monetization (two steps)**: (1) hedge most of the risk (short sale, total return swap, options, futures, forward sale; typically a zero-cost collar), (2) borrow against the hedged stock at a high loan-to-value, invest the proceeds in a diversified portfolio. No sale, so no capital gain, if the hedge is not a constructive sale.
- **Estate planning objectives (the Lee/Harper checklist)**: income and liquidity, control over assets, asset protection, tax-aware transfer, preservation of family wealth (governance), business succession, charitable goals.
- **Relative value of a gift vs bequest (RV)**: ratio of the after-tax future values (formulas below). Decides whether to give now or at death.
- **Family governance structure**: family constitution (non-binding, made binding via shareholder agreements/trusts), board of directors (with outsiders), family council, family assembly, family office, family foundation.

## Key Concepts
- **Tax basis / cost basis**: purchase price plus costs; capital gain = net selling price minus basis. Step-up on death resets basis to fair value (US) so heirs owe no tax on the embedded gain.
- **Short-term vs long-term gain**: holding period beyond a statutory minimum earns the lower long-term rate; incentive for long-term investors.
- **Qualified dividend (US)**: lower-rate dividend if stock held at least 61 days (common) / 91 days (preferred) and unhedged; in-the-money or under-30-day calls written against the stock can disqualify it.
- **Withholding tax**: source-country tax on gross interest/dividends/royalties with no expense offset; reducible by treaty and recoverable via a certificate of residence.
- **Tax haven / territorial / worldwide system**: no or very low tax for foreign investors / only local-source income taxed (Hong Kong, Singapore) / all income taxed regardless of source (most OECD countries). The US also taxes citizens worldwide regardless of residence.
- **Common Reporting Standard (CRS)** and **FATCA**: automatic exchange of account data (OECD/G20, 100+ jurisdictions) and US reporting regime (30% withholding on US income for non-compliant institutions).
- **Tax avoidance vs evasion**: legal minimization vs illegal concealment of taxes due; an activity with no merit beyond tax and misleading in form is likely unethical or illegal. Never help disguise ownership.
- **Post-liquidation return**: after-tax return assuming the portfolio is sold at the horizon, deducting tax on embedded gains; the SEC requires funds to report it.
- **Tax-efficiency ratio (TER)**: after-tax annualized return divided by pre-tax annualized return.
- **Tax alpha**: after-tax excess return minus pre-tax excess return; isolates the value of tax management.
- **Potential capital gain exposure (PCGE)**: net unrealized gains (losses) / total net assets of a fund; a high value signals future taxable distributions, a negative one banked losses.
- **Completion portfolio**: index-based portfolio that, combined with the retained concentrated stock, replicates benchmark exposures (underweights the stock's own sector).
- **Constructive sale**: US doctrine treating a hedge that eliminates essentially all risk as a sale; collars are built to keep some risk.
- **Exchange fund**: partnership where holders contribute low-basis stock for a pro rata share of a diversified pool; no taxable event; basis carries over; minimum seven years; 20% in "qualified assets" (usually REITs).
- **Charitable remainder trust (CRT)**: irrevocable gift of shares; donor gets a deduction, trust sells tax-free, pays income to named beneficiaries for life, remainder goes to charity.
- **Donor-advised fund (DAF)**: account used only for the donor's giving; assets (stock, real estate) sold tax-free inside it.
- **Leveraged recapitalization / ESOP / personal loan against shares**: ways for a private owner to take liquidity without a full sale (details below).
- **Forced heirship**: statutory minimum shares for spouse/children (civil law, Shari'a; Spain, France, Switzerland); common law countries (UK, US, Canada) grant freedom of disposition.
- **Estate tax vs inheritance tax**: levied on the estate before distribution vs paid by each beneficiary; plus gift tax and generation-skipping transfer (GST) tax (US: 40% above the lifetime exclusion).
- **Will, probate, intestacy**: will outlines rights over property after death; probate validates it; intestacy means a court applies statutory rules.
- **Trust**: legal relationship (not a legal person) where a trustee holds assets for beneficiaries; revocable (settlor still taxed, creditors can reach) vs irrevocable (more protection); fixed vs discretionary (beneficiaries have no legal right to distributions, so their creditors struggle).
- **Foundation**: civil-law legal entity that holds assets in its own name; the donor keeps more control than with a trust (US private foundations must distribute 5% a year).
- **Controlled foreign corporation (CFC) / personal holding company (PHC)**: offshore company that may defer tax on income until distributed; CFC rules push tax back to the owner's home country.
- **Durable power of attorney / living will**: tools for incapacity, financial/medical decisions without a court guardianship.
- **Shirtsleeves to shirtsleeves**: wealth rarely survives three generations; ~70% of family businesses fail or are sold before the second generation manages them. Causes: dilution, disinterest, lack of education/planning.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
Pre-tax holding period return: `R = (value₁ − value₀ + income) / value₀`.
After-tax holding period return: `R′ = (value₁ − value₀ + income − tax) / value₀ = R − tax/value₀`, with `tax = Σᵢ transactionᵢ · tᵢ` (each realized gain, loss, dividend, interest item at its own marginal rate; realized losses enter as a tax credit, assuming they can be fully used).
Linked after-tax return (n periods): `R′_G = [(1+R′₁)(1+R′₂)…(1+R′ₙ)]^(1/n) − 1`.
Intra-month approximation (modified Dietz): `R′ ≈ R − tax / [value₀ + Σⱼ Cⱼ·(N−j)/N]`, N = days in month, j = day of flow.
Post-liquidation return: `R_PL = [(1+R′₁)…(1+R′ₙ) − liquidation tax/final value]^(1/n) − 1`; `liquidation tax = (final value − tax basis) × capital gains rate` (for embedded gain g as % of ending value: deduct `g × t_cg`).
After-tax excess return: `x′ = R′ − B′`; pre-tax `x = R − B`; tax alpha `α_tax = x′ − x`.
Tax-efficiency ratio: `TER = R′ / R` (breaks down for negative returns: −12% after tax vs −10% pre-tax gives a nonsense 120%; use tax alpha or plot R′ vs R).
Accumulation by account type (flat rate t, return R, n years):

| Account | Future value of 1 (after tax) |
|---|---|
| Tax-exempt | `(1+R)ⁿ` |
| Taxable (taxes paid annually) | `[1 + R(1−t)]ⁿ` |
| Tax-deferred (lump-sum liquidation) | `(1+R)ⁿ · (1−t)` |

PCGE: `PCGE = net gains (losses) / total net assets`.
Relative value of a tax-free gift vs bequest:
`RV_taxfree gift = FV_gift / FV_bequest = [1 + r_g(1−t_g)]ⁿ / { [1 + r_e(1−t_e)]ⁿ · (1−T_e) }`
(r_g, t_g = return and tax rate on returns in the recipient's hands; r_e, t_e = same for the donor's estate; n = years to the donor's death; T_e = estate/inheritance tax rate). If returns and tax rates are equal this reduces to `1/(1−T_e)`: with T_e = 40%, 1.67x.
Taxable gift (tax T_g paid by recipient): `RV_taxable gift = [1 + r_g(1−t_g)]ⁿ (1−T_g) / { [1 + r_e(1−t_e)]ⁿ (1−T_e) }`.
Progressive estate tax: sum tax bracket by bracket. UK-style: `IHT = (estate − nil-rate band) × rate` (e.g. (700,000 − 325,000) × 40% = 150,000).
Forced heirship: reserved shares are computed on the total estate; only the remainder is freely disposable (spouse 1/3, children 1/3 of €800k leaves €266,666 free).
Cross-border after-tax return (Swiss/Hong Kong example): after-tax return = (gross income − withholding on dividends/interest) / capital; gains often exempt at source.

Tax characteristics of vehicles:

| Vehicle | Key tax trait |
|---|---|
| Partnership (hedge/PE fund) | Pass-through to partners; distributions often capital gains |
| Mutual fund | Income and net realized gains distributed; co-investor redemptions can create your tax; losses not passed through; new buyers inherit embedded gains |
| ETF | In-kind creation/redemption can eliminate gains; low-basis lots delivered out |
| SMA | One owner; losses offset gains anywhere in the client's accounts |

Tax status by holder: individual brokerage and personal trust taxable; IRA tax-deferred; Roth IRA, charitable trust, foundation, pension, endowment, sovereign wealth fund tax-exempt; Australian superannuation taxed at a discounted rate.

## Mental Models
- Treat taxes as the third friction beside fees and trading costs; ask "is my alpha big enough to cover its taxes?" before putting an active or high-turnover strategy in a taxable account.
- Momentum is tax-friendly despite turnover (lets winners run, realizes losers); value and small-cap strategies selling at targets realize gains more often; style-box manager mosaics force gain realization on style drift and rebalancing.
- Tax-exempt compounding is the scarce resource: spend taxable dollars first, give the tax-advantaged account the highest-expected-return assets, and keep appreciation outside the estate.
- A hedge is not a sale unless it removes all risk: design collars with residual risk (strikes apart) to avoid constructive-sale treatment.
- Sell-and-diversify is usually the baseline; every alternative (collar plus loan, exchange fund, CRT) must beat paying the tax, accounting for fees, lock-up, loss of control and complexity.
- Estate plans get stronger when one structure meets many non-tax needs (succession, creditor and divorce protection, political risk); a tax-only motive invites general anti-avoidance rules.
- Governance is behavioral risk management: outside directors counter founder overconfidence; diverse councils counter social proof; business exit triggers endowment bias.

## Worked Example
**1. Account-type accumulation (recomputed).** ¥1,000,000, 10% return, flat 20% tax, 20 years.
- Taxable: `1,000,000 × [1 + 0.10×0.80]²⁰ = 1.08²⁰` = **¥4,660,957**.
- Tax-deferred: `1.10²⁰ × 0.80` = 6.7275 × 0.8 = **¥5,382,000**.
- Tax-exempt: `1.10²⁰` = **¥6,727,500**.
- Interpretation: ranking tax-exempt > tax-deferred > taxable; deferral is worth ¥721k over annual taxation here, and exempt beats deferred by exactly the 20% terminal tax.

**2. Relative value of gift vs bequest (Zachary).** Donor taxed 48% on returns, recipient 20%, 6% pre-tax return, 20 years, bequest taxed 60%, taxable gift taxed 30% (60% less 50% relief).
- Tax-free gift: numerator `[1+0.06×0.80]²⁰ = 1.048²⁰` = 2.554; denominator `[1+0.06×0.52]²⁰ × 0.40 = 1.0312²⁰ × 0.40` = 1.8488 × 0.40 = 0.7395; `RV = 3.45`.
- Taxable gift: numerator 2.554 × 0.70 = 1.7878; `RV = 2.42`.
- Annual exclusion: €20,000 grows to €20,000 × 2.554 = **€51,081** in real terms with no transfer tax.
- Contrast case (Enlow nephew): gift return 1% vs donor 6%, gift tax 33% vs inheritance tax 39%: `RV = 0.83`, so the bequest wins because the donor's return advantage outweighs the lower recipient tax rate. Niece (9% return, high 52% tax, no gift tax within allowance vs 47% inheritance tax): `RV = 1.99`, gift wins.

**3. Tax-aware decumulation.** Two accounts of $1m, 10% pre-tax, 25% tax drag on the taxable account (7.5% after tax), $200k withdrawn each year-end. Taxable first: after 10 years **$1,800,752** left. Tax-exempt first: **$1,480,097**. The $320k gap comes from keeping the 10% compounder invested longer.

**4. Exchange fund vs sell (Stark).** $1m Exxon, basis $100k, 25% tax, 10% return, 7 years. Sell: pay $225k, reinvest $775k, grows to $1,510,256, tax on $735,256 gain = $183,814, final **$1,326,442**. Exchange fund: $1,948,717, tax on $1,848,717 gain = $462,179, final **$1,486,538**: deferral is worth ~$160k before fund fees and the lock-up.

## Anti-patterns
- **Launching a high-turnover or tactical strategy for a taxable client without after-tax backtests**: realized gains can exceed the excess return.
- **Reading a fund's pre-tax rank only**: top-decile funds differ widely in TER (e.g. 85% vs 40% for taxable bond funds); check TER, post-liquidation return, PCGE.
- **Buying a mutual fund late in the year or with high PCGE**: you inherit embedded gains you did not earn (JEMStone example: Mateo gets $83,333 of gain with a flat investment).
- **Accepting custodian default FIFO**: nullifies tax-aware trades; instruct the specified lot or HIFO.
- **Harvesting losses and then repurchasing inside 30 days (US)**, or into a retirement account: loss disallowed; and ignoring that harvesting lowers basis.
- **Selling a call deep in the money to "lock in" a gain**: premium is taxed short term while a sale would be long term.
- **Equity hedge that removes all risk**: triggers constructive sale.
- **Holding a concentrated position out of loyalty to the tax basis** ("don't want to pay the tax"): the risk may outweigh the tax; but also don't sell blindly without testing alternatives.
- **Owning US stocks directly from a non-treaty jurisdiction** without considering US estate tax and 30% dividend withholding.
- **Helping hide ownership or using shell loss schemes** (a Seattle CEO and attorney got six years for fabricated tax losses): evasion, violation of Standards I and III.
- **Structure with only tax motive**: exposed to general anti-avoidance rules.
- **Skipping annual gift exclusions**: unused exclusions expire and the compounding is lost.
- **Naming a conflicted family member trustee or relying on a will alone for an at-risk heir** (Frank in the Astrid case): use a trust and a durable power of attorney.

## Exam Focus & Traps
- Account formulas: taxable uses `(1+R(1−t))ⁿ`; deferred uses `(1+R)ⁿ(1−t)`; exempt uses `(1+R)ⁿ`. Do not compound taxable growth at the pre-tax rate.
- Post-liquidation: compound the **after-tax** returns first, then subtract `embedded gain % × tax rate` from the ending value (1.1882 − 0.01 = 1.1782; ^(1/3) − 1 = 5.62%), then annualize.
- PCGE sign: negative PCGE (net embedded losses) means more tax-efficient going forward. Compute net gains as unrealized gains less losses (distributed gains already left the fund).
- Step-up in basis: basis = value at death, so selling at $180k vs $200k step-up gives a loss and a negative tax of −$4,000 at 20%.
- Worldwide-taxing citizenship: a US citizen resident in Singapore (territorial) is still taxed on all income worldwide. Territory-based Singapore taxes only local-source income.
- Personal loan against private shares: no immediate tax **only without a put back to the company**; with a put it is likely a taxable event. Leveraged recapitalization is taxable (partial) and dilutes control. ESOP can defer gains but loses control. Mortgage on property, especially non-recourse, monetizes without tax and keeps sole ownership; DAF/CRT/sale-leaseback give up ownership or trigger tax.
- Which tax lot: HIFO is usually best, but a short-term loss is worth more than a long-term loss, so a specified lot can beat HIFO (Vodafone example lot C). Check long/short status dates (holding period over six months in the problem).
- Gift vs bequest: higher expected return in the donor's hands favors the bequest (nephew); a tax-free allowance, a higher recipient return and a long horizon favor the gift. Gift tax is "tax-exclusive" (paid by recipient in the model); in the UK a gift is clawed back if the donor dies within seven years.
- Collar payoffs: with 15,000 shares at $70, sold $80 call at $5, bought $70 put: maximum profit $150,000 (10 × 15,000), loss floor zero (premiums offset). Covered call alone: max $225,000 (premium $75,000 + $150,000 appreciation) but no downside protection.
- Estate vs inheritance tax: estate tax paid from the estate; inheritance tax paid by the beneficiary. Forced heirship leaves only the disposable share free (cannot bequeath €300k when free portion is €266,666).
- Trust asset protection: irrevocable protects from settlor's creditors; discretionary protects from beneficiaries' creditors; revocable offers neither and keeps the settlor taxed.

## Key Takeaways
1. Compound taxable accounts at after-tax returns and judge strategies by TER, post-liquidation return, and tax alpha, never by pre-tax return alone.
2. Locate assets deliberately: tax-managed equity and tax-exempt bonds in taxable accounts, taxable bonds and high-turnover strategies in sheltered accounts, subject to goals and horizon.
3. In decumulation, spend taxable money first at a flat rate; use low brackets of the retirement account first under progressive taxes.
4. Use SMAs and ETFs for tax control, specify tax lots, harvest losses year-round with substitutes and an optimizer, and remember harvesting only defers tax.
5. For concentrated positions list options by tax and control outcome: sell, stage with a completion portfolio, collar plus loan, exchange fund, CRT/DAF, loan/mortgage; pick by position size, basis, restrictions and emotional attachment.
6. Know the jurisdiction map (haven, territorial, worldwide, citizenship), withholding and treaties, CRS/FATCA; start from the home-country rules and never assist evasion.
7. Gift early when the recipient's after-tax growth or lower tax rate beats the estate's; otherwise bequeath; always compute RV.
8. Match trust, foundation, insurance and company tools to control, protection and tax goals; build family governance and plan for divorce and incapacity before they happen.

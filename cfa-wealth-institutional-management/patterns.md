# Patterns — Wealth and Institutional Management Recipes

## Private Client Intake to IPS
**When to use**: a new private client or a major life event.
**How**: 1. Collect personal, financial, tax and other information; 2. quantify and rank goals (planned vs unplanned); 3. take risk tolerance as the lower of willingness and capacity, goal by goal; 4. test capital sufficiency (Monte Carlo, 75%-90% success, inspect shortfall size); 5. write the IPS with allocation bands, discretion, rebalancing and review; 6. report and review annually.
**Trade-offs**: questionnaires are framing-sensitive; goals-based aggregate may sit off the frontier. (ch01)

## After-Tax Return and Manager Screen
**When to use**: comparing funds or managers for a taxable account.
**How**: 1. Compute after-tax return with each item at its own rate; 2. TER = after-tax / pre-tax (not for negative returns); 3. post-liquidation return by deducting embedded-gain tax; 4. tax alpha = after-tax minus pre-tax excess return; 5. check PCGE before buying a fund.
**Trade-offs**: needs realistic tax assumptions and usable losses. (ch02)

## Asset Location and Decumulation
**When to use**: client holds taxable, deferred and exempt accounts.
**How**: 1. Taxable: tax-managed equity, tax-exempt bonds; sheltered: taxable bonds, high turnover; 2. compound taxable at `(1+R(1−t))^n`, deferred at `(1+R)^n(1−t)`; 3. withdraw taxable first at a flat rate; under progressive tax fill low brackets with retirement withdrawals first.
**Trade-offs**: short-horizon goals and embedded gains override the rule. (ch02)

## Concentrated Position Decision
**When to use**: one holding dominates wealth.
**How**: 1. Rank six routes (sell, staged sale with completion portfolio, collar plus loan, exchange fund, charitable, deferral or step-up); 2. screen by concentration, basis, liquidity, restrictions, tax rate, attachment; 3. beat the sell-and-diversify baseline net of fees and lock-up; 4. avoid a hedge that removes all risk (constructive sale).
**Trade-offs**: caps upside, counterparty and leverage risk, loss of control. (ch02)

## Gift versus Bequest
**When to use**: transfer planning with a long horizon.
**How**: compute `RV = [1+r_g(1−t_g)]^n / ([1+r_e(1−t_e)]^n(1−T_e))`; use annual exclusions every year; gift when RV > 1.
**Trade-offs**: loss of control; look-back rules; a recipient with a lower return favors bequest. (ch02)

## Economic Balance Sheet and Insurance Gap Check
**When to use**: any household review, especially young or pension-rich clients.
**How**: 1. Value human capital (survival-weighted, risk-adjusted discount); add pension PVs; list debts, PV of consumption and bequests; 2. identify earnings, death, longevity, property, liability risks; 3. size disability cover as PV (annuity due, growth-adjusted rate) of net pay minus state benefit; 4. size life cover by human life value and by needs analysis, treat the gap as a range; 5. retain what the buffer can absorb; 6. re-run at each trigger.
**Trade-offs**: all inputs are assumptions; HC is illiquid. (ch03, ch06)

## Correlation-with-Human-Capital Fix
**When to use**: the client's portfolio loads on the employer or industry.
**How**: 1. Map income, local property and portfolio to one factor; 2. stop adding to the correlated holdings; 3. route new savings to diversified low-cost pooled funds; 4. reject leveraged local real estate; 5. use pension wrappers for tax subsidy.
**Trade-offs**: realizing gains may trigger tax, so switch gradually. (ch06)

## Goals-Based Sub-Portfolio Carve-Out
**When to use**: goals differ in horizon and required probability of success.
**How**: 1. Size each goal as a PV (deferred annuity for a future liability); 2. near-certain goal: hedge with inflation-linked bonds, phase in with a tax allowance; 3. flexible goal: growth assets; 4. check retirement income funds the base lifestyle; 5. review annually.
**Trade-offs**: aggregate efficiency lost; long duration cannot be matched. (ch01, ch06)

## Retirement Decumulation Mix
**When to use**: DC pots at retirement.
**How**: 1. Cover the expense need with state pension plus indexed annuity; 2. take tax-free lump sums where allowed; 3. leave the rest invested for bequest and flexibility; 4. portfolio sized to the client's drawdown limit; 5. add expert checks for relocation (tax, estate, currency).
**Trade-offs**: indexed annuity pays about 10% less starting income; annuity adds counterparty risk. (ch06)

## Institutional IPS Build
**When to use**: any institution.
**How**: 1. Stakeholders, liabilities, horizon, liquidity, constraints; 2. return objective against the liability measure (discount rate, spending rate, funding cost); 3. risk measure matched to type; 4. choose operating model by capability (Norway, Endowment, Canada, LDI); 5. liquidity band and rebalancing policy; 6. annual review, appendix for allocation.
**Trade-offs**: copying peers ignores own liabilities. (ch04)

## Liquidity Budget and Stress Check
**When to use**: raising illiquid exposure or setting policy.
**How**: 1. Define cumulative time-to-cash buckets; 2. profile vehicle by vehicle; 3. aggregate; 4. stress calls, gates, smoothing and higher distributions; 5. confirm every minimum (and the illiquid cap) holds; 6. set triggers on invested plus uncalled commitments.
**Trade-offs**: bucket assumptions are judgment; margins can be thin. (ch05, ch07)

## Increase Illiquid Allocation
**When to use**: peer gap, lower return outlook, long horizon.
**How**: 1. Unsmooth private inputs, net of fees; 2. optimize with caps; 3. Monte Carlo on purchasing power; 4. verify volatility inside tolerance and liquidity budget under stress; 5. phase in over 2-3 years by commitment pacing; 6. diversify by manager and vintage.
**Trade-offs**: higher volatility and fees, rebalancing cost. (ch05, ch07)

## Overlay Vehicle Choice
**When to use**: tactical tilt or exposure change.
**How**: 1. Sum costs in bp: commission, spread, impact, fee, mispricing, roll, funding; 2. add leverage term (borrowed share × Libor + spread for ETF; × Libor for futures and TRS); 3. compare; 4. check cash use, speed, operations (margin, roll), counterparty risk; 5. recompute if leverage changes.
**Trade-offs**: ignoring return on freed cash overstates cost of futures. (ch05)

## Cash vs Derivatives Rebalance
**When to use**: a class breaches its corridor.
**How**: 1. Choose target or edge; 2. cost both routes: spread, impact, tracking or mispricing (rises with horizon), cash drag (rises with size), roll; 3. small, temporary, fast: futures; large, permanent: cash managers; 4. illiquids: pace commitments.
**Trade-offs**: two futures legs add tracking error. (ch05)

## Conflicted Manager Search
**When to use**: a candidate has ties to the owner, trustees or staff.
**How**: 1. Disclose all ties in writing; 2. recuse conflicted people; 3. verify returns against third-party data and GIPS claims; 4. check for use of confidential information; 5. document the decision.
**Trade-offs**: slower hiring; may lose a strong manager. (ch05)

## ESG Controversy Response
**When to use**: stakeholders demand divestment.
**How**: 1. Check ESG monitoring and manager diligence; 2. engage the manager and the company (collaborative engagement, votes); 3. reply with the policy; 4. separate operational decisions; 5. set a review date; divest only if engagement fails.
**Trade-offs**: reputational pressure while engaging. (ch05)

## Unsmoothing and Proxy Risk
**When to use**: private or hedge-fund returns feed an allocation model.
**How**: 1. Estimate first-order autocorrelation; 2. `r* = (r_t − ρr_{t−1})/(1−ρ)`; 3. or use a public proxy; 4. rebuild covariance; 5. still cap weights.
**Trade-offs**: short samples give unreliable ρ. (ch07)

## Five-Step Liquidity Plan
**When to use**: asset owner with illiquids.
**How**: 1. Set parameters and bands; 2. assess liquidity from legal terms; 3. model flows; 4. stress; 5. emergency order of sales, agreed with the board.
**Trade-offs**: stress design is an art. (ch07)

## Direct Deal Risk Review
**When to use**: a long-term direct investment.
**How**: 1. Scale and liquidity; 2. key driver risks with low/base/high cases; 3. currency, political, climate, social and reputational checks; 4. conditional action plan and hedges; 5. monitor after investing.
**Trade-offs**: more work for small tickets. (ch07)

## Distressed Minority Position
**When to use**: a direct minority stake goes wrong.
**How**: 1. Engage management and change incentives; 2. lobby government on binding rules; 3. offer retraining or reduced pay in transition; 4. set a deadline (one year); 5. exit by sale to a strategic buyer or a specialist team.
**Trade-offs**: reputation risk persists until exit. (ch07)

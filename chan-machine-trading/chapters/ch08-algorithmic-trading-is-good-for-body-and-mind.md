# Chapter 8: Algorithmic Trading Is Good for Body and Mind

> Chan, *Machine Trading*, Chapter 8. The human side of trading.

## Core Idea

The closing chapter argues that **algorithmic trading** is *better* than discretionary trading for the body and the mind: (1) **no emotional bias** (the algorithm does not panic, euphoria, or revenge-trade), (2) **consistent execution** (the algorithm does not skip a signal), (3) **no screen time** (the algorithm runs unattended), (4) **diversification** (the algorithm can run many strategies), (5) **backtestability** (the algorithm is verifiable, the discretionary trader is not).

## Key Concepts

- **Emotional bias** — fear, greed, regret; the discretionary trader's enemy.
- **Discretionary vs systematic** — the contrast; systematic is verifiable.
- **Consistency** — the algorithm does not skip a signal.
- **Diversification** — running many strategies; reduces the dependence on a single idea.
- **Backtestability** — every signal is testable; discretionary is not.
- **The algo as a co-pilot** — using the algo to *augment* discretion, not replace it.
- **The algo as a teacher** — running the algo *informally* to learn what works.
- **Risk management** — the most important function; the algo enforces it.
- **The discipline of stop-loss** — the algo enforces the stop-loss, the human may not.

## Key Takeaways

1. Systematic trading is verifiable; discretionary is not.
2. The algo enforces the stop-loss and the position size.
3. Diversification across strategies is the institutional defense.

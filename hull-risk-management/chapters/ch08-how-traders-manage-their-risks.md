# Chapter 8: How Traders Manage Their Risks

> Hull 4e, pp. 153–173. The day-to-day risk management of a trading desk.

## Core Idea

A trading desk manages risk through: (1) **delta hedging** for option books; (2) **limits** on position sizes, Greeks, and VaR; (3) **stop-loss** orders to limit drawdowns; (4) **diversification** across asset classes, geographies, and strategies; (5) **liquidity management** to ensure the desk can unwind positions without affecting the market. The **chief risk officer (CRO)** is responsible for the firm-wide risk framework.

## Key Concepts

- **Delta hedging** — the dynamic rebalancing of the option's delta to zero.
- **Gamma scalping** — profiting from the realised volatility being different from the implied.
- **Vega hedging** — managing the volatility exposure of an option portfolio.
- **Stop-loss** — an automatic exit at a specified loss.
- **Risk limit** — a hard cap on a specific risk metric (position size, Greek, VaR, drawdown).
- **Diversification** — the principle that uncorrelated risks reduce the aggregate.
- **Drawdown** — the peak-to-trough loss; the most painful risk metric for an investor.
- **Chief Risk Officer (CRO)** — the firm-level risk manager.

## Key Takeaways

1. Delta hedging is the basic option-book discipline.
2. Risk limits are the institutional control; the CRO enforces them.
3. Drawdown is the most painful risk metric.

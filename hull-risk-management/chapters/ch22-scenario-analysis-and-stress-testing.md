# Chapter 22: Scenario Analysis and Stress Testing

> Hull 4e, pp. 463–479. The art of imagining bad futures.

## Core Idea

**Scenario analysis** is the discipline of testing a portfolio under hypothetical (often extreme) conditions. **Stress testing** is scenario analysis with *extreme* scenarios. **Reverse stress testing** finds the scenario that would cause a given loss. The institutional standard is to combine (1) **historical scenarios** (1987 crash, 2008 crisis, COVID 2020), (2) **hypothetical scenarios** (e.g. a 100bp parallel shift, a 30% equity crash), and (3) **reverse stress tests** (the scenario that breaks the bank).

## Key Concepts

- **Scenario analysis** — testing a portfolio under hypothetical conditions.
- **Stress testing** — scenario analysis with extreme scenarios.
- **Historical scenario** — replay a past event.
- **Hypothetical scenario** — a constructed scenario.
- **Reverse stress test** — find the scenario that causes a given loss.
- **Sensitivity analysis** — the first-order derivative; how much does the portfolio lose for a 1bp move?
- **Worst-case scenario** — the scenario that produces the largest loss.
- **Top-down vs bottom-up** — top-down: firm-wide; bottom-up: per desk.
- **Regulatory stress tests** — CCAR (US Fed), EBA (EU), BoE (UK).
- **CCAR/DFAST** — Comprehensive Capital Analysis and Review; the Fed's annual stress test.
- **EBA stress test** — the EU's biennial stress test.

## Key Takeaways

1. Scenario analysis must combine historical, hypothetical, and reverse stress tests.
2. The institutional standards are CCAR (US), EBA (EU), BoE (UK).
3. Reverse stress testing is required by most regulators.

# Chapter 2: Banks

> Hull 4e, pp. 25–43. The institutional structure: how banks earn money, the balance sheet, regulatory constraints.

## Core Idea

A bank is a **maturity transformer**: it takes short-term deposits and makes long-term loans. The transformation generates the **net interest margin (NIM)** — the difference between the interest received on assets and the interest paid on liabilities. Banks also earn **non-interest income** (fees, trading income, FX). The bank's **balance sheet** is the key instrument: assets (loans, securities, trading book) are funded by liabilities (deposits, wholesale funding, equity). The **Basel III capital requirements** (CET1, AT1, Tier 2) constrain the size and risk of the asset book.

## Key Concepts

- **Net interest margin (NIM)** — the average spread between asset yields and liability costs.
- **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)** — HQLA / net cash outflows over 30 days, ≥ 100%.
- **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)** — available stable funding / required stable funding, ≥ 100%.
- **Leverage ratio** — Tier 1 capital / exposure, ≥ 3% (Basel III).
- **Capital conservation buffer** — 2.5% CET1 above the minimum.
- **G-SIB surcharge** — 1% to 3.5% CET1 for global systemically important banks.

## Key Takeaways

1. Banks are maturity transformers; the NIM is the primary income source.
2. Basel III liquidity and capital constraints are binding.
3. The trading book is the source of market risk (ch05, ch12, ch17).

# Chapter 4: Mutual Funds and Hedge Funds

> Hull 4e, pp. 71–91. The other asset managers: structure, fees, alignment of interests.

## Core Idea

**Mutual funds** are open-end, daily-redeemable, regulated (UCITS in EU, 40 Act in US), with limited leverage and shorting. **Hedge funds** are private partnerships, with lockups, performance fees (typically 1.5% management + 20% performance), broad mandate (long/short, leverage, derivatives), and limited regulation. The **2-and-20 fee structure** aligns (somewhat) the manager's incentives with the investor's; the **high-water mark** and **hurdle rate** prevent the manager from earning performance fees on prior losses.

## Key Concepts

- **Open-end vs closed-end** — daily redemption vs fixed-share.
- **UCITS** — EU directive on retail funds; restricts leverage, shorting, derivatives.
- **AIFMD** — EU directive on alternative investment fund managers; broader mandate.
- **2-and-20** — the standard hedge fund fee structure.
- **High-water mark** — the manager earns performance fees only on returns above the previous peak.
- **Hurdle rate** — the minimum return before the performance fee applies.
- **Side letter** — a separate agreement with a specific investor (e.g. lower fees).
- **Lockup period** — the time before the investor can redeem.

## Key Takeaways

1. Mutual funds are liquid, regulated, cheap; hedge funds are illiquid, lightly regulated, expensive.
2. The 2-and-20 structure is the standard for hedge funds; high-water mark and hurdle rate are critical.
3. UCITS restricts the strategy universe (no leverage, limited shorting); AIFMD allows more.

# Chapter 7: Valuation and Scenario Analysis: The Risk-Neutral and Real Worlds

> Hull 4e, pp. 137–151. The two worlds of finance: pricing vs real.

## Core Idea

The **risk-neutral world** (or "Q-measure") is the world used for pricing derivatives: expected returns are the risk-free rate, and prices are discounted at the risk-free rate. The **real world** (or "P-measure") is the actual world: expected returns include risk premia, and the discount rate is higher. The risk-neutral world is *mathematically convenient*; the real world is *operationally relevant*. **Scenario analysis** must use the real world, not the risk-neutral world.

## Key Concepts

- **Risk-neutral world (Q-measure)** — pricing measure; expected return = risk-free rate.
- **Real world (P-measure)** — actual measure; expected return = risk-free + risk premium.
- **Risk-neutral probability** — the implied probability from market prices.
- **State price / Arrow–Debreu security** — a security that pays $1 in one state and 0 in all others.
- **Pricing kernel** — the stochastic discount factor; the foundation of asset pricing.
- **Scenario analysis** — testing a portfolio under a hypothetical set of conditions.
- **Stress testing** — scenario analysis with extreme scenarios.
- **Reverse stress test** — finding the scenario that would cause a given loss.

## Key Takeaways

1. Pricing is in the risk-neutral world; scenario analysis is in the real world.
2. The risk premium is the difference between the two.
3. Reverse stress testing is required by some regulators.

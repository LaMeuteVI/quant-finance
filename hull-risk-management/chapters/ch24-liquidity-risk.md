# Chapter 24: Liquidity Risk

> Hull 4e, pp. 501–525. Funding liquidity and market liquidity; the 2008 case study.

## Core Idea

**Liquidity risk** has two dimensions: **funding liquidity** (the ability to meet obligations as they come due) and **market liquidity** (the ability to unwind a position without affecting the market). The Basel III **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)** requires HQLA ≥ 100% of net cash outflows over 30 days. The **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)** requires available stable funding ≥ 100% of required stable funding. The 2008 crisis showed that **liquidity risk can be the binding constraint**, even for institutions that are well-capitalised.

## Key Concepts

- **Funding liquidity** — ability to meet obligations.
- **Market liquidity** — ability to unwind a position.
- **LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio)** — HQLA / net cash outflows over 30 days, ≥ 100%.
- **NSFR (Net Stable Funding Ratio)** — available stable funding / required stable funding, ≥ 100%.
- **HQLA (High-Quality Liquid Assets)** — Level 1 (cash, government bonds), Level 2A (covered bonds, agency MBS), Level 2B (lower-rated).
- **Haircut** — the discount applied to HQLA Level 2.
- **Intraday liquidity** — the liquidity needed for payment systems; a separate regulatory focus.
- **Repo haircut** — the haircut on a repo; the funding cost of a position.
- **Central bank facilities** — the lender-of-last-resort facilities (Fed discount window, ECB MRO, BoE DWF).
- **Fire-sale price** — the price at which a forced seller must accept; the empirical measure of market liquidity.
- **Bid-ask spread** — a measure of market liquidity; wide in stress.
- **Kyle's lambda** — the price impact of a trade; a measure of market liquidity.

## Formulas & Tables

- **LCR**: `HQLA / (net cash outflows over 30 days) ≥ 100%`.
- **NSFR**: `ASF / RSF ≥ 100%`, with ASF and RSF defined by Basel III.
- **Kyle's lambda**: `λ = ΔP / Q`, the price impact per share traded.

## Key Takeaways

1. Liquidity risk has two dimensions: funding and market.
2. LCR and NSFR are the Basel III binding standards.
3. The 2008 crisis showed that liquidity can be the binding constraint.

# Chapter 6: The Credit Crisis of 2007

> Hull 4e, pp. 121–135. The case study that motivated Basel III and the FRTB.

## Core Idea

The 2007 credit crisis was triggered by the collapse of the US subprime mortgage market and the **structured products** built on it (CDOs, CDO-squareds, ABX). The crisis revealed: (1) the **Gaussian copula's failure** for tranche pricing in stress; (2) the **pro-cyclicality** of the Basel II capital framework; (3) the **liquidity risk** in apparently liquid markets; (4) the **wrong-way risk** of bank exposures to monoline insurers. The policy response was Basel III (capital, liquidity, leverage) and the FRTB (market risk).

## Key Concepts

- **Subprime mortgage** — a mortgage to a borrower with low credit quality.
- **CDO (Collateralised Debt Obligation)** — a structured product that tranches a pool of debt.
- **ABX index** — a credit default swap index on subprime MBS; the "canary" of the crisis.
- **Gaussian copula** — the model used to price CDO tranches; collapsed in stress.
- **Monoline insurer** — a financial guarantor; insured AAA tranches.
- **Wrong-way risk** — exposure rises as the counterparty's credit deteriorates.
- **Repo run** — the failure of the repo market in September 2008.
- **Lehman Brothers** — the investment bank that failed on 15 September 2008.

## Key Takeaways

1. The Gaussian copula's failure is the canonical case study of model risk.
2. Liquidity risk is binding even in liquid markets.
3. Wrong-way risk can turn a small exposure into a large loss.

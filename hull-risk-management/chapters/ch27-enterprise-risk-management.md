# Chapter 27: Enterprise Risk Management

> Hull 4e, pp. 565–577. The firm-wide risk framework.

## Core Idea

**Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)** is the firm-wide framework for managing all risks: market, credit, operational, liquidity, model, business, and strategic. The **CRO** is responsible for the ERM framework; the **board of directors' risk committee** sets the risk appetite. The **risk appetite statement (RAS)** defines the firm's tolerance for risk; the **risk budget** translates the RAS into limits for each business line. The **risk culture** is the most important factor: a culture that rewards risk-taking without regard to the downside is the most common cause of failure.

## Key Concepts

- **ERM (Enterprise Risk Management)** — the firm-wide framework.
- **CRO (Chief Risk Officer)** — the firm-level risk manager.
- **Board risk committee** — sets the risk appetite; oversees the CRO.
- **Risk appetite** — the firm's tolerance for risk.
- **Risk appetite statement (RAS)** — the formal document defining the risk appetite.
- **Risk budget** — the translation of the RAS into per-business-line limits.
- **Risk culture** — the firm's attitude towards risk; the most important factor.
- **Three lines of defence** — business, risk management, internal audit.
- **COSO ERM framework** — the standard for ERM.
- **ISO 31000** — the international standard for risk management.

## Key Takeaways

1. ERM is the firm-wide framework; the CRO is responsible.
2. The risk appetite statement and risk budget translate strategy into risk limits.
3. Risk culture is the most important factor; the financial crises of 2008 and 2020 had clear cultural failures.

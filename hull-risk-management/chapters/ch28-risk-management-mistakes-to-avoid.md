# Chapter 28: Risk Management Mistakes to Avoid

> Hull 4e, pp. 579–584. The institutional lessons from the 2008 crisis and other blowups.

## Core Idea

The chapter enumerates the most common risk management mistakes. The 2008 crisis is the canonical case study; the lessons apply to every institution. The list (paraphrased):

1. **Treating VaR as the only risk measure.** VaR is not coherent; ES is the modern replacement.
2. **Ignoring liquidity risk.** The 2008 crisis showed that liquidity can be the binding constraint.
3. **Trusting the Gaussian copula.** The copula's failure was the root cause of the 2008 CDO losses.
4. **Trusting the rating agencies.** The AAA ratings on CDO tranches were wrong; the agencies were not independent.
5. **Ignoring model risk.** Every model is wrong somewhere; the institutional response is independent validation.
6. **Incentivising risk-taking without regard to the downside.** Compensation that rewards upside without penalising downside is the most common cause of blowups.
7. **Inadequate stress testing.** Scenario analysis must include extreme scenarios, not just historical ones.
8. **Concentration risk.** A concentrated portfolio (e.g. subprime mortgages) is fragile; diversification is the institutional defence.
9. **Pro-cyclicality.** The Basel II capital framework was pro-cyclical; capital requirements rose in stress, just when capital was needed.
10. **Groupthink.** A culture that discourages dissent is dangerous; the dissenting voice is often correct.

## Key Takeaways

1. VaR is not the only risk measure; ES is the modern replacement.
2. Liquidity risk can be the binding constraint; ignore it at peril.
3. The Gaussian copula is wrong; use Student-t or a richer model.
4. Compensation matters: align incentives with the firm's risk appetite.

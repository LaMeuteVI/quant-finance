# Chapter 3: Insurance Companies and Pension Plans

> Hull 4e, pp. 45–69. The other side of the maturity transformation: long-dated liabilities.

## Core Idea

Insurance and pension plans have **long-dated liabilities** (annuities, claims that may arise decades later) funded by long-dated assets. The risk is the **mismatch** between assets and liabilities: if asset returns fall or liability values rise (because interest rates fall), the institution becomes under-funded. **ALM (Asset-Liability Management)** is the discipline of matching.

## Key Concepts

- **Liability-driven investment (LDI)** — investing to match the duration and convexity of liabilities.
- **Immunisation** — duration matching to immunise against small rate changes.
- **Pension surplus** — assets minus liabilities (mark-to-market); the health of the plan.
- **Withdrawal risk (longevity)** — annuitants living longer than expected.
- **Mortality table** — the actuarial table; the input to the liability valuation.

## Key Takeaways

1. Insurance and pension plans are the "long liability" side of the maturity transformation.
2. LDI is the discipline of matching asset and liability cash flows.
3. Longevity is the primary actuarial risk.

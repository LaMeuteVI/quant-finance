# Chapter 5: Trading in Financial Markets

> Hull 4e, pp. 93–119. The market microstructure: how trades happen, who participates, what drives prices.

## Core Idea

Financial markets match buyers and sellers through **exchanges** (centralised, transparent) or **OTC** (decentralised, relationship-based). The **bid–ask spread** is the dealer's compensation; the **order book** aggregates limit orders; **market makers** provide liquidity. **Algorithmic trading** (HFT, market-making, statistical arbitrage) is now the dominant share of US equity volume. **Dark pools** are alternative trading systems (ATS) that do not display pre-trade quotes.

## Key Concepts

- **Exchange vs OTC** — centralised vs decentralised.
- **Bid–ask spread** — the half-spread is the dealer's compensation.
- **Limit order book** — the list of pending buy/sell orders at various prices.
- **Market order** — execute at the best available price; **limit order** — execute only at the specified price or better.
- **Stop order** — becomes a market order when the stop price is hit.
- **Iceberg order** — a large order displayed as a small order.
- **Market maker** — provides liquidity by quoting both sides.
- **Dark pool** — an alternative trading system with no pre-trade transparency.
- **HFT (high-frequency trading)** — algorithmic trading at sub-second latency.
- **Best execution** — the regulatory requirement to obtain the best price for the client.

## Key Takeaways

1. Markets are dominated by algorithms; HFT is a large share of volume.
2. The bid–ask spread and the order book are the institutional structure.
3. Best execution is the regulatory standard.

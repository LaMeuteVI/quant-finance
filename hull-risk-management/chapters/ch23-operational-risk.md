# Chapter 23: Operational Risk

> Hull 4e, pp. 481–499. The risk of loss from processes, people, systems, and external events.

## Core Idea

**Operational risk** is the risk of loss from inadequate or failed internal processes, people, systems, or external events (Basel definition). It includes **fraud** (internal and external), **system failures**, **process errors**, **legal risk**, **model risk** (ch25), and **cyber risk**. The Basel III framework has three approaches: **BIA** (Basic Indicator Approach, a flat 15% of average revenue), **TSA** (The Standardised Approach, by business line), and **AMA** (Advanced Measurement Approach, internal models).

## Key Concepts

- **BIA (Basic Indicator Approach)** — `OR_capital = 15% × average annual revenue (3 years)`.
- **TSA (The Standardised Approach)** — by business line, with different beta factors.
- **AMA (Advanced Measurement Approach)** — internal models; no longer approved by Basel III in its original form.
- **Internal fraud** — fraud by employees (e.g. rogue trader).
- **External fraud** — fraud by external parties.
- **Cyber risk** — the risk of cyber attacks; rapidly growing.
- **Business continuity** — the discipline of ensuring operations continue in a crisis.
- **The ORX database** — the Operational Riskdata eXchange; the institutional database of operational losses.
- **Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)** — leading indicators of operational risk.
- **Three lines of defence** — the model: business, risk management, internal audit.

## Key Takeaways

1. Operational risk is the risk of loss from processes, people, systems, external events.
2. Basel III's three approaches: BIA, TSA, AMA (no longer approved).
3. The ORX database is the institutional source for operational loss data.

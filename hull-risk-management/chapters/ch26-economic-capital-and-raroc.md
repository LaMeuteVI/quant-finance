# Chapter 26: Economic Capital and RAROC

> Hull 4e, pp. 547–563. The internal capital allocation framework.

## Core Idea

**Economic capital (EC)** is the bank's internal estimate of the capital needed to absorb unexpected losses over a 1-year horizon at a high confidence (typically 99.9%). It is computed as `EC = UL × multiplier`, where `UL` is the unexpected loss and the multiplier accounts for the diversification benefit across business lines. **RAROC (Risk-Adjusted Return on Capital)** is `RAROC = (revenue - expected loss - operating cost) / economic capital`. The bank's goal is to allocate capital to the businesses with the highest RAROC.

## Key Concepts

- **Economic capital (EC)** — internal capital at high confidence (typically 99.9%).
- **Unexpected loss (UL)** — loss in excess of expected loss.
- **Expected loss (EL)** — the average loss; reserved as a provision.
- **RAROC** — `RAROC = (revenue - EL - operating cost) / EC`.
- **Diversification benefit** — the reduction in total EC from aggregating across business lines.
- **Capital allocation** — the allocation of EC to business lines; based on RAROC.
- **Capital hurdle** — the minimum RAROC for a business to receive capital.
- **Hurdle rate** — the required return; typically cost of equity + risk premium.
- **Shareholder Value Added (SVA)** — `SVA = (RAROC - hurdle) × EC`.
- **Capital adequacy** — the regulatory minimum; the binding constraint.

## Formulas & Tables

- **EC**: `EC = UL × multiplier`; the multiplier reflects the diversification benefit.
- **RAROC**: `RAROC = (revenue - EL - operating cost) / EC`.
- **SVA**: `SVA = (RAROC - hurdle) × EC`.

## Key Takeaways

1. Economic capital is the bank's internal estimate of the capital needed.
2. RAROC is the standard performance metric; the higher the RAROC, the better.
3. Capital is allocated to the highest-RAROC businesses.

# Glossary — CFA Level III Equity Portfolio Management (2023)

**Active return** — Portfolio return minus benchmark return; equals Σ active weight × security return (ch 04)
**Active risk (tracking error)** — Standard deviation of active returns; realized (historical) or predicted (forward) (ch 02, 04)
**Active Share** — ½ Σ |w_portfolio − w_benchmark|; share of holdings that differ from the benchmark, 0 to 1 (ch 04)
**Active weight** — Portfolio weight minus benchmark weight (ch 04)
**Activist investing** — Taking a stake (typically below 10%) and pushing for governance, strategic, operational or capital-structure change (ch 01, 03)
**Alpha** — Active return not explained by rewarded-factor exposure; skill (timing, selection) plus luck (ch 04)
**Alternative beta** — Return once labelled alpha that is really exposure to rewarded factors (ch 04)
**Authorized participant** — ETF market maker who creates and redeems shares in kind against stock baskets (ch 02)
**Availability bias** — Over-weighting easily recalled outcomes; leads to familiar, undiversified holdings (ch 03)
**Back-test** — Simulation of a strategy on historical data at a fixed rebalance frequency (ch 03)
**Basis risk** — Futures price not moving one-for-one with the index (e.g., dividends) (ch 02)
**Bottom-up strategy** — Starts at company level (valuation, quality, momentum) and builds the portfolio from stock views (ch 03, 04)
**Breadth (BR)** — Number of independent (uncorrelated) active decisions per year (ch 04)
**Buffering** — Band around index breakpoints so stocks migrate only after crossing the outer edge (ch 02)
**Cash drag** — Tracking error from uninvested cash; negative in rising, positive in falling markets (ch 02)
**Closet indexer** — Fund marketed as active with low Active Share and low active risk (ch 01, 04)
**Confirmation bias** — Seeking information that supports existing views ("stock love") (ch 03)
**Contrarian investing** — Buying against sentiment on price lows and sharp moves, often depressed cyclicals (ch 03)
**CVaR (expected shortfall)** — Average loss beyond the VaR cutoff (ch 04)
**Deep value** — Very low P/B, often distressed, informationally inefficient names (ch 03)
**Dividend capture** — Buying before the ex-date to collect the dividend; price drop and taxes can offset it (ch 01)
**Dollar neutral vs market neutral** — Equal long and short dollars versus beta (and factor) exposure netting to zero (ch 04)
**Drawdown** — Loss from a portfolio's peak until recovery starts (ch 04)
**Effective number of stocks** — 1/HHI; equal-weighted stock count with the same concentration (ch 02)
**Equitizing** — Using futures, ETFs or swaps to give cash an equity exposure and restore beta (ch 02)
**Equal weighting** — 1/n weights; lowest concentration, small-cap bias, needs rebalancing, limited capacity (ch 02)
**Event-driven strategy** — Exploits mispricing around corporate events: M&A, spin-offs, buybacks, special dividends (ch 03)
**Excess return** — Rp − Rb; signed, distinct from tracking error (ch 02)
**Factor-mimicking portfolio (FMP)** — Theoretical dollar-neutral portfolio with unit exposure to one factor and none to others (ch 03)
**Factor tilt / factor rotation** — Deliberate over/underweighting of risk factors; changing the tilts over the cycle (ch 02, 03)
**Free-float adjustment** — Weights based only on publicly tradable shares (ch 02)
**Free-rider problem** — Non-engaging holders gain from another investor's engagement (ch 01)
**Full replication** — Holding every index constituent at index weight (ch 02)
**Fundamental indexation** — Weighting by sales, cash flow, book value, dividends, employees rather than price (Arnott, Hsu, Moore) (ch 02)
**Fundamental law of active management** — E(R_A) = IC × √BR × σ_RA × TC (ch 04)
**GARP** — Growth at a reasonable price; screen with PEG (ch 03)
**Gross / net exposure** — Longs + |shorts| / longs − |shorts| (ch 04)
**Growth trap** — Growth stock whose price already reflects growth or whose growth fades (ch 03)
**Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI)** — Σ w²; 1/n to 1 (ch 02)
**High-quality value** — Valuation plus consistent earnings, high ROE, strength and good management (ch 03)
**High-water mark** — Prior peak net value that must be exceeded before a performance fee is paid (ch 01)
**Holdings-based style analysis** — Aggregates stock-level style scores bottom-up; more accurate, shows current style (ch 03)
**Illusion of control** — Overestimating one's influence on outcomes; excessive trading, concentration (ch 03)
**Information coefficient (IC)** — Correlation between factor score (or forecast) and subsequent return; Spearman rank version is robust (ch 03, 04)
**Information ratio** — Active return / active risk (ch 04)
**Investable index** — Index whose performance can be replicated in the market (ch 02)
**Long extension (130/30)** — 130% long, 30% short, net 100%, gross 160% (ch 04)
**Loss aversion** — Preferring to avoid losses; holds losers, sells winners early (ch 03)
**Market-neutral strategy** — Long/short with factor/beta exposure netting to zero; often equitized with an overlay (ch 04)
**Market-on-close (MOC) order** — Executes at the closing price used by the index (ch 02)
**Minimum-variance index** — Weights chosen by optimizer to minimize volatility (ch 02)
**Momentum** — Winners over past 12 months keep winning for 2-12 months; short-term reversal at one month (ch 03)
**Optimization (index tracking)** — Minimize tracking error subject to constraints such as number of holdings (ch 02)
**Overlay** — Derivative layer (completion, rebalancing, currency) managed separately from the securities (ch 02, 03)
**Packeting** — Moving a migrating stock between indexes in tranches (ch 02)
**Pairs trading / statistical arbitrage** — Long laggard, short leader when the spread deviates; exit at mean; structural-break risk (ch 03)
**PEG ratio** — P/E divided by expected EPS growth in % (ch 03)
**Rewarded factor** — Risk exposure with persistent premium: Market, Size, Value, Momentum (also quality, low beta, liquidity) (ch 03, 04)
**Risk arbitrage** — Merger arbitrage: long target (and short acquirer in a stock deal), earns deal spread, risks deal failure (ch 03)
**Risk budgeting** — Allocating the total risk appetite among positions, factors, sectors (ch 04)
**Returns-based style analysis** — Constrained regression of fund returns on style indexes (Sharpe 1992) (ch 03)
**Securities lending** — Collateralized lending of shares; fee plus collateral reinvestment income; borrower gets votes, manufactures dividends (ch 01, 02)
**Short squeeze** — Forced buy-ins after a sharp rise and collateral calls (ch 04)
**Slippage** — Execution price versus bid-ask midpoint at order entry; market impact plus trend cost (ch 04)
**Smart beta** — Rules-based factor or alternative-weighted indexing (ch 02)
**Specials** — Stocks in high borrowing demand with high lending fees (ch 01)
**Spearman rank IC** — Pearson correlation of ranks; robust to outliers (ch 03)
**Stratified sampling** — Holding a sample chosen to match strata (sector, country, style) weights of the index (ch 02)
**Style box (Morningstar)** — 3x3 grid size by value/core/growth; stocks belong to one cell (ch 03)
**Survivorship bias / look-ahead bias** — Testing only on survivors / using data not yet published (ch 03)
**Top-down strategy** — Starts from macro, country, sector, style and volatility views; relies on factor timing (ch 03, 04)
**Tracking error** — σ(Rp − Rb); non-negative (ch 02, 04)
**Transfer coefficient (TC)** — Ability to turn insights into weights without constraint; 1 = unconstrained (ch 04)
**Value trap** — Cheap stock with deteriorating prospects and no catalyst (ch 03)
**VaR / IVaR / MVaR** — Minimum loss at a probability over a horizon / change from adding a position / effect of a tiny change (ch 04)

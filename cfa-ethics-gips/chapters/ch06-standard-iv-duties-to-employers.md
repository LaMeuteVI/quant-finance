# Chapter 6: Standard IV, Duties to Employers (Loyalty, Additional Compensation, Responsibilities of Supervisors)
> **Source**: CFA Program Curriculum 2023, Level III, Reading 32 (Guidance for Standards I-VII; Standards of Practice Handbook, 11th ed.) | CFA Institute

## Core Idea
Standard IV governs the employment relationship: be loyal and do not harm the employer (**IV(A)**), do not take outside compensation that competes or conflicts without written consent of everyone involved (**IV(B)**), and, if you supervise anyone, make reasonable efforts to ensure they comply with law and the Code and Standards (**IV(C)**). Employer interests are always **below** client interests and market integrity: when an employer acts illegally or unethically, acting against its instructions, including whistleblowing, can be justified if the intent is to protect clients or markets, not personal gain.

## Learning Outcomes Covered
- Apply IV(A) to IV(C) to fact patterns (departure, competition, outside income, supervision).
- Recommend procedures: termination, competition, incident-reporting, compliance systems, incentives.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **IV(A) Loyalty**
  - *Scope*: act for the employer's benefit in employment-related matters; do not deprive it of your skills, divulge confidential information or cause harm. Follow employer policies unless they conflict with law or the Code. Not a duty to subordinate personal or family obligations; talk to the employer if personal matters regularly interfere. Employers need not adopt the Code, but should not create conflicting policies; senior management must design incentives that do not encourage misconduct.
  - *Independent practice*: preparation is allowed; competitive practice for pay requires notifying the employer (services, duration, compensation) and **written consent** before you start. "Practice" means services the employer currently sells.
  - *Leaving an employer (permitted vs. prohibited)*

| Permitted | Prohibited (especially in combination) |
|---|---|
| Preparing a new business off hours (registering a firm, arranging premises) without soliciting | Soliciting employer's current or prospective clients before leaving |
| After departure: contacting former clients using public information, absent a noncompete or non-solicitation agreement | Taking records, files, client lists, models or software (any medium, including phones and home computers) without written permission |
| Using skills, experience and general knowledge at the new employer | Misappropriating trade secrets or confidential information; self-dealing in the employer's opportunity |
| Relying on an industry protocol (e.g., broker recruiting) if all steps are followed | Competing for the same RFP before resignation takes effect without consent from employer and RFP issuer |

  - Once notice is given, follow the employer's policy on informing clients. Work product made for the employer belongs to it; even unpaid interns count as employees. Honor noncompete terms. Names of clients and general experience are not confidential by themselves.
  - *Social media*: firm-approved business accounts are firm assets; keep personal and professional accounts separate; follow client-notification policy on departure; do not leak firm views via a personal blog.
  - *Whistleblowing*: personal and employer interests are secondary to market integrity and clients. Contradicting instructions, breaching certain policies, or copying records to preserve evidence may be justified only to protect clients or markets and never for personal gain. Escalate through internal channels first when possible.
  - *Nature of employment*: employee vs. independent contractor is decided by control (hours, location, facilities, expense reimbursement, other clients). Contractors are governed by their client agreement.

- **IV(B) Additional Compensation Arrangements**: any gift, benefit or compensation (cash or in kind, including performance bonuses from clients and director perks) that competes with or could conflict with the employer's interests requires **written consent from all parties** (documentable email counts). Procedure: immediate written report to supervisor and compliance specifying the nature, approximate amount and duration of the arrangement, confirmed by the offering party. Part-time staff: discuss competing services up front.

- **IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors**
  - *Who*: anyone with control or influence over employees, whether or not they are CFA members.
  - *Reasonable efforts to ensure compliance (11th ed.)*: includes prevention and detection, but goes beyond them. Delegation is allowed, but you must instruct delegates.
  - *Adequate compliance system*: exists before violations; clearly written, tailored manual; designated compliance officer with authority and resources; supervision hierarchy; checks and balances; scope; documentation of monitoring and testing; permissible conduct; reporting and sanctions; education and recurring training; incentives that reward ethical behavior; professional-conduct evaluation in performance reviews; best-practice adoption (GIPS, Asset Manager Code).
  - *If inadequate*: raise with senior management, recommend corrective action; if you clearly cannot supervise, **decline in writing** supervisory responsibility until reasonable procedures exist.
  - *On learning of a violation*: respond promptly, investigate scope (do not rely on the employee's assurance), warn and report upward is not enough, restrict or increase monitoring pending the outcome, and revise procedures.
  - *Safe harbour*: reasonable procedures and effort may mean no breach if a violation is not detected, but violations may show inadequacy, and a supervisor who knows or should know procedures are not followed can breach.
  - *Code of ethics vs. compliance procedures*: keep the code short, principled and plain-language; keep detailed procedures separate.

## Key Concepts
- **Duty of loyalty to employer**: subordinate to clients and markets.
- **Independent practice**: competitive paid service while employed; requires disclosure and written consent.
- **Noncompete / non-solicitation agreement**: contract limits on post-employment competition; follow them in addition to the Code.
- **Employer property**: records, models, files, client lists, firm-approved social media accounts.
- **Industry recruiting protocol**: agreement among firms permitting limited client information to move with brokers if procedures are followed.
- **Written consent**: any retrievable documented communication.
- **Supervisor**: any person with control or influence over others' conduct.
- **Compliance system**: written, tailored, communicated, monitored and enforced set of procedures plus a compliance officer.
- **Whistleblowing**: justified disclosure or contrary action to protect clients or markets.
- **Incentive structure**: compensation that must reward how results are achieved.
- **Independent contractor**: low employer control; duties set by agreement.
- **Incident-reporting procedure**: confidential, often anonymous channel for suspected wrongdoing.

## Mental Models
- Use the **loyalty ladder**: clients and market integrity, then employer, then self; employer loyalty ends where it would harm clients or markets.
- Use **"prepare, do not compete"** when planning to leave: anything you would do for the new venture that touches clients, files or time is competition.
- Use **"employer's property = anything you produced on their time, in any medium"** to test document taking.
- Use **consent in writing from everyone affected** for outside compensation.
- Use **"system plus supervision"** for IV(C): written procedures are necessary; checks, training and incentives make them real.

## Worked Example
*Departure checklist (reconstructed).* A portfolio manager plans to join a competitor and has a client list on a laptop, a non-solicitation clause for 12 months, and a pending client RFP.
- Before notice: do not contact clients; do not copy files; do not respond to the RFP for the new firm. Registering the new entity off-hours is acceptable.
- At notice: follow the firm's client-notification policy; return or erase firm records unless permission is given in writing.
- After leaving: general experience and publicly available contact data may be used; the non-solicitation clause still bars solicitation for 12 months regardless of IV(A).
- Result: contact only through permitted channels; interpretation: the Code adds the duty of loyalty during employment, contract law adds restrictions after, and the stricter obligation governs.

## Anti-patterns
- Soliciting clients while still on the payroll.
- Taking "your own" models, spreadsheets or client contacts out the door.
- Accepting side income from clients, issuers or boards without written consent.
- Supervisors who rely on assurances, delegate without instruction, or ignore red flags because the unit is profitable.
- Mixing code of ethics with procedure manuals, producing documents nobody reads.
- Pay structures that reward results regardless of method.
- Blogging or tweeting firm views from a personal account.
- Using whistleblowing for personal gain rather than client or market protection.

## Exam Focus & Traps
- Loyalty to employer never overrides duties to clients or market integrity; whistleblowing is justified only for protecting clients or the market, not personal gain.
- Departing employees may prepare but not solicit; may not take records; may use public information afterwards unless a noncompete or non-solicitation agreement applies.
- IV(B) requires **written consent from all parties**; IV(A) independent practice requires employer consent; **client gifts need only disclosure** (I(B)).
- IV(C) in the 11th edition: **"ensure compliance"**, including detecting and preventing; supervisors who cannot discharge duties because of a missing system should **decline in writing**.
- Supervisor titles are irrelevant: anyone with control or influence is covered, CFA member or not.
- A compliance system must pre-exist the violation; assurances are not an investigation.
- Employer policies bind you only where they do not conflict with law or the Code.
- Interns and contractors: employee status depends on control; interns using firm resources are employees for IV(A).
- Delegation does not remove supervisory duty; instruct delegates.
- Prior work for a first party: the first party gets first chance to act on it.

## Key Takeaways
1. Be loyal to the employer within the limits set by clients and market integrity.
2. Prepare for a new job off the clock; do not solicit, copy files or compete before departure.
3. Disclose and get written consent for outside compensation that conflicts, from every party.
4. Whistleblowing is legitimate when aimed at protecting clients or markets.
5. Supervisors must build, communicate, monitor and enforce a tailored compliance system.
6. On learning of a violation, investigate scope immediately and restrict the person pending results.
7. Incentive design and training are part of supervision, not extras.
8. Keep a short, principled code of ethics separate from procedural manuals.

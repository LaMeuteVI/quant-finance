# Chapter 2: Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct (Overview and Master Table)
> **Source**: CFA Program Curriculum 2020, Level I, Ethical and Professional Standards, Reading 2 (Standards of Practice Handbook, 11th ed., Preface through the Code and Standards text) | CFA Institute

## Core Idea
The **Code of Ethics** holds six high-level aspirational principles; the **Standards of Professional Conduct** hold seven standards (I to VII, 22 sub-standards) that turn those principles into enforceable behaviour. They are an "interwoven tapestry": most real cases touch several standards at once. Adherence is personal (members and candidates), applies regardless of job function, culture or local law, and is enforced by the **Professional Conduct Program (PCP)** with the **Disciplinary Review Committee (DRC)**. The rationale: markets work only if investors trust that they are fair and transparent, and law alone cannot guarantee that.

## Learning Outcomes Covered
- Describe the structure of the Professional Conduct Program and the enforcement process.
- State the six components of the Code of Ethics and the seven Standards.
- Explain the ethical responsibilities each Standard and sub-section requires.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Code of Ethics (six principles)** Members and candidates must:
  1. Act with integrity, competence, diligence and respect, ethically, toward the public, clients, prospective clients, employers, employees, colleagues and other market participants.
  2. Place the integrity of the profession and clients' interests above their own.
  3. Use reasonable care and independent professional judgment in analysis, recommendations, actions and other professional activities.
  4. Practise and encourage others to practise professionally and ethically, reflecting credit on themselves and the profession.
  5. Promote the integrity and viability of the global capital markets for the ultimate benefit of society (replaced the older "uphold the rules governing capital markets").
  6. Maintain and improve their professional competence and that of other investment professionals.

- **Professional Conduct Program enforcement flow**
  1. **Inquiry sources**: (a) the annual Professional Conduct Statement (self-disclosure of litigation, criminal investigation, written complaints); (b) written complaints; (c) media, regulatory notices, public sources; (d) exam-day proctor reports; (e) post-exam score analysis and monitoring of social media for leaked exam content.
  2. **Investigation** by Professional Conduct staff: written explanation, interviews (member, complainants, third parties), documents.
  3. **Outcome**: no sanction, **cautionary letter**, or proceedings to discipline.
  4. The member or candidate may **accept or reject** charges and proposed sanction. If rejected, a **DRC panel** (volunteer charterholders) reviews and decides violation and sanction.
  5. **Sanctions**: public censure; suspension of membership and use of the designation; revocation of the charter. Candidates can be suspended or barred from the CFA Program.
  - The Board of Governors oversees the PCP. The Standards of Practice Council (SPC) maintains and interprets the Code and Standards.

- **Adopting the Code (firms and non-members)**: a party whose code meets all principles may claim compliance only with the statement that the claim "has not been verified by CFA Institute". The separate **Asset Manager Code of Professional Conduct** is firm-level (six areas: loyalty to clients, investment process, trading, compliance, performance evaluation, disclosure).

- **Changes in the 11th edition (testable)**:
  - Code principle 5 reworded (capital markets "for the ultimate benefit of society", social-contract idea).
  - IV(C) shifted from "detect and prevent violations" to "make reasonable efforts to **ensure** compliance" (broader than detect and prevent).
  - V(B) gained an explicit duty to disclose **significant limitations and risks** of the investment process.
  - VII(A) renamed "Conduct as **Participants in CFA Institute Programs**" (covers CIPM and Investment Foundations, not only the CFA Program).
  - New guidance themes: social media and reliance on quantitative models.

## Key Concepts
- **Code vs. Standards**: Code = aspirational principles; Standards = practical rules members "must" follow. Both apply together.
- **Strictest-rule principle**: where law, employer policy and the Code and Standards differ, follow the strictest (explicit in I(A)).
- **Professional Conduct Statement**: annual self-disclosure that is one of the PCP's inquiry sources.
- **Cautionary letter**: a PCP outcome that is not a disciplinary sanction.
- **Culture of integrity**: senior-management-driven ethical culture that goes beyond legal compliance. Possibly the single most important factor in employee behaviour.
- **Market sustainability**: individual and aggregate decisions, products and compensation designs can affect global market stability. Those in authority carry special responsibility.
- **Universal ethics**: for a global industry, trust and integrity should override local custom.
- **Handbook examples**: hypothetical, not all-inclusive. Individuals are assumed to be CFA members unless stated.
- **False sense of security**: any set of ethical rules gives it unless fully understood, enforced and embedded in daily practice.
- **Ethics vs. law**: law is "what is required"; ethics is what is morally right. Regulators lack resources, so law alone cannot create an ethical culture.
- **Claiming compliance**: needs the unverified-claim wording above.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
No formulas. **Master table of the Standards** (what each demands in one line):

| Std | Name | Core requirement (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|
| I(A) | Knowledge of the Law | Know and comply with all applicable laws, rules, regulations (including the Code and Standards); follow the stricter where they conflict; do not knowingly participate or assist in a violation and dissociate from one |
| I(B) | Independence and Objectivity | Use reasonable care and judgment to keep independence and objectivity; do not offer, solicit or accept anything that could reasonably compromise your or another's independence and objectivity |
| I(C) | Misrepresentation | Do not knowingly misrepresent analysis, recommendations, actions or other professional activities |
| I(D) | Misconduct | No professional conduct involving dishonesty, fraud or deceit, and no act reflecting adversely on professional reputation, integrity or competence |
| II(A) | Material Nonpublic Information | If you hold MNPI that could affect an investment's value, do not act or cause others to act on it |
| II(B) | Market Manipulation | No practices that distort prices or artificially inflate volume with intent to mislead participants |
| III(A) | Loyalty, Prudence, and Care | Duty of loyalty to clients; act with reasonable care and prudent judgment; clients' interests ahead of employer's and your own |
| III(B) | Fair Dealing | Deal fairly and objectively with all clients in analysis, recommendations, actions |
| III(C) | Suitability | Advisory: reasonable inquiry into experience, objectives, constraints, update regularly; judge suitability vs. written objectives and in total-portfolio context. Mandate: act only consistently with stated objectives and constraints |
| III(D) | Performance Presentation | Make reasonable efforts so performance information is fair, accurate and complete |
| III(E) | Preservation of Confidentiality | Keep current, former and prospective client information confidential unless it concerns illegal activity, disclosure is legally required, or the client permits it |
| IV(A) | Loyalty (employer) | Act for the employer's benefit; do not deprive it of your skills, divulge confidential information or cause it harm |
| IV(B) | Additional Compensation Arrangements | Do not accept gifts, benefits or compensation that compete with or may conflict with the employer's interest unless all parties give **written consent** |
| IV(C) | Responsibilities of Supervisors | Make reasonable efforts to ensure those under supervision comply with laws, rules and the Code and Standards |
| V(A) | Diligence and Reasonable Basis | Diligence, independence, thoroughness; reasonable and adequate basis supported by research and investigation |
| V(B) | Communication with Clients | (1) Disclose process basics and promptly any material change; (2) disclose significant limitations and risks; (3) use reasonable judgment on which factors matter and include them; (4) separate fact from opinion |
| V(C) | Record Retention | Develop and maintain records supporting analyses, recommendations, actions and client communications |
| VI(A) | Disclosure of Conflicts | Full and fair disclosure of anything that could impair independence or duties to clients, prospects, employer; prominent, plain language, effective |
| VI(B) | Priority of Transactions | Client and employer transactions come before those where you are beneficial owner |
| VI(C) | Referral Fees | Disclose to employer, clients, prospects, as appropriate, compensation or benefits received or paid for referrals |
| VII(A) | Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs | Do not compromise the reputation or integrity of CFA Institute or the designation, or the integrity, validity or security of its programs |
| VII(B) | Reference to CFA Institute, Designation, Program | Do not misrepresent or exaggerate the meaning of membership, designation or candidacy |

Mnemonic of grouping: I Professionalism (4), II Integrity of Capital Markets (2), III Duties to Clients (5), IV Duties to Employers (3), V Investment Analysis, Recommendations and Actions (3), VI Conflicts of Interest (3), VII Responsibilities as CFA member or candidate (2). Total 22.

## Mental Models
- Use the **strictest-rule rule** whenever law, firm policy and the Code and Standards differ: comply with whichever is most demanding.
- Use **"which standard owns the verb?"** to classify a fact pattern: protect or misuse information = II(A)/III(E)/IV(A); present numbers = III(D); communicate views = V(B); personal benefit = I(B), IV(B), VI.
- Use **client-first, market-integrity-over-client** ordering when duties collide (employer duty is subordinate where it harms clients or markets).
- Use **interwoven tapestry** reading: check at least the adjacent standards (e.g., a gift touches I(B), IV(B), VI(A)).
- Use **disclosure is necessary, not sufficient** where a conflict is serious: disclosure helps VI(A) but does not cure a breach of loyalty or independence.

## Worked Example
*Mapping a fact pattern (reconstructed).* A portfolio manager accepts an expensive trip from a broker who executes her trades, does not tell her employer, then tells clients her firm "uses a fully quantitative process" though stock picks are partly discretionary.
- Step 1, who is affected: employer, clients, markets. Step 2, behaviours: gift, non-disclosure, process claim.
- Step 3, standards: I(B) (independence compromised), VI(A) (conflict undisclosed to employer and clients), IV(B)/IV(A) (benefit conflicting with employer's interest without written consent), V(B)(1) and I(C) (inaccurate description of process), III(A) (client interests behind own).
- Result: several simultaneous violations. Remedy: refuse or return gift or obtain proper approval and disclosure, correct client materials promptly.

## Anti-patterns
- Reading a Standard in isolation and missing companion standards.
- Treating the Code as decoration and the Standards as the only binding part (both must be followed).
- Assuming compliance with local law satisfies the Code.
- Claiming "CFA Institute compliance" without the unverified-claim wording.
- Taking Handbook examples as an exhaustive list of violations.
- Ignoring the annual Professional Conduct Statement duty to self-disclose litigation, investigations or complaints.

## Exam Focus & Traps
- Only **Standard VII** concerns CFA Institute and its programs; loyalty is in **III(A)** (clients) and **IV(A)** (employer) and not VII; supervisors are **IV(C)**.
- Pairs often confused: gifts affecting independence = **I(B)**; compensation competing with employer = **IV(B)** (written consent from all parties allows it); referral fees = **VI(C)**; fair, accurate and complete performance = **III(D)**; reasonable and adequate basis = **V(A)**; fact vs. opinion = **V(B)(4)**; suitability in total-portfolio context = **III(C)(1)(c)**; price distortion = **II(B)**; MNPI "cause others to act" = **II(A)**.
- Old vs. new wording: IV(C) now "ensure compliance"; Code principle now includes "viability ... for the ultimate benefit of society"; VII(A) now covers all CFA Institute programs.
- PCP: DRC panel is convened only if the member rejects charges or sanction; media reports can start inquiries; sanctions include censure, suspension and revocation.
- Handbook examples are not all-inclusive; Handbook explains but does not list penalties.
- Priority of Transactions is about the member's own (beneficial-owner) trades: clients and employer come first, whatever the employer's view.
- Code principle 2 is about "integrity of the profession and the interests of clients", not employers.

## Key Takeaways
1. Six Code principles state the spirit; seven Standards (22 sub-standards) state the minimum conduct.
2. The Standards are binding on every member and candidate worldwide, and the strictest applicable rule governs.
3. Standards interlock; analyse fact patterns against all that apply.
4. PCP flow: inquiry, investigation, charges, accept or reject, DRC panel, sanction.
5. Sanctions range from cautionary letter (not a sanction) to censure, suspension and charter revocation.
6. Laws are insufficient; a culture of integrity set by senior management is the main defence.
7. Eleventh-edition changes (supervisors, risk disclosure, VII(A), Code principle 5) are high-yield.
8. Firms may adopt the Code but must not claim verified compliance.

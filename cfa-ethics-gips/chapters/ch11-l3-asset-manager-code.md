# Chapter 11: Asset Manager Code of Professional Conduct
> **Source**: CFA Program Curriculum 2023, Level III, Reading 34 · Ethical and Professional Standards | Schacht, Stokes, Doggett (CFA Institute Asset Manager Code, 2nd edition, 2010)

## Core Idea
The Asset Manager Code (AMC) is the firm-level counterpart of the individual Code and Standards. It is voluntary, applies to every employee of a "Manager" (firm managing separate accounts or pooled vehicles, including hedge funds), and is organised as six General Principles and six sections A-F. It adds firm-level obligations the individual Standards lack: compliance officer, independent verification of client information, business continuity, firmwide risk management, valuation controls, and a detailed disclosure list. A firm either complies with all of it or may not claim compliance at all.

## Learning Outcomes Covered
- Explain the purpose of the Asset Manager Code and the benefits of adoption
- Explain the responsibilities under the six General Principles of Conduct
- Determine whether a manager's practices and procedures are consistent with the Code
- Recommend practices and procedures to prevent violations

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Six General Principles of Conduct**: act in a professional and ethical manner; act for the benefit of clients; act with independence and objectivity; act with skill, competence and diligence; communicate with clients in a timely and accurate manner; uphold applicable capital-market rules. Use as the headings when mapping a firm's policy manual.
- **Code structure A-F**: A Loyalty to Clients; B Investment Process and Actions; C Trading; D Risk Management, Compliance and Support; E Performance and Valuation; F Disclosures. The Appendix gives recommendations; required items are "must", recommended items are "should".
- **Claiming compliance (all-or-nothing)**: (1) adopt at the board or senior-management level; (2) meet every principle and provision (existing policies need not be rewritten if at least consistent); (3) use the exact statement: the firm claims compliance with the CFA Institute Asset Manager Code of Professional Conduct and the claim has not been verified by CFA Institute; (4) notify CFA Institute through its online notification process (communication only, no enforcement). Partial claims ("complies except for ...", "complies with parts A, B and C") are prohibited. Failure mode: any single gap voids the claim.
- **Compliance-program checklist (D)**: written policies and procedures; compliance officer who is competent, credible, empowered and, where possible, independent of investment and operations and reporting to the CEO or board; employee acknowledgment of the Code; training; documentation and prompt action on breaches; independent third-party verification of client information; records; qualified staff and resources; continuity plan; independent risk function.
- **Disclosure list (F4 a-l)**: use as a checklist (see table).

## Key Concepts
- **Manager**: any firm managing client assets as separate accounts or pooled funds, including mutual funds, funds of funds and hedge funds.
- **Client interests first (A1)**: align compensation with clients and avoid incentives that induce conflicting actions; applies to selection, transactions, monitoring and custody.
- **Confidentiality (A2)**: privacy policy covering collection, storage, protection and use; does not override duty to report suspected illegal activity; consider an anti-money-laundering policy.
- **Gifts (A3)**: refuse gifts or entertainment above minimal value from providers, targets or partners; firm sets limits (for example per vendor per period), bans cash, requires documentation and disclosure through supervisor or compliance.
- **Mandate discipline (B5)**: act only within stated objectives and constraints; allow flexibility only when expressly agreed; disclose material changes well in advance and let clients redeem without undue penalty.
- **Separate-account suitability (B6)**: understand objectives, risk tolerance (ability and willingness), horizon, liquidity, constraints, tax, legal; written IPS reviewed with the client at least annually; agree the benchmark and evaluation details.
- **Market manipulation (B2)**: transaction-based (for example trading illiquid stocks at period end to lift performance, or cornering a position with a related derivative) and information-based (false rumours, pressuring sell-side analysts).
- **Material nonpublic information (C1)**: information barriers (firewalls); pending trades and fund holdings can themselves be MNPI; M&A information before announcement; mosaic theory remains allowed.
- **Priority of client trades (C2)**: no own trades ahead of clients; limit employee trading; restricted and watch lists; pre-approval for IPOs and private placements; quarterly trade confirmations and annual holdings statements; co-investment allowed only if clients are not disadvantaged.
- **Commissions (C3)**: commissions are client property; soft dollars may buy only products or services that directly assist investment decision making (research, analysis, trading and risk analysis, selection, allocation, suitability), not firm management; disclose; the CFA Institute Soft Dollar Standards offer best practice.
- **Best execution (C4)**: seek the best overall result considering size, market, liquidity, speed, anonymity, impact; when clients direct brokerage, warn them and obtain written acknowledgment.
- **Fair allocation (C5)**: block trades with average price, pro rata allocation, written policies for IPOs and private placements; no account routinely traded first.
- **Records (D4)**: hard copy or electronic; keep at least seven years unless local law requires otherwise.
- **Risk management (D7)**: objective, independent of portfolio managers; identifies market, credit, liquidity, counterparty, concentration and operational risks; stress, scenario and back-tests; outsource if too small.
- **Valuation (E2)**: fair-market prices and good-faith fair-value methods for unquoted assets; independent third party or independent directors approve policy and review valuations to remove the conflict with performance fees.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
No formulas. Disclosure checklist (F4):

| Item | What must be disclosed |
|---|---|
| a Conflicts | Broker relationships, other accounts, fee structures; soft dollars, referral fees, directed brokerage, allocation, personal holdings, co-investment, affiliated brokers |
| b Regulatory/disciplinary actions | Significant instances affecting integrity, ethics or competence |
| c Investment process | Strategies, risks, lock-ups, derivatives, leverage |
| d Fees and costs | Methodology; gross and net returns; retrospective actual fees; average or expected costs to prospects |
| e Soft/bundled commissions | Amount, what is received, client benefit |
| f Performance | Regular, at least quarterly, ideally within 30 days of quarter end |
| g Valuation methods | By asset class, not boilerplate |
| h Voting policies | Policy and how to obtain voting record |
| i Trade allocation | Policy and changes |
| j Review/audit results | For funds submitted to audit |
| k Significant personnel or organizational changes | Turnover, M&A |
| l Risk management | Process, material changes, risk metrics to be provided |

Who reports to whom: the CCO should report to the CEO or board; risk management independent of portfolio managers; valuation independent where fees depend on returns.

## Mental Models
- **"Required vs recommended"**: Use when grading a firm's practice. Missing a "must" voids compliance; missing a "should" (for example CCO independence) is still graded as inconsistent in the exam key.
- **"Commissions belong to the client"**: Use for soft-dollar questions. Test: does it directly aid the investment decision? If it supports firm management, no.
- **"Size drives depth"**: Use for continuity, risk and compliance questions: a large hedge-fund manager needs backup monitoring and trading plans, not just offsite data backups.
- **"Tell clients before you change"**: Use for any strategy drift. Continue the agreed strategy until disclosure and a redemption window exist.
- **"Independence cures valuation conflict"**: Use whenever fees depend on marks.

## Worked Example
**Playbook: Bornelli versus the Code (policy audit).**
Facts: independent third-party review of portfolio information (good, D3); seven-year hard-copy and electronic records (good, D4); CCO investigates complaints but reports to the CIO; business continuity relies on twice-daily offsite backups plus an outsourced firm for communication plans, but no alternative plans for monitoring and trading; fee disclosure states a 2% management fee and 20% incentive fee but no expected or average cost; hedge fund reports quarterly within 30 days; alternative fund reports semiannually during a three-year lock-up and uses internal valuation models when no quotes exist, with disclosure.
- Violations or gaps: CCO reporting line (D2: should be independent and report to CEO or board); continuity plan (D6: should include alternative plans for monitoring, analysis and trading); fee disclosure lacks average or expected expenses for prospects and actual itemised fees for clients (F4d); semiannual reporting during lock-up (F4f: at least quarterly regardless of lock-up so clients can manage total asset allocation).
- Consistent: quarterly reporting within 30 days with gross and net returns; a client may request itemisation and the firm should provide specific management fee, incentive fee and commissions; internal models are acceptable when no independent quotation exists if applied in good faith and disclosed (and independently reviewed where the pooled fund has independent directors or third parties).
Interpretation: the Code is judged item by item; one gap blocks the compliance claim.

**Playbook: SZR (small advisory firm).** Facts and findings: broadened strategy without notice: B5(b) breach (disclose first, allow redemption). Board seat and options in a micro-cap that the fund can now buy: conflict (A1, A3) and should be ended or disclosed and restricted. Sastre directs trades to a local adviser with written acknowledgment: permitted for a discretionary account. Zane's rule to place Sastre's trades first through the local adviser before the commingled fund: breaches best execution and fair allocation (C4, C5) for the other clients. Schmidt reveals Sastre's hiring and fee discount to a consultant: confidentiality (A2). Compliance officer hired as a relative with sales background and a continuity plan consisting of taking records home: fails D2, D5 and D6.

## Anti-patterns
- Claiming partial compliance with the Code.
- Compliance officer who reports to the investment head.
- Treating a lock-up as a reason for infrequent performance reporting.
- Using client commissions to pay for firm overhead (rent, office systems).
- Placing the favoured client's trades first and the pool second.
- Changing the fund's strategy and telling investors in the next newsletter.
- Valuation done by the portfolio manager when fees depend on returns.
- Relying on boilerplate valuation disclosure.
- Staffing compliance with a relative without relevant experience.
- Business-continuity plan that relies on one employee taking tapes home.

## Exam Focus & Traps
- AMC is for firms; the Code and Standards are for individuals. The Code and Standards bind individual members and candidates; a firm is only encouraged to build them into its own code of ethics. The firm-level document a firm adopts and claims compliance with is the AMC (Level III practice item: Nano's statement was correct only for the AMC).
- Verified? No. The compliance statement includes "has not been verified by CFA Institute".
- Seven-year record retention is the recommended minimum when law is silent; local regulation that is longer or shorter controls.
- CCO independence, reporting to CEO or board, is a "should" yet appears as the correct inconsistent item.
- Quarterly reporting within 30 days is the minimum; monthly is not required unless the client asks.
- Directed brokerage: acceptable if the client acknowledges possible loss of best execution; the manager must not harm other clients while implementing it.
- Soft dollars must serve the investment decision process; disclose amount, goods received and client benefit.
- Fee disclosure: gross and net returns at minimum; prospects get expected fees; clients get actual fees, with itemisation on request.
- Fund-level confidentiality extends to the fact a client has hired the firm and its fee terms.

## Key Takeaways
1. The AMC translates the Code and Standards into firm duties and adds compliance, risk, valuation, continuity and disclosure requirements.
2. Compliance is binary and requires the exact unverified-claim statement plus online notification.
3. Section C requires client trades first, fair allocation, best execution and disciplined soft-dollar use.
4. Section D requires an empowered, independent compliance officer, independent verification of client information, qualified staff, continuity and independent risk management.
5. Section E requires fair, complete performance and independent valuation processes.
6. Section F lists twelve disclosures; fees, soft dollars, risk process and performance cadence are the most tested.
7. Mandate changes require advance notice and penalty-free redemption.
8. GIPS is the recommended model for performance reporting under E1 (see ch12).

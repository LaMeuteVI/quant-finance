# Chapter 10: Application of the Code and Standards (Level III)
> **Source**: CFA Program Curriculum 2023, Level III, Reading 33 · Ethical and Professional Standards | Case authors: Stokes, Kard, McMillan, Morey, Teo and others (CFA Institute Ethics Cases, 11th-edition Standards); practice problems 2015

## Core Idea
Level III turns the Standards into longer, multi-actor playbooks set in private wealth, sell-side research, IPO distribution, fund management, and private banking. The same Standards repeat, but the traps are subtler: a conflict that is disclosed in a prospectus is still not disclosed to clients; a favored client who calls after publication is fine but one who is called before is not; a supervisor who "escalates" has not necessarily investigated. Reading each case as facts, Standards implicated, violation analysis, correct conduct is the efficient way to learn it.

## Learning Outcomes Covered
- Evaluate practices, policies and conduct relative to the Code of Ethics and Standards of Professional Conduct
- Explain how practices, policies or conduct do or do not violate the Code and Standards

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Framework for Ethical Decision Making**: Identify (facts, stakeholders and duties, principles, conflicts), Consider (situational influences, guidance, alternatives), Decide and act, Reflect. Use it when pressure (political, bonus, boss) distorts judgment; the "Consider" step is where situational influence is named.
- **Governance as the antidote to bias (Sovereign Investment Corporation)**: for a mandate subject to political pressure, a rigorous written governance framework for investment decisions beats political approval or conflict disclosure alone. Why: disclosure does not replace independent analysis; delegating approval upward can import the bias.
- **Dissociation and escalation ladder (I(A))**: confront, then escalate internally (supervisor, compliance, legal counsel), then dissociate; reporting to regulators is not compelled unless law requires but may be prudent. First step in the Gabby Sim case after a CIO dismisses money-laundering concerns: compliance or legal counsel.
- **Supervisor investigation test (IV(C))**: on learning of a possible violation, promptly start an assessment to find the scope; escalation or the employee's assurances alone are not enough. Balmer fails when she only talks to Graeme and reports upward; she passes on the Milgram complaint because she summoned Graeme and sat in on the review.
- **IPS-first suitability routine (III(C))**: gather experience, risk and return objectives, constraints; write the IPS before any recommendation; update at least annually and before material changes; judge each holding in the context of the total portfolio. A client request that conflicts with the IPS requires reasonable effort to reconcile, not mere execution.
- **Conflict-disclosure test (VI(A) vs VI(C) vs IV(B))**: VI(C) covers benefits paid or received for a recommendation of services; IV(B) covers outside compensation that competes with or conflicts with the employer; VI(A) covers any matter that could impair objectivity toward clients, prospects or employer, including employer-paid bonuses tied to client behavior.

## Key Concepts
- **Dual mandate**: a vehicle with socioeconomic plus return objectives must not promise return maximization; promising a set or maximal return contradicts the mandate (III(C)).
- **Front-running**: trading personally before a client or employer trade for advantage; VI(B) is violated even if clients are not hurt. Wait until the client or fund trade is complete.
- **Beneficial interest (VI(A))**: direct or indirect pecuniary interest or power to dispose; options and preferred shares in a parent give an interest in controlled subsidiaries.
- **Tie-in arrangement**: IPO allocation conditioned on aftermarket purchases; manipulation under II(B) and unfair under III(B).
- **Flipping restrictions for some clients only**: differential treatment of clients with the same IPO needs is a fair-dealing problem when it disadvantages some.
- **Leaving an employer (IV(A))**: free to prepare (lease space, register, ask colleagues to join) on own time; no soliciting employer clients, using employer materials or breaching a confidentiality request before departure; contact after departure is generally fine without a noncompete.
- **Misrepresenting who manages (I(C), V(B))**: stating that a departed or non-involved star runs the fund conceals the real process.
- **Selective information flow (III(B))**: acting for some accounts on analyst changes before public dissemination breaches fair dealing, even if the information is not material for the general public.
- **Record retention (V(C))**: keep notes that support recommendations; never destroy them at a client's request; hard copy or electronic both acceptable.
- **Client authority (I(D), III(A))**: signing or initialing for a client, or acting on instructions from a spouse without written authority, is misconduct or a breach of loyalty and care.
- **Misleading cause of an error (I(C), I(D))**: blaming a "computer error" for a human typo is dishonesty even if the corrected figure is provided.
- **CFA designation (VII(B))**: charterhood requires program completion, work experience and membership in good standing (dues, Professional Conduct Statement); a lapsed member cannot use the designation; candidates may not cite expected completion dates or imply a partial designation.
- **Exam confidentiality (VII(A))**: telling students the exam was harder than expected is fine; naming specific questions or topical areas tested or not tested is not.
- **Disclosure of referral benefits (VI(C))**: state the nature, basis and estimated amount of the benefit, before any agreement.

## Mental Models
- **"Disclosed somewhere is not disclosed to my client"**: Use when a conflict is in a prospectus, filing or firm policy. VI(A) wants prominent, plain-language disclosure to the people being advised.
- **"Ahead of the client"**: Use for personal or favored-account trades. If anyone trades before the clients (or before public release), look for VI(B) or III(B).
- **"Prepare off-hours, solicit after leaving"**: Use for departure cases. Anything on own time that does not use employer assets or contact employer clients before exit is generally allowed.
- **"IPS first, product second"**: Use whenever a recommendation comes before a profile, or the IPS is stale.
- **"Investigate, do not only escalate"**: Use for supervisor questions.
- **"Public-record test"**: Use for MNPI. Acting after widespread publication is fine; acting on rumors plus public news of insider sales is not MNPI.

## Worked Example
**Playbook: Graeme and the Delaneys (new-issue churn).**
Facts: In January 2017 the Delaneys, with $250,000 as most of their investable assets, a 15-20 year horizon and low risk tolerance, receive new-issue purchases equal to about three quarters of dollar purchases; no new issue is held longer than five months. Graeme earned $477,000 in new-issue commissions over 2016-2017; BOA earned $1,908,900 on the same deals (about 4.0 times Graeme's, `1,908,900 / 477,000 = 4.002`). Commissions are disclosed only if clients ask. Balmer talks to Graeme and reports to compliance but no assessment follows.
- Standards implicated: III(C) (unsuitable for low risk tolerance, not held to horizon), VI(A) (commission conflict not disclosed to all, prospectus and BOA policy irrelevant), IV(C) (supervisor).
- Violation analysis: a long horizon does not rescue the account because low risk tolerance, not horizon, drives suitability; high turnover also raises III(A). Disclosure in the issuer prospectus tells the client what the issuer pays the syndicate, not that Graeme's recommendation is paid per issue.
- Correct conduct: write an IPS with a risk limit, cap new issues, disclose new-issue compensation to every client in plain language, and have the supervisor investigate all of Graeme's accounts, not only the Delaneys.
Interpretation: the multi-account pattern (majority of Graeme's other accounts also bought new issues) is why a scope assessment is needed.

**Playbook: BankGlobal's early-trade habit.** Analysts phone Hockett's team about changes about 45 minutes before web publication; the team trades its discretionary accounts first. Standard III(B) requires every client a fair opportunity to act; non-discretionary and other-team clients lose. It is not MNPI under II(A) because the clients paying for the research are the audience. Fix: equal dissemination, block-trade allocation, or fix the IT bottleneck.

## Anti-patterns
- Choosing between projects on political grounds and calling it a development mandate.
- Promising a return level to a prospective investor.
- Co-investing personally ahead of one's own fund.
- Hiring locally connected "sub-advisers" with no documentation and expensing the payments.
- Using the CFA designation after dues lapse; remaining silent while a colleague misstates credentials.
- Listing "CFA, Level I" and an expected year on a business card.
- Excluding terminated accounts from composites.
- Making all clients buy the proprietary product because "we are shareholders".
- Rewarding analysts with deal-based bonuses and not disclosing them.
- Selling short a stock you rate Buy without disclosure.
- Tie-in IPO allocations and selective anti-flipping threats.
- Initialing or signing for clients; acting on a spouse's instruction without authority.
- Letting an alleged unwind ("allocation error") remove client interest: clients must not lose.
- Destroying notes because a client asked.
- Blaming a computer for a human mistake.

## Exam Focus & Traps
- Item-set structure: after the casebook there are 47 practice problems; same concepts reappear (Locke/NGM, Nano/Markoe, Jollie, Finnegan/PlusAccount, Vinken/Khadri, Omega, Riser, Danko/PIA).
- Locke: primary duty is to all plan beneficiaries (the ultimate clients), not trustees or the government. Doing nothing drastic while evaluating NGM is acceptable; after full evaluation, if the manager cannot show compliance, terminating is the best course (the source key chooses elimination).
- Trade corrections: suitability must be reviewed before allocation; clients wrongly allocated shares get interest on cash used; no client may lose interest due to the manager's error.
- Nano: a former unpaid intern may not solicit employer's clients before departure but may after; the Code and Standards apply to individuals, firms adopt the Asset Manager Code; decline supervisory responsibility in writing until adequate procedures exist; performance must include terminated accounts; one-size model portfolios fail suitability; conflict disclosure covers all conflicts, not only those tied to holdings; violations require prompt investigation, not just a warning; compensation arrangements need written employer consent, not mere discussion.
- Jollie: waiting for public release is enough; no need for the slowest channel. Thinly traded market order without manipulative intent is not II(B). Unattributed use of another's report, even with her permission, still needs attribution; citing the original academic study is sufficient. Legal does not equal ethical. Disclose conflicts prominently and in plain language regardless of the member's materiality judgment; disclose client gifts to the employer regardless of value.
- Finnegan/Klein: PlusAccount recommended where cost saving exists is fine; claiming "best qualified as a CFA charterholder" breaches VII(B); referral fee needs nature and estimated value; supervisor must enforce the annual review; delegating supervision is permitted.
- Vinken/Khadri: simulated plus actual results with labelling is fine; record retention in hard copy or electronic format is fine (source says Vinken keeps five years as regulators require; the Handbook recommends at least seven years absent a regulation, so check local rules; **⚠ Correction** for a real-world answer); partial GIPS compliance may not be claimed; opinions must be separated from facts; performance needs gross or net label; misattributed cause is misconduct and misrepresentation.
- Omega: net-basis trades for institutions with disclosure are fine; retail promised no markup but charged 0.01 per share violates I(C) and III(B); sharing squawk box over speakerphone breaches duties to clients and employer; listening is I(D), and acting on it is II(A).
- Riser: needs written employer consent for board pay; recommending a friend's firm needs conflict disclosure; resigning properly and keeping the board seat is fine; buying personally before the block purchase for clients may be front-running; holdings and board relationship are disclosed to clients, but the board pay is not a referral fee.
- Danko/PIA: candidates may say "Level III candidate" or "passed Level II" but not expected completion; trading for research is acceptable when best execution is established; FTI earnings projections are MNPI so do not use or disclose; buying on third-party "strong buy" without analysis breaches V(A); oversubscribed IPO excludes related-person accounts and fills clients first; blackout periods are the consistent procedure.

## Key Takeaways
1. Political, bonus and boss pressure are situational influences; the cure is process (governance, written procedures), not disclosure alone.
2. Personal trades behind client trades are VI(B) violations even when no harm is shown.
3. Prospectus disclosure never substitutes for VI(A) disclosure to the clients being advised.
4. Preparing to leave is allowed on own time; soliciting before leaving is not; telling the team after the board asked for confidentiality is not.
5. A supervisor must investigate scope, not merely escalate or accept assurances.
6. Suitability starts with an IPS that is current; client requests that contradict it need reasonable balancing, explanation and documentation.
7. Never sign for a client, never accept spouse instructions without authority, never destroy supporting records.
8. CFA references are narrow: no expected dates, no partial designations, no use after lapse; exam details stay confidential.

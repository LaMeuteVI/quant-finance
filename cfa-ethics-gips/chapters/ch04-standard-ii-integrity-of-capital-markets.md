# Chapter 4: Standard II, Integrity of Capital Markets (Material Nonpublic Information, Market Manipulation)
> **Source**: CFA Program Curriculum 2023, Level III, Reading 32 (Guidance for Standards I-VII; Standards of Practice Handbook, 11th ed.) | CFA Institute

## Core Idea
Standard II protects the belief that markets are not rigged. **II(A)**: if you hold **material nonpublic information (MNPI)** you must neither trade on it nor cause others to trade on it, across every instrument (equities, bonds, derivatives, funds, alternatives). **II(B)**: do not distort prices or fake volume, by false information or by deceptive transactions, with intent to mislead. Both rest on the same logic: when investors suspect insiders win, they stay out or demand higher risk premiums, which hurts capital allocation and the economy. Market integrity is also the one duty that can override client interests.

## Learning Outcomes Covered
- Apply II(A) and II(B) to fact patterns; judge materiality, public status, mosaic theory.
- Distinguish legitimate research and trading from MNPI use and manipulation.
- Recommend firewall, disclosure and compliance procedures.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **MNPI test (II(A)): Material AND Nonpublic**
  - *Material*: disclosure would probably move the price, or reasonable investors would want it before deciding (it would significantly alter the "total mix"). Drivers: specificity, difference from public information, nature, and **reliability of source**. Ambiguous price effect or speculative sources reduce materiality; time can make it immaterial.
  - *Nonpublic*: not yet disseminated to the marketplace in general. A small-group briefing, conference call to select analysts, or private social media group does not make it public. Reasonable expectation that the market has received it is enough; you need not wait for the slowest channel.
  - *If you conclude information is MNPI*: (1) make reasonable efforts to get public dissemination (ask the issuer); (2) if impossible, tell only designated supervisor or compliance, take no action, and do not change recommendations; (3) never induce insiders to leak.
  - *Exception*: information given legitimately for a defined purpose (due diligence for a merger, loan underwriting, rating, offering) may be used for that purpose only, never to trade.

- **Mosaic theory**: an analyst may act on conclusions drawn from public information plus nonmaterial nonpublic items, even if the conclusion would be material had the company said it outright. Analysts' work improves market efficiency. *Safeguard*: keep complete records of the research (V(C)) to demonstrate the mosaic. *Failure mode*: a single piece of company-sourced information that is itself material breaks the mosaic.

- **Firewall (information barrier)**: minimum elements: control of interdepartmental communication through a compliance or legal clearance area; **watch, restricted and rumor lists**; documented procedures and enforcement; heightened review or restriction of proprietary trading while holding MNPI; physical separation of departments and files; **no personnel overlap** (an employee is on one side only; an analyst who helps bankers is treated as banker until the information is public); a single authorized person deciding materiality and public status; personal trading limits and reporting (including family accounts); records and training.
  - *Restricted list* (visible, no trading or recommendations) vs. *watch list* (confidential, monitoring only): restricted lists leak signals if widely circulated, so use both.
  - Proprietary trading: do not blanket-prohibit market making (withdrawal would tip the market; make market makers passive, taking only the contra side of unsolicited trades); suspend **risk arbitrage** when the stock is on the watch list.

- **Two forms of manipulation (II(B))**
  1. **Information-based**: false rumors, "pump and dump", exaggerated or misleading projections to induce trading, manipulating model inputs to inflate ratings.
  2. **Transaction-based**: trades that create artificial price or volume or false appearance of liquidity; cornering a dominant position to exploit a derivative or its underlying.
  - **Intent** is critical; legitimate strategies exploiting inefficiencies are not covered.

## Key Concepts
- **Material information**: likely to affect a security's price or investor decisions; includes earnings, M&A, asset quality, product or regulatory events, contract wins or losses, management or auditor changes, security-related events (defaults, buybacks, splits, dividends), bankruptcy, legal disputes, government economic reports, **large orders before execution**, and rating or recommendation changes.
- **Nonpublic information**: not available to the market in general. Select-group disclosure leaves it nonpublic.
- **Selective disclosure**: company gives material information to a few analysts or investors first, creating insider-trading risk. Analysts must also watch for "guidance" on public filings.
- **Mosaic theory**: lawful aggregation of public and nonmaterial nonpublic facts into material conclusions.
- **Expert networks**: outside experts may be paid for insight, but you may not ask for or act on confidential information (e.g., preliminary trial results). Signed no-MNPI agreements do not replace your own materiality judgment.
- **Analyst reports as MNPI**: a well-known analyst's rating change can move prices but is the product of mosaic work. It need not be published to everyone, yet pre-publication leaks to outsiders (e.g., a journalist) are MNPI violations.
- **Cause others to act**: tipping or relaying MNPI breaches II(A) even if the recipient does not trade; family does not exempt.
- **Firewall / Chinese wall**: barrier restricting the flow of confidential information to those needing it.
- **Watch, restricted, rumor lists**: compliance tools to control personal and proprietary trading.
- **Pump and dump**: promote then sell into the inflated price.
- **Pump-priming (liquidity pumping)**: volume incentives at product launch; acceptable only if fully disclosed.
- **Artificial volatility**: timing negative or exaggerated reports (e.g., during an issuer's quiet period) to move prices for clients' benefit.
- **Model-input manipulation**: choosing optimistic scenarios to obtain ratings or support sales.

## Mental Models
- Use **"material AND nonpublic"** as a two-gate filter; if either gate is open (immaterial or public), the standard does not bite.
- Use **source reliability** to discount rumours: unnamed, unconnected or speculative sources rarely pass the materiality gate.
- Use **mosaic documentation** whenever a conclusion is bold: file the public and nonmaterial inputs.
- Use **look-over-the-wall via compliance** whenever a banker or analyst needs information from the other side.
- Use **intent plus effect** for II(B): ask whether the act creates a false picture of price, volume or liquidity, and whether the audience was misled.

## Worked Example
*MNPI decision tree (reconstructed).* An analyst on a covered stock attends a call with 12 investors where the CFO says quarterly revenue will miss guidance by 8%. The release is scheduled for next week; no press release exists.
- Gate 1: material? A guidance miss of that size almost certainly moves the price and comes from the CFO (reliable): yes.
- Gate 2: nonpublic? Only 12 investors heard it; not disseminated to the market: yes.
- Action: no trading and no change of recommendation; tell compliance; ask the company to issue a release; when it is public (after the release is distributed), act.
- No numbers to compute here; the lesson is process. Interpretation: waiting for public dissemination costs the client a day or two, which is preferable to a II(A) violation; the same logic applies if the analyst tells the PM, which would be "causing others to act."

## Anti-patterns
- Treating a small-group meeting or private social media group as "public".
- Assuming a signed expert-network agreement means you can trade.
- Letting one person sit on both sides of the firewall.
- Circulating the restricted list broadly (it signals the deal).
- Trading derivatives or funds "on the side" because the standard is thought to cover only stocks.
- Timing reports or sales messages to move prices.
- Using positive-only scenarios to get a better rating.
- Deleting research files, which removes proof of the mosaic.

## Exam Focus & Traps
- Standard covers **all instruments**, not only equities, and **tipping** ("cause others to act") even without a personal trade.
- Public dissemination is required; sharing with a handful of analysts is not enough; **no need to wait for the slowest delivery method**.
- **Materiality depends on source reliability and specificity**; rumours and whispers are often "noise".
- Mosaic theory permits conclusions from public and nonmaterial nonpublic data; document it (V(C)).
- Information legitimately received for due diligence (merger, underwriting, rating) can be used for that purpose only.
- Recommended steps once you hold MNPI: seek public dissemination, otherwise inform only supervisor and compliance; do not act; do not change recommendations.
- Firewalls: no personnel overlap, clearance via compliance, watch and restricted lists; a wholesale ban on proprietary trading is not required (market making continues but passive); risk arbitrage should be suspended.
- II(B): pump-priming is acceptable **if disclosed**; "intent" is key; legitimate inefficiency-based strategies are fine.
- Material information includes large unexecuted orders and rating or recommendation changes.
- Do not confuse II(B) price manipulation with III(D) or I(C) misrepresentation; the same fact pattern often hits several standards (e.g., V(A), VI(A), III(B)).

## Key Takeaways
1. Trade on public information and your own analysis, never on material nonpublic information, and do not tip.
2. Test information on two gates: materiality (price impact, source reliability) and public dissemination.
3. The mosaic theory protects diligent analysis; keep records.
4. Selective disclosure does not make information public; seek dissemination or stay passive.
5. Firewalls with clearance, lists, documentation and no personnel overlap prevent leakage.
6. Do not rely on expert-network paperwork; judge materiality yourself.
7. Market manipulation is either false information or deceptive trading; intent matters; disclose launch liquidity incentives.
8. Manipulated model inputs or timed reports for client gain are II(B) violations with knock-on breaches elsewhere.

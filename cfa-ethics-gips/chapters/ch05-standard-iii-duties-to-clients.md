# Chapter 5: Standard III, Duties to Clients (Loyalty/Prudence/Care, Fair Dealing, Suitability, Performance Presentation, Confidentiality)
> **Source**: CFA Program Curriculum 2023, Level III, Reading 32 (Guidance for Standards I-VII; Standards of Practice Handbook, 11th ed.) | CFA Institute

## Core Idea
Standard III is the client-protection core. **III(A)** sets one floor for every member regardless of job or law: loyalty, prudence and care, with client interests ahead of employer and self. **III(B)** forbids favouring clients in recommendations or trades (fair, not equal). **III(C)** makes advisers learn the client, write an IPS, and test every action against it in the total-portfolio context. **III(D)** requires fair, accurate, complete performance. **III(E)** keeps client information confidential unless illegality, law or consent says otherwise. Together they answer: who is the client, what do they need, am I treating them and their peers fairly, am I telling the truth about results, and am I protecting what they told me?

## Learning Outcomes Covered
- Apply III(A) to III(E) in fact patterns; identify the true client.
- Judge brokerage, allocation, suitability, performance and confidentiality conduct.
- Recommend policies: IPS, allocation, disclosure, GIPS, data security.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **III(A) Loyalty, Prudence and Care**
  - *Requirement*: act for the client's sole benefit as you believe best given known facts; apply the care you would use for your own affairs; follow the client's investment parameters; balance risk and return. Prudence = care, skill and diligence of a reasonable person in like capacity.
  - *Single floor*: applies even where no legal fiduciary duty exists; it does **not** by itself make everyone a fiduciary. Execution-only brokers owe best execution within instructions; blended advisers (limited product shelf) must disclose limits up front and recommend the best fit within them. Legal fiduciary duty (and custody, i.e., any direct or indirect access to client funds, which heightens responsibility) must be honored too under I(A).
  - *Who is the client?*: individuals: the person. Pension or trust: the **beneficiaries**, not the sponsor who hires you. Fund managed to an index or mandate: duty is to follow the mandate; individual fund investors are the responsibility of their own advisers. Research analysts and some corporate roles: the investing public or shareholders, so independence outranks organisational loyalty.
  - *Soft dollars*: paying higher commissions for goods or services that do not benefit the client breaches loyalty. **Directed brokerage** (client-instructed) is acceptable; still seek best price and execution, confirm benefit to beneficiaries, and disclose that best execution may be sacrificed. **Best execution** = trading process that maximises portfolio value within objectives and constraints (not simply the lowest commission).
  - *Proxy voting*: part of loyalty; vote informed, not blindly with management on non-routine governance; a cost-benefit decision not to vote is allowed; disclose the policy.
  - *Procedures*: quarterly itemised statements, disclose custody location and movements, segregate assets, "what would I demand as the client?", written client approval when in doubt, conflict and compensation disclosure, regular review, diversification unless guidelines say otherwise.

- **III(B) Fair Dealing** ("fairly", not "equally")
  - *Recommendations*: every client gets a fair opportunity to act; changes in recommendations matter more than initial ones (tell clients who acted on the old advice, and warn clients who place contrary orders before accepting them). Different service levels allowed if disclosed, available to all and not disadvantaging anyone. Selecting recipients by suitability and known interest, not favoured status.
  - *Dissemination controls*: limit people who know; shorten decision-to-release time (flash reports); no pre-dissemination discussion or trading; simultaneous distribution to branches and clients with employee trading restrictions until widely distributed; follow-up with individuals only afterwards; maintain client-holdings list.
  - *Investment action*: allocate new issues to all suitable clients per documented policy; if oversubscribed, **prorate** (round-lot basis; minimum lot sizes allowed); forgo your own and family orders unless family accounts are managed like regular fee-paying clients; same price and commission for block participants; partial fills pro rata; time-stamp orders; allocate by client before execution; disclose allocation procedures (disclosure never cures an unfair policy). Never withhold "hot issues" for yourself or use them as rewards.
  - *Account review*: supervisors compare accounts to detect favoritism (document why one account buys while another sells).

- **III(C) Suitability** (advisory relationships; fund managers follow the mandate)
  1. Reasonable inquiry into **experience, risk and return objectives, financial constraints** before advising; reassess regularly (at least annually and before material changes).
  2. Determine suitability against **written** objectives, mandates and constraints.
  3. Judge in the **total portfolio** context (not security by security).
  - *IPS contents*: client identification (type, beneficiaries, portion of assets managed); objectives (return, risk tolerance); constraints (liquidity, cash flows, investable funds, horizon, taxes, legal/regulatory, preferences and unique needs, proxy voting); benchmarks; roles; review schedule.
  - *Suitability test*: look beyond return: effect on diversification, risk vs. assessed tolerance, fit with strategy. Leverage and illiquidity in derivatives and synthetic products bear directly on suitability.
  - *Unsolicited unsuitable requests*: refrain until you discuss; small impact: educate and get written acknowledgement per firm policy; material impact: update the IPS; client insists without change: consider an unmanaged account or ending the relationship.
  - *Mandate managers* (index, style, income fund) owe consistency with the stated mandate; they do not judge fund suitability for end investors. Information limits: suitability rests on information the client provides (clients may withhold other assets).

- **III(D) Performance Presentation**: fair, accurate, complete; applies to accounts, composites, pooled funds and analysts' recommendation track records; never imply clients will earn a past return. Best method: **GIPS compliance** (claims must meet all requirements). Without GIPS: consider audience sophistication; use weighted composites of similar portfolios (not one showcase account); include terminated accounts and show termination dates; disclose simulated or model results, prior-firm records and role, gross/net of fees, after-tax; keep supporting data; brief presentations must note limits and provide detail on request.

- **III(E) Confidentiality**: protects current, former and prospective clients; applies to information received because of the special relationship. Exceptions: (1) client illegal activity, (2) legally required disclosure, (3) client permission. If law requires secrecy, do not disclose even illegal activity. Share inside the firm only with colleagues working for that client. Cooperating with a CFA Institute PCP investigation does not breach it. Follow firm electronic-security policy; train all staff; discuss secure channels with clients.

## Key Concepts
- **Duty of loyalty**: place the client first and avoid or disclose conflicts.
- **Prudence**: reasonable care, skill and diligence; staying within the client's parameters.
- **Custody**: any direct or indirect access to client funds; triggers heightened duties.
- **Soft dollars / soft commissions**: client commissions used to buy research or services; allowed only if it benefits the client and is reasonable.
- **Directed brokerage**: client-instructed use of a broker; manager still seeks best price and execution where possible and discloses.
- **Best execution**: trading process maximising client value within constraints.
- **Hot issue**: oversubscribed offering trading at a premium once it opens.
- **Pro rata allocation**: distribution in proportion to order size (subject to minimum lots).
- **IPS (investment policy statement)**: written statement of client objectives, constraints and roles; basis for suitability.
- **Unsolicited trade request**: client-initiated trade possibly inconsistent with the IPS.
- **Composite**: aggregation of portfolios with a similar mandate for performance presentation.
- **Cherry picking / simulated results**: selective accounts or hypothetical returns presented without disclosure.
- **Agency cross transaction**: adviser acts as broker for both sides; needs disclosure and advance client consent.

## Mental Models
- Use **"who is the client?"** first, then apply loyalty; for plans and trusts, answer is the beneficiary.
- Use **"fair, not equal"** to judge differences in service: allowed if disclosed, available to all, and costless to others; timing of access is not allowed.
- Use **IPS-as-contract**: every recommendation must trace to a written objective or constraint; if the request does not, update the IPS or decline.
- Use **"would I want this as the client?"** when uncertain; if doubt remains, disclose in writing and get approval.
- Use **allocate before you trade** to remove after-the-fact cherry picking of winners.

## Worked Example
*Oversubscribed bond allocation (Example B7, recomputed).*
- Inputs: requests of 10,000, 10,000, 10,000, 50,000, 50,000 (total 130,000); allocation received 55,000; minimum lot 5,000; firm avoids odd lots.
- Pure pro rata fraction = 55,000 / 130,000 = 42.31%, giving 4,231 to each small account (below the minimum lot) and 21,154 to each large account.
- Adjust: give each small account the 5,000 minimum (15,000 total); remaining 40,000 split equally between the two equal-sized large requests = 20,000 each; total 15,000 + 40,000 = 55,000.
- Interpretation: deviation from strict pro rata is justified by liquidity (can resell in lots), is applied systematically, and no client is singled out, so III(B) is met. Document the policy and disclose it.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating the person who hired you (plan sponsor) as the client instead of the beneficiaries.
- Soft-dollar arrangements that pay your overhead or perks.
- Churning to reach commission targets or to fund research.
- Allocating after the fact (cherry picking winners to a favored account).
- Pre-briefing favoured clients or tweeting recommendations early.
- Selling a product because the fee is low or the firm wants flow, without an IPS fit.
- Never updating the IPS after life or market changes.
- Showing one best account, hiding terminated accounts, or unlabeled simulated results.
- Discussing client details to help a charity or to answer a curious third party.

## Exam Focus & Traps
- III(A) is the minimum for all members; it **does not impose fiduciary status** on execution-only roles. If law imposes fiduciary duty, follow it (stricter rule).
- Pension client = **beneficiaries**; fund investors are the clients of their own advisers, not the fund manager.
- **Directed brokerage** is acceptable; **soft dollars** must benefit the client; best price and execution still apply.
- "Fairly" is not "equally"; premium service is allowed if disclosed and available to all; disclosure of an unfair allocation policy does not cure it.
- Changed recommendations: tell clients who acted on the old one, and before accepting contrary orders.
- Family-member accounts: excluded from oversubscribed new issues **only if** not managed like regular fee-paying accounts.
- Suitability is judged on the **total portfolio**; IPS updated at least annually and on material changes; unsolicited unsuitable trades need discussion, acknowledgement, possibly IPS update or an unmanaged account.
- Mandate managers do not assess suitability for the end investor.
- III(D): GIPS is the best way; claiming GIPS without full compliance is a violation; terminated accounts must be included; label simulated, gross or net, prior-firm results.
- III(E): exceptions are illegality, legal requirement, client permission; **PCP cooperation is allowed**; confidentiality survives the end of the relationship.
- A suitability determination does not guarantee against losses.

## Key Takeaways
1. Identify the real client first; loyalty and prudence run to them in every role.
2. Control brokerage conflicts: best execution, no overhead-funding soft dollars, disclose direction.
3. Fair dealing means equal opportunity to act on recommendations and pro rata, pre-set allocation of trades.
4. Build and maintain a written IPS; test every action in total-portfolio context and refresh at least annually.
5. Push back on unsuitable client requests with education, documentation and, if needed, structural alternatives.
6. Present performance fairly: composites, terminated accounts, labels, GIPS, consistent methods.
7. Keep client information confidential except for illegality, legal requirement or consent, and secure electronic channels.
8. Compensation and sales pressures are classic triggers of III(A) and III(C) breaches; disclose conflicts and resist.

# Chapter 12: Global Investment Performance Standards (GIPS)
> **Source**: CFA Program Curriculum 2023, Level III, Reading 35 (Overview of the GIPS Standards, 2020 edition; Lawton) combined with CFA Program Curriculum 2020, Level I, Readings 4 and 5 (Introduction to GIPS; GIPS 2010 provisions) · Ethical and Professional Standards

## Core Idea
GIPS are voluntary, self-regulated ethical standards for how an investment firm calculates and presents historical performance to prospective clients and investors. The guiding principles are fair representation and full disclosure. The mechanism is: define the firm, put every actual, fee-paying, discretionary portfolio into a composite defined by mandate, calculate time-weighted returns that neutralise client cash flows, report at least 5 (building to 10) years of standardised statistics, and, optionally, have an independent verifier test firm-wide processes. Compliance is all-or-nothing and firm-wide.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Eight sections of the 2020 GIPS Standards for Firms**: 1 Fundamentals of Compliance; 2 Input Data and Calculation Methodology; 3 Composite and Pooled Fund Maintenance; 4 Composite Time-Weighted Return Report; 5 Composite Money-Weighted Return Report; 6 Pooled Fund TWR Report; 7 Pooled Fund MWR Report; 8 Advertising Guidelines. The 2020 edition also has separate chapters for Asset Owners and Verifiers. The 2010 edition (still printed in L1) had nine sections: Fundamentals, Input Data, Calculation Methodology, Composite Construction, Disclosure, Presentation and Reporting, Real Estate, Private Equity, Wrap Fee/SMA.
- **Why GIPS exist: abuses they prevent**: representative accounts (showing the best portfolio), survivorship bias (excluding terminated accounts), varying time periods, partial-period annualisation, simulated returns shown as actual, hand-picked benchmarks, unsupported valuations.
- **Who can claim**: only firms (or asset owners under their own chapter) that actually manage assets on a discretionary basis and compete for business. Consultants, software vendors and custodians cannot be "compliant". Plan sponsors may claim only if they actually manage assets. A firm may claim compliance only as a whole; claims for a single product or composite are prohibited, and so are statements of partial compliance or that a calculation method is "in accordance with" GIPS.
- **Definition of the firm**: an investment firm, subsidiary or division held out to the public as a distinct business entity, organizationally and functionally segregated, with discretion and autonomy over investment decisions. Best practice: the broadest, most meaningful definition. Total firm assets = fair value of all discretionary and non-discretionary assets (fee-paying or not), including sub-advised assets the firm selects; excluding advisory-only assets and uncalled committed capital. Organizational change must not alter historical composite performance.
- **Definition of discretion**: a portfolio is discretionary if the manager can implement the intended strategy. The firm needs a written definition applied consistently; client restrictions (ESG screens, no-sale of legacy holdings, no derivatives, pre-approval of trades, scheduled large withdrawals) make a portfolio non-discretionary only if materially preventing the strategy.
- **Composite construction rules**: all actual, fee-paying, discretionary segregated accounts in at least one composite; pooled funds included if they meet a composite definition (a strategy offered only as a pooled fund needs no composite); non-discretionary and simulated or model portfolios never included; non-fee-paying discretionary accounts may be included with disclosure; composites defined ex ante by mandate, objective or strategy (hierarchy: mandate, asset class, style or strategy, benchmark, risk-return) with definition changes never retroactive.
- **Portfolio movements**: new portfolios added on a timely, consistent basis (policy often next full period after funding, or after substantially invested); terminated portfolios stay in history through the last full period under discretion (if notified 20 May of termination and told to stop trading, include through 30 April); switches only for documented mandate change or composite redefinition, history stays in the original composite; significant cash flows may remove a portfolio temporarily (defined ex ante per composite) or be handled with a temporary account; minimum asset levels apply prospectively and may use different add and remove thresholds (for example add at 1 million, remove below 900,000).
- **Valuation hierarchy (recommended)**: use objective, observable, unadjusted quoted prices for identical investments in active markets; else (1) quoted prices for similar investments in active markets; (2) quoted prices for identical or similar investments in inactive markets; (3) other observable market-based inputs; (4) subjective unobservable inputs. Fair value includes accrued income; hierarchy should be composite-specific; disclose if materially different (2010 text) and policies available upon request.
- **Portability tests (all must hold, composite by composite)**: (1) substantially all decision makers are at the new firm; (2) decision process substantially intact and independent; (3) records support the performance; (4) no break in the track record. If tests 1-3 pass but there is a break, the past record may be shown but not linked. One-year grace period to bring acquired non-compliant assets into compliance.
- **Verification**: voluntary, by an independent qualified third party; firm-wide only, never a single composite; tests whether composite and pooled fund policies and procedures are designed in compliance and implemented firm-wide; does not certify any specific report or internal controls. Verifier samples portfolios (numbers of composites, portfolios, type, total assets, controls, years, systems, external measurer), recomputes returns, checks discretion classification, timing of inclusion and exclusion, switches, GIPS Reports and ads. A performance examination of a specific composite is optional and requires verification. Firms may say a composite was "examined" only if a report was issued.

## Key Concepts
- **Composite**: aggregation of portfolios managed to a similar mandate, objective or strategy; its return is the asset-weighted average of member portfolio returns; the central anti-cherry-picking device.
- **Segregated account (SMA)** vs **pooled fund**: broad distribution (regulated, publicly offered, e.g. mutual funds, UCITS) vs limited distribution (private equity, hedge funds).
- **GIPS Report**: the required performance presentation (Composite Report or Pooled Fund Report); must be given to all prospective clients or limited-distribution investors, representing the strategy marketed.
- **External cash flow**: client-initiated contribution or withdrawal; **large cash flow**: firm-defined (absolute amount or percentage of assets) per composite; revalue and break the period at that date; policy must be applied without exception.
- **Time-weighted return (TWR)**: removes the impact of external cash flows, so it measures manager skill; required for all but narrow MWR cases; sub-period returns must be geometrically linked.
- **Money-weighted return (MWR)**: internal rate of return including client cash flows; permitted only where the firm controls external cash flows and portfolios are closed-end, fixed life, fixed commitment, or illiquid investments are significant; annualised since-inception MWR at least annually; daily external cash flows for periods from the 2020 effective date.
- **Modified Dietz**: day-weighted approximation of TWR when no revaluation at the cash flow date; **Modified IRR** gives the same estimate numerically.
- **Transaction costs**: commissions, exchange fees and taxes, bid-offer spreads; custody fees charged per transaction are custody fees, not transaction costs; returns are after transaction costs; if bundled and not separable, gross-of-fee composites deduct the entire bundled fee or its transaction-cost part.
- **Cash and equivalents**: returns must be included even when another firm invests the cash.
- **Annualisation**: periods under one year are never annualised.
- **Internal dispersion**: spread of full-year portfolio returns in a composite: high/low, range, equal-weighted or asset-weighted standard deviation; required when six or more portfolios held the full year (not required for five or fewer).
- **Three-year ex post standard deviation**: of monthly composite and benchmark returns, annualised, at each year end where monthly returns are available.
- **Minimum asset level**: composite-specific threshold below which portfolios are excluded.
- **Compliance statement**: used only inside a GIPS Report; sample (2020 style): the firm claims compliance with GIPS and has prepared and presented the report in compliance; independently verified for stated periods; verification does not provide assurance on any specific report.

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- Single period, no flows: `r = (V1 - V0) / V0`
- True TWR with revaluation: `r_TWR = (1 + r_1) × (1 + r_2) × ... × (1 + r_n) - 1`, where each `r_k = (V_end,k - V_start,k) / V_start,k` with `V` measured before and after each large flow.
- Modified Dietz: `r = (V1 - V0 - CF) / (V0 + Σ CF_i × w_i)`, `w_i = (CD - D_i) / CD` (flows at end of day); beginning-of-day convention: `w_i = (CD - D_i + 1) / CD`. `CF = ΣCF_i`.
- Modified IRR: solve `V1 = V0 (1 + r) + Σ CF_i (1 + r)^{w_i}`.
- MWR: solve `Σ CF_t / (1 + r)^t = 0` with beginning value and contributions as outflows and ending value as inflow.
- Composite return (beginning assets): `r_C = Σ r_i × (V0,i / ΣV0,j)`; (beginning assets plus weighted flows): `r_C = Σ r_i × (V_p,i / ΣV_p,j)`, `V_p = V0 + Σ CF × w`; Aggregate Return method: apply Modified Dietz to the whole composite.
- Dispersion: equal-weighted `s = sqrt(Σ(r_i - r̄)^2 / n)`; asset-weighted `s = sqrt(Σ w_i (r_i - r_proxy)^2)` with `r_proxy = Σ w_i r_i`.
- Net-of-fee model fee: monthly gross return minus one-twelfth of the highest annual fee (1.00% / 12 = 0.083%).

Requirement ledger:

| Topic | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Valuation | Fair value; at least monthly (private market quarterly); on date of every large cash flow; calendar month end |
| Returns | Total return; TWR with geometric linking; sub-period return at large cash flows; cash included; after transaction costs |
| Composite return | At least monthly asset-weighting (private market excepted) |
| History | At least 5 years annual (or since inception), building to 10; extend annually |
| Report items | Composite and benchmark annual returns for all years; number of portfolios if 6 or more; composite assets; total firm assets (or percentage); dispersion; 3-year ex post standard deviation |
| Benchmark | Total-return benchmark reflecting the strategy; description and rationale disclosed |
| Conflicts with law | Follow the law, disclose the conflict |
| Distribution | Reasonable efforts to give every prospect a GIPS Report; list of composites and pooled funds available on request |

2010 vs 2020 differences to know: 2010 had nine sections (real estate, private equity, wrap fee separate) and a "verified or not verified" statement pair; 2010 forbade estimated trading expenses while 2020 allows estimates only if actual costs are unknown; 2020 splits report types by TWR and MWR and by composite and pooled fund; 2020 adds asset-owner and verifier chapters; both require 5-to-10 years and no partial compliance. The 2010 Advertising Guidelines require the compliance statement without a verification clause, the firm definition, how to obtain a GIPS-compliant presentation, composite description, annualised 1-, 3-, 5-year returns (or since inception) or period-to-date plus annual returns, gross/net identification, benchmark, currency and leverage disclosure.

## Mental Models
- **"Composite is a promise made before the results"**: Use when asked whether a portfolio may be included or excluded. Inclusion rules are ex ante and mechanical; hindsight selection is the abuse GIPS prevent.
- **"TWR for skill, MWR for control"**: Use TWR when the client moves the money; use MWR only when the firm, not the client, controls the timing and assets are illiquid or closed-end.
- **"Reval on big flows"**: Use whenever a flow exceeds the firm's definition; Modified Dietz is only for small flows or when daily weighting is allowed.
- **"All or nothing"**: Use for any claim of compliance. No partial, no per-product, no "calculated in accordance".
- **"Ported record test"**: Use for acquisitions or hires: people, process, records, continuity.
- **"Verification is about the firm, not the number"**: Use for any statement that a composite is verified (it cannot be).

## Worked Example
**TWR vs MWR with a large external cash flow (recomputed).**
Inputs: portfolio starts the year at 1,000,000. On 30 June, value before the flow is 1,100,000; the client contributes 500,000 (50% of beginning value, a large flow under a 10% policy), so the portfolio restarts at 1,600,000. Value at 31 December is 1,725,000.
- Sub-period 1: `1,100,000 / 1,000,000 - 1 = 10.00%`.
- Sub-period 2: `1,725,000 / 1,600,000 - 1 = 7.8125%`.
- TWR: `1.10 × 1.078125 - 1 = 18.59%`.
- MWR (IRR with outflows 1,000,000 at t = 0 and 500,000 at t = 0.5, inflow 1,725,000 at t = 1): about `18.15%`; Modified Dietz estimate with `w = 0.5` gives `(1,725,000 - 1,000,000 - 500,000) / (1,000,000 + 250,000) = 18.00%`.
Interpretation: the manager earned 18.59% on the dollars actually under their control; the investor's IRR is lower because 500,000 only worked for half the year in a lower-return second half. If the contribution had come after a 10% loss and before a 10% gain, TWR would be -1.00% but IRR +3.21%; if it came after a 10% gain and before a 10% loss, TWR is still -1.00% but IRR is -4.79%. TWR is identical in all three timings of the same manager returns; MWR varies with timing, which is why GIPS require TWR where the client controls flows.

**Source reference case (30 June month).** Start 100,000 on 31 May; 109,000 on 5 June after a 10,000 contribution; 110,550 on 30 June. True TWR: `(99,000 / 100,000) × (110,550 / 109,000) - 1 = 0.99 × 1.01422 - 1 = 0.41%`. Modified Dietz: `(110,550 - 100,000 - 10,000) / (100,000 + 10,000 × 25/30) = 550 / 108,333 = 0.51%`, a 10 bp overstatement that grows in volatile markets (volatile market example with 10,000 flow on 15 June after a 10% fall and before a 20% rise: true TWR 8.00% vs Modified Dietz 9.52%).

**Composite of four portfolios (June).** Portfolio returns by Modified Dietz: A 0.51%, B 0.28%, C 0.32%, D 1.36%; beginning weights 23.00, 22.40, 25.97, 28.63 percent give `0.65%`; weights using beginning assets plus weighted flows (24.56, 24.41, 25.61, 25.42 percent) give `0.62%`; aggregate method on total beginning 434.81, flows -8.00 (net), weighted denominator 440.97 and ending 429.55 also gives `0.62%`.

**Source consistency note.** In the printed derivation of the true TWR, sub-period 1 is shown as `0.01`; the correct sub-period return is `-1.00%` (99,000 / 100,000 - 1). **⚠ Correction**: the printed result 0.41% is right.

## Anti-patterns
- Claiming compliance for one composite or "except for private equity".
- Describing a calculation as "consistent with GIPS".
- Including only portfolios that did well (representative accounts) or dropping terminated portfolios.
- Moving a portfolio between composites to lift a composite's return and moving its history with it.
- Adding backtested or model returns to a live record.
- Annualising a period shorter than one year.
- Omitting cash and cash equivalents from returns or ignoring per-trade costs.
- Using Modified Dietz around a huge flow without revaluation when policy requires it.
- Hiding poor early years by starting a presentation later than the required five-to-ten-year window.
- Calling a composite "verified" or treating verification as accuracy assurance.
- Using a benchmark chosen after the fact.
- Treating firm-defined "large cash flow" as optional in calm markets.

## Exam Focus & Traps
- Verification is recommended, not required; it is firm-wide; no assurance on any single report or on internal controls.
- Minimum history: a 12-year-old firm must show 10 years; a firm initially claiming compliance shows at least 5 years; the record is extended annually until 10 years (Macondo case: cannot drop years before 2015 and must show 2010 to 2019).
- Number of portfolios must be shown only if six or more; dispersion required only if six or more for the full year (the 2010 text words this as "five or fewer not required").
- A composite may consist of a single portfolio; it must have no minimum number; non-fee-paying discretionary accounts may be included; non-discretionary must not.
- An account meeting two composite definitions goes into both.
- Terminated portfolios: history stays; last full period under discretion.
- Minimum asset level: portfolio removed prospectively when below; history stays; "miscellaneous" composites are not allowed.
- Client pre-approval of all trades or a large scheduled withdrawal program likely makes a portfolio non-discretionary (answer to SMERF); a ban on derivatives is irrelevant if the strategy does not use them; a ban on selling gains is more likely to impair discretion than credit-quality or developed-market constraints.
- Total firm assets include non-discretionary assets but not advisory-only assets.
- Real estate and private equity fall under their own provisions in the 2010 text, not in L3 scope.
- GIPS conflicts with law: obey the law and disclose.
- Past-firm performance portability needs all four tests to link; break in track record means present but do not link.
- Practice items (Level III problem 4, revaluing when flows exceed 5%): January 4.00%, February `(1.0433)(1.0233) - 1 = 6.76%`, March `(1.0266)(1.0208) - 1 = 4.80%`, quarter `(1.04)(1.0676)(1.048) - 1 = 16.36%`. Level III problem 5: Modified Dietz August returns A 3.65%, B 1.92%, C 2.58%; composite 2.57% (beginning assets), 2.61% (beginning assets plus weighted flows and aggregate method).
- Standard deviation example: 14 portfolios, equal-weighted mean 2.32%, standard deviation about 0.22% (population form); asset-weighted 0.21%; range 0.73%.
- Edition trap: the Level I text (2010) says returns must use actual trading expenses, never estimates; the Level III text (2020) allows estimated transaction costs only when actual costs are unknown. Per-trade custody fees are custody fees, not transaction costs, in both. Answer according to the edition in the question.

## Key Takeaways
1. GIPS are voluntary ethical standards for performance presentation based on fair representation and full disclosure; compliance is firm-wide and all-or-nothing.
2. Composites (ex ante, mandate-based, all actual fee-paying discretionary accounts) are the anti-cherry-picking mechanism.
3. TWR strips out client cash flows; large flows trigger revaluation and geometric linking; MWR only for controlled-flow, closed-end or illiquid strategies.
4. Returns include cash, are after transaction costs, are never annualised under a year, and use fair value with a recommended hierarchy.
5. A GIPS Composite Report shows 5 growing to 10 years of annual composite and benchmark returns, portfolio counts, assets, dispersion, and 3-year ex post standard deviation.
6. Portfolio inclusion, exclusion and switching follow written, consistent policies; history never moves.
7. Past-firm performance can be linked only if people, process, records and continuity are intact.
8. Verification adds credibility to the firm-wide claim, is independent, and is not an accuracy guarantee.

# Chapter 8: Standards VI and VII, Conflicts of Interest and Responsibilities as a CFA Institute Member or Candidate
> **Source**: CFA Program Curriculum 2023, Level III, Reading 32 (Guidance for Standards I-VII; Standards of Practice Handbook, 11th ed.) | CFA Institute

## Core Idea
Standard VI is about **conflicts**: disclose anything that could impair independence or objectivity (**VI(A)**), put client and employer trades ahead of your own (**VI(B)**), and disclose any payment or benefit given or received for referrals (**VI(C)**). Standard VII is about **the CFA brand**: do not compromise the integrity of CFA Institute programs or exams (**VII(A)**) and do not overstate what membership, the charter or candidacy means (**VII(B)**). The recurring remedy for conflicts is disclosure that is prominent, in plain language, and timely; the remedy for personal trading is priority to clients plus procedures (preclearance, blackout, reporting). Avoiding the conflict, or its appearance, is the best practice; disclosure is the fallback when it cannot be avoided.

- Interpret VI(A) to VI(C) and VII(A) to VII(B) in fact patterns (violation or not, and why).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts**
  - *Requirement*: full and fair disclosure of all matters that could reasonably be expected to impair independence and objectivity or interfere with duties to clients, prospects and employer. Disclosure must be **prominent, in plain language, and communicate effectively**.
  - *Audiences*: employer (enough information to judge the impact; report promptly, including inadvertent conflicts), clients and prospects (so they judge motives and bias themselves).
  - *Always-disclose list*: issuer relationships (directorship, consultancy, investment banking, underwriting, financial ties), broker/dealer market-making (signals trades against the firm's principal account), material beneficial ownership of the stock, nonstandard fee arrangements and subadvisory agreements, fee rebates (for example mutual fund service fees), and outside compensation.
  - *Beneficial ownership* (for VI(A)): a direct or indirect pecuniary interest, or the power to vote, direct the voting of, dispose of or direct the disposition of the security.
  - *Stock ownership*: sell-side disclose any materially beneficial ownership in a recommended security; buy-side disclose their procedures for personal-transaction reporting. A blanket ban on owning stocks is overly burdensome.
  - *Cross-departmental conflicts*: banking, underwriting, dealing or marketing pressure on analysts; broker-sponsored venture partnerships promoted in the secondary market. Resolve or disclose.
  - *Directors*: three conflicts (duties to clients vs. shareholders; receipt of company securities or options as pay; access to material nonpublic information). Isolate the director from investment decisions by firewalls.
  - *Updating*: update disclosure when a conflict worsens materially (for example, bonuses moving from annual to quarterly profits); err on the side of caution.
  - *Procedures*: disclose special compensation that may conflict with client interests (short-term bonuses, commissions, incentive and performance fees, referral fees). If the firm bars the disclosure, document the request and consider dissociating. Disclose performance fees to clients. Footnote outstanding agent options (amount and expiry) in research.
  - *Appearance counts*: employers may ban personal trading or board seats to avoid the appearance of a conflict; comply.

- **VI(B) Priority of Transactions**
  - *Rule*: client and employer transactions come before transactions in which you are the beneficial owner. Personal accounts include own, family (spouse, children, other immediate family), and any account with a direct or indirect pecuniary interest (trust, retirement account).
  - *Three-part test for acceptable personal trading*: (1) the client is not disadvantaged, (2) you do not profit personally from trades done for clients, (3) you comply with applicable rules. The same test applies to a forced personal sale (tuition, house deposit, margin call) that runs against your client advice.
  - Co-investing alongside clients is not itself a conflict; personal positions must never adversely affect clients.
  - *Nonpublic information*: do not convey pending client trades or research recommendations before release to anyone whose accounts make you a beneficial owner; do not convey material nonpublic information to anyone.
  - *Family client accounts* get no special treatment, good or bad. If you hold beneficial ownership you may still face preclearance and reporting.
  - *Procedures* (the personal-investing policy toolkit):

| Tool | What it does | Key detail |
|---|---|---|
| Limited participation in IPOs | Stops taking scarce allocations from clients; avoids favors that buy influence | Best: do not participate; at minimum preclear; do not benefit from clients' market position (preferred trading, oversubscription) |
| Restrictions on private placements | Same loyalty and influence concerns; incentive to push the deal later | Strict limits plus supervisory review; no favors or gifts that could sway judgment |
| Blackout / restricted periods | Prevents front-running (own trade before client trade) | At minimum everyone in the decision process; in a large firm a blackout is near a trading ban, in a small firm it prevents front-running |
| Reporting | Enforcement | Disclose holdings at hire and at least annually (confidentially), duplicate confirmations and statements, preclearance of planned trades |
| Disclosure of policies | Reassures clients | Must be informative, not boilerplate |

- **VI(C) Referral Fees**
  - *Rule*: disclose to employer, clients and prospects, as appropriate, any compensation, consideration or benefit received from or paid to others for recommending products or services (payer **and** recipient).
  - *Timing*: before entering any formal agreement for services, ideally in writing at the time of referral.
  - *Content*: nature (flat or percentage, one-time or continuing, performance-based, soft dollars or other non-cash benefit like research) and the **estimated dollar value**. All consideration counts, cash, soft dollars or in kind.
  - *Why*: clients can assess (1) partiality in the recommendation and (2) the full cost of the service.
  - *Scope*: no distinction between third-party payments and internal payments between departments or subsidiaries of the same firm.
  - *Procedures*: the firm may prohibit referral fees or define an approval process; notify clients of approved programs; professionals report compensation received to the employer at least quarterly.

- **VII(A) Conduct as Participants in CFA Institute Programs**
  - *Rule*: no conduct that compromises the reputation or integrity of CFA Institute or the CFA designation, or the integrity, validity or security of CFA Institute programs (CFA, CIPM, Investment Foundations).
  - *Covered conduct*: giving or receiving help on exams; breaking testing rules (calculator, belongings, Candidate Pledge); disclosing confidential exam or program information; circumventing security; misusing the CFA association for personal or professional gain; misrepresenting information on the Professional Conduct Statement or continuing education records.
  - *Confidential until released by CFA Institute*: specific question details, **and** broad topic areas or formulas tested or not tested. Discussing nonconfidential curriculum with study groups is fine.
  - *Volunteers* (exam development, administration, grading, committees): do not disclose questions, deliberations or scoring information, nor use the volunteer role as a business advantage.
  - *Opinions*: free to criticize CFA Institute policy, procedures or advocacy; never include exam-specific content in doing so.

- **VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute, the CFA Designation and the CFA Program**
  - *Rule*: do not misrepresent or exaggerate the meaning or implications of membership, the charter or candidacy, in any medium (cards, bios, letterhead, ads, resumes, oral remarks).
  - *Allowed*: factual statements, accurate descriptions of the requirements, praise of the Program's rigor and commitment to ethics, and relative-merit claims clearly stated as the speaker's opinion.
  - *Not allowed*: implying or promising superior performance or competence from the charter, or from passing quickly.
  - *Membership*: remit the annual Professional Conduct Statement and pay dues; if either lapses the member is inactive, must not present as active (may say "formerly"), and loses the right to the designation. Retired status exists through a CFA Institute process.
  - *Candidacy*: a candidate is an accepted registrant enrolled for a specified exam, or who has sat and awaits results. Say "candidate in the CFA Program". Never imply a partial designation, cite an expected date of completion, or write "CFA Level II" or "Charter Pending".
  - *Online*: do not attach the designation to a pseudonym; use the full name when citing the designation.
  - *Multiple designations*: order is personal preference, separated by commas.
  - *Procedures*: circulate VII(B) guidance to legal, compliance, PR and marketing; use compliance-approved templates.

## Key Concepts
- **Conflict of interest**: interests of the client, employer and professional (or firm) that diverge; best avoided, otherwise disclosed.
- **Full and fair disclosure**: prominent, plain-language and effective communication to let the audience evaluate bias.
- **Beneficial ownership**: pecuniary interest, voting power or disposition power in a security or account.
- **Agent options**: rights received as compensation for corporate finance work; amount and expiry must be disclosed in research.
- **Performance fee**: fee tied to capital appreciation; disclose to clients and the employer where relevant.
- **Front-running**: trading a personal (or firm) account ahead of client orders using knowledge of them.
- **Preclearance**: supervisor review of planned personal trades before execution.
- **Blackout / restricted period**: time window around client trades or recommendations when insiders may not trade.
- **Duplicate confirmations**: brokers send trade confirmations and statements directly to the firm to verify personal trading.
- **Referral fee**: any compensation, consideration or benefit paid or received for recommending a service.
- **Soft dollars**: non-cash benefits (for example research) funded by client commissions; they count as consideration under VI(C).
- **Candidate Pledge**: candidate commitment not to give, receive or disclose exam content and to follow program rules.
- **Professional Conduct Statement (PCS)**: annual member attestation; lapse removes the right to the designation.
- **CFA Institute member vs. charterholder vs. candidate**: three statuses with different permitted wording.
- **Partial designation**: any implication that passing levels earns a title, prohibited.

## Mental Models
- Use the **disclosure test** when a relationship, holding or payment exists: could a reasonable client or the employer read it as bias? If yes, disclose; if the conflict can be removed (reassign, recuse), do that first.
- Use **"clients first, firm second, me last"** for any trade timing question; delay personal trades until clients have had a chance to act.
- Use **"family accounts are just accounts"**: never favor or short-change a family client; apply beneficial-ownership rules only for preclearance and reporting.
- Use the **referral triangle** (who pays, who gets paid, who is told) for VI(C): both payer and recipient disclose, before engagement, with value.
- Use **"facts yes, promises no"** for VII(B) and **"opinions yes, exam content never"** for VII(A).

## Worked Example
*Quantifying a VI(C) referral disclosure (numbers from the Handbook case, reorganized).* Lewis Brothers, an investment counselor, receives referrals from Brady Securities. In return it supplies Brady with research (incremental cost US$20,000 a year), holds monthly reviews, and routes commissions to Brady, which cost clients about US$10,000. Last year the referrals generated US$200,000 of fees.
- Step 1: total identifiable consideration given back to the referrer = 20,000 + 10,000 = US$30,000 a year.
- Step 2: as a share of referral fee income = 30,000 / 200,000 = 15%.
- Step 3: the prospect (Maxwell) was told only "Brady recommends you". The required disclosure, before the agreement: the nature (services plus directed commissions), that it is continuing, and its estimated value (US$30,000 a year or roughly 15% of fees), and the added client cost.
- Interpretation: Maxwell's CFO may reassess Brady's endorsement, which is exactly the purpose of VI(C). White's silence breaches the standard even if the manager is competent.

## Anti-patterns
- Boilerplate such as "employees are subject to personal trading policies" as the only disclosure.
- Disclosing once and never updating after compensation or ownership changes.
- Taking IPO or private placement allocations as perks from brokers you reward with business.
- Trading your own or the firm's account before clients' orders or before a published report reaches clients.
- Treating a relative's client account differently, better or worse.
- Accepting side payments or referral fees without telling both employer and clients.
- Leaking exam topics or formulas, even in vague forum posts, after the exam.
- Writing "CFA Level II", "charter pending" or linking the charter to performance in ads.
- Continuing to use the designation after a lapse in PCS or dues.

## Exam Focus & Traps
- VI(A) disclosure goes to **clients, prospects and the employer**; the employer must also hear about outside boards, ownership, and extra pay.
- Best practice is to **avoid** the conflict; disclosure alone is the minimum. In the spouse-owns-stock case, reassigning the report is best, but disclosure is required if the analyst writes.
- VI(B): the three-part test (client not disadvantaged, no personal profit from client trades, rules obeyed). Co-investment is allowed.
- Reporting at hire and **at least annually**; duplicate confirmations; preclearance for personal trades and IPOs; participating in IPOs is best avoided.
- Payroll deductions into diversified funds do not need preclearance (unless firm policy says so).
- VI(C): disclose **before** agreement; include nature and **estimated dollar value**; applies to payer and recipient, and to internal referrals; consideration may be non-cash.
- Performance fees tied to capital appreciation: client disclosure under VI(A) and, if paid by a client on top of firm pay, employer consent under IV(B).
- VII(A): **topics tested or not tested and formulas are confidential**; "this exam was hard" is allowed. Members who solicit confidential exam content also violate.
- VII(A) opinions about CFA Institute are permitted; exam content is not.
- VII(B): no partial designation; no expected completion; "candidate" only if enrolled for a specified exam or awaiting results; lapse of PCS or dues removes the designation.
- Passing three levels in consecutive years may be stated as fact but not as proof of superiority.
- A pseudonym must not carry the designation.

## Key Takeaways
1. Disclose conflicts that could reasonably impair objectivity or duties, in prominent, plain language, to employer, clients and prospects; update on material change.
2. Avoid conflicts or their appearance when possible; comply with employer restrictions.
3. Client and employer trades come first; personal trading must not disadvantage clients, profit from client trades, or break rules.
4. A good personal-trading policy has IPO and private placement limits, blackout periods, holdings disclosure, duplicate confirmations, preclearance, and client-facing disclosure.
5. Disclose referral arrangements before any formal agreement, including nature and estimated value, whether paid or received, inside or outside the firm.
6. Exam topics, formulas and questions are confidential after the exam; opinions about CFA Institute are free.
7. Never claim or imply superior ability or a partial designation from CFA status; keep references factual and approved.
8. Membership requires the annual PCS and dues; lapses end the right to use the designation.

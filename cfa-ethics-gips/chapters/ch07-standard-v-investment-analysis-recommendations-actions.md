# Chapter 7: Standard V, Investment Analysis, Recommendations, and Actions (Diligence and Reasonable Basis, Communication with Clients, Record Retention)
> **Source**: CFA Program Curriculum 2023, Level III, Reading 32 (Guidance for Standards I-VII; Standards of Practice Handbook, 11th ed.) | CFA Institute

## Core Idea
Standard V covers the work product. **V(A)**: do the work (diligence, independence, thoroughness) and have a reasonable and adequate basis backed by research. **V(B)**: tell clients how you invest, the significant risks and limitations, the factors that drove your view, and which statements are fact versus opinion. **V(C)**: keep records that prove what you did and why. Diligence is judged on what was knowable at the time, not on outcomes: a thorough analysis can lose money, a sloppy one can win, and only the first complies.

## Learning Outcomes Covered
- Apply V(A) to V(C): reasonable basis, third-party and quantitative research, manager selection, client communication, record retention.
- Recommend research-review, model-validation, disclosure and records policies.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **V(A) Reasonable basis test**
  - *Requirement*: effort scales with the product, your role and firm resources, but you must cover all pertinent issues. Candidate inputs: macro conditions, company operating and financial history, industry and cycle stage, fund fees and management history, model outputs and limitations, asset quality in securitizations, appropriateness of peer groups.
  - *Third-party and secondary research*: you may rely on it (and on firm-level due diligence on vendors) in good faith **unless you have reason to doubt it**. Check assumptions, rigor, timeliness, and objectivity/independence. Web blogs and social media require more checking than established providers. Firms should review approved providers regularly.
  - *Quantitative research*: understand parameters, assumptions and limitations (not every technical detail); validate model output before use; test breadth of scenarios including extreme positive and negative cases; developers owe a higher standard than users; consider data horizon (databases may miss full cycles) and test outside the observable range; test components **as an integrated whole**, not just individually.
  - *External advisers and subadvisers*: review as diligently as securities: code of ethics, compliance and internal controls, quality of published returns, process and adherence to stated strategy, fees *and* fit with client needs; verify RFP data (interviews, regulatory filings, custodian or auditor); GIPS claim plus return rank is not enough.
  - *Group research*: you need not dissociate from a consensus report you disagree with if you are satisfied the process gave a reasonable, adequate, independent basis. You may ask to remove your name when the product contradicts your view.
  - *Procedures*: supervisory analyst or review committee approves reports before circulation; written due-diligence guidance; measurable research-quality criteria (accuracy over time); minimum scenario testing for models; provider assessment; standard external adviser criteria.
  - *Timeliness*: update reports and recommendations when new material information arrives before the client acts.

- **V(B) four disclosures**
  1. **Process**: basic format and general principles of how you analyze, select and construct portfolios; **promptly disclose changes that could materially affect them**. Disclose to existing clients and prospects alike.
  2. **Significant risks and limitations**: leverage is a significant risk by default; consider counterparty, country, sector, security, credit, liquidity (ability to liquidate on a timely basis at reasonable cost) and capacity (amount beyond which returns suffer). Judged **ex ante**: risks you knew at the time. Ignorance of a risk shows a V(A) problem, not necessarily a V(B) breach.
  3. **Important factors**: use reasonable judgment on what drives the recommendation and include it; a report may be narrow if its scope is stated; capsule recommendations should tell readers more analysis exists.
  4. **Fact vs. opinion**: forecasts, estimated reserves, statistical projections and model output are opinion; separate them and state limits. Do not oversell model accuracy; discuss assumptions with clients; keep methodology consistent and highlight changes.
  - *Communication channels*: any medium; social media may not reach all clients (fairness and public dissemination).

- **What counts as a material process change (disclose to all clients)**: change in market-cap ceiling or universe (adding non-US names), introducing derivatives, relaxing beta or other constraints, new valuation model, shift to passive management, shift from analysts to a committee, move to outside managers, change of external adviser, material model errors and corrections.

- **V(C) Record retention**: keep what substantiates the scope of research and the reasons for actions or conclusions, including reviews that led to no trade. Examples: meeting notes, company releases, model inputs and outputs, risk analyses, external-adviser selection criteria, client IPS meeting notes, outside research. Includes e-mail, text messages, blog and Twitter posts. Records belong to the **firm**: cannot be taken to a new employer without consent, and old recommendations cannot be reused unless supporting records are re-created from public or company sources, not from memory or from the former employer. Follow regulator and firm periods; absent any, **keep at least seven years**.

## Key Concepts
- **Diligence**: effort proportionate to role, product and resources.
- **Reasonable and adequate basis**: research and investigation sufficient to support a recommendation.
- **Secondary research**: produced elsewhere in your firm. **Third-party research**: outside the firm.
- **Model validation**: testing outputs, assumptions and integration before reliance.
- **Scenario breadth**: inclusion of extreme and historically unobserved cases.
- **Capacity**: investment size beyond which returns decline.
- **Liquidity limitation**: ability to exit on time at reasonable cost.
- **Ex ante risk disclosure**: judged on information known at the time.
- **Fact vs. opinion**: past data versus forecasts, estimates and conclusions.
- **Material process change**: change clients would view as significant to what they hired you for.
- **Supervisory analyst / review committee**: independent pre-publication check.
- **Record retention period**: regulator or firm rule, else seven years.
- **Dissociation from group research**: not required if the process is sound.

## Mental Models
- Use **"basis before opinion"**: no recommendation leaves your desk without a documented reason you could defend to a reviewer.
- Use **scenario ladder**: base, downside, extreme downside, extreme upside; show ranges, not single points.
- Use **"would the client change their decision if they knew this?"** to choose which factors, risks and process changes to disclose.
- Use **ex ante vs. ex post**: judge diligence and disclosure on what was knowable, not the later loss.
- Use **"file it or it did not happen"** for research notes, model parameters and client communications.

## Worked Example
*Stress scenario check (reconstructed from the housing-price case).*
- Inputs: model of subprime securitisation; manager's base data contains only years of rising house prices; the researcher adds a path of -10% in year 1, -5% in year 2, then 0% in years 3 to 5.
- Cumulative price index after year 2: 0.90 x 0.95 = 0.855, i.e., a 14.5% fall from the starting level, with no recovery by year 5 (index stays 0.855).
- Interpretation: the database cannot contain this scenario, but the stress is plausible because recent rapid appreciation was funded by looser credit. Under V(A), omitting it would produce an incomplete analysis; running it and acting on a cautionary result is diligence. Record the scenario, inputs and outcome (V(C)).
- Second micro-check (capacity): a strategy seeded at US$12M with estimated capacity US$100M uses 12% of capacity. V(B) still requires disclosing the limit to investors because the limitation is material to expected returns once assets grow.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating a vendor, boss or blog as "the basis".
- Reporting maximum or best-case outcomes as expectations.
- Validating model parts but not the integrated strategy.
- Selecting managers by fee, recent return, or friendship.
- Changing mandate, universe, valuation model or decision body without telling all clients.
- Stating estimates and forecasts as facts.
- Burying risks in a prospectus page nobody reads.
- Failing to retain emails, texts and social media posts that support recommendations.

## Exam Focus & Traps
- Diligence is judged on **process**, not result; a sound recommendation that loses is compliant.
- You may rely on firm-vetted third-party research unless you have reason to question it; check **assumptions, rigor, timeliness, objectivity**.
- Model users need not be experts, but must understand assumptions and limitations; **developers** owe more; test components **and** the whole.
- Group report: no need to dissociate if you are confident in the process.
- V(B)(1): disclose process and **promptly** disclose material changes, **including to existing clients**.
- V(B)(2): leverage is a significant risk; liquidity and capacity are limitations; judged on knowledge at the time.
- V(B)(4): estimates, forecasts, statistical projections are **opinion**.
- V(C): records are firm property; seven years if no rule applies; includes social media and texts; do not take them when leaving; re-create from public sources.
- Selecting managers: review code of ethics, controls, returns quality, process and adherence; verify RFP data.
- Distinguish: V(A) reasonable basis vs. III(C) suitability (client fit) vs. V(B) disclosure; many fact patterns involve two.

## Key Takeaways
1. Every recommendation needs a reasonable and adequate, documented basis scaled to its context.
2. Rely on third-party and model outputs only after due diligence and with their limits understood.
3. Stress your models with extreme and unobserved scenarios and validate the integrated whole.
4. Select managers with a standard, full review, not on fee, performance rank or friendship alone.
5. Disclose investment process, significant risks and limitations (including leverage, liquidity, capacity), and material changes to all clients.
6. Separate facts from opinions, estimates and model output.
7. Keep records of research, inputs and communications (including new media) for the required period, at least seven years if unspecified.
8. Records and models created at work belong to the employer.

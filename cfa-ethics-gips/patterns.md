# Patterns — CFA Ethics and GIPS

## Ethical Decision-Making Framework
**When to use**: duties conflict or pressure (bonus, boss, politics) clouds judgment.
**How**: 1) Identify facts, stakeholders, duties, principles, conflicts. 2) Consider situational influences, firm policy and Code guidance, alternatives. 3) Decide and act. 4) Reflect on the outcome and what was missed. Seek advice from someone outside your incentive structure.
**Trade-offs**: iterative, no guarantee of a fully acceptable option. (ch01, ch09, ch10)

## Violation-Spotting Routine for Cases
**When to use**: any multi-actor vignette.
**How**: 1) List each actor and act. 2) Name the single best-fit Standard per act. 3) Apply the stricter-of-law-or-Code rule. 4) Check timing of disclosure or consent (before agreement or act). 5) Separate the actor from the supervisor. 6) Check near-miss distractor standards.
**Trade-offs**: choose the standard that fits the act, not the harm. (ch02, ch09, ch10)

## Stricter-Rule Mapping
**When to use**: cross-border work or law/policy/Code differences.
**How**: identify applicable law (residence, business place, client country), compare with the Code, follow the more restrictive; if law is silent follow the Code. **Trade-offs**: disclosure under the Code does not satisfy a stricter local ban. (ch03, ch09)

## Escalate, Document, Dissociate
**When to use**: you know of a client or colleague violation.
**How**: confront and fix; escalate to supervisor, compliance or senior management; if unresolved step away (remove your name, decline the client), resign in extreme cases; document. **Trade-offs**: reporting to regulators is not compelled unless law requires; jumping straight to resignation or clients leaves harm unaddressed. (ch03, ch06, ch09)

## MNPI Two-Gate Filter and Wall-Crossing
**When to use**: you hold or receive non-public facts.
**How**: gate 1 material (price impact, source reliability, specificity); gate 2 nonpublic (not broadly disseminated; small-group briefings stay nonpublic). If MNPI: seek public release, tell only compliance, do not trade or change recommendations. Banker/analyst contact goes through a compliance clearance area. **Trade-offs**: waiting costs a day or two; a leak costs a career. (ch04)

## Mosaic Documentation
**When to use**: bold conclusions from channel checks and public data.
**How**: file every public and nonmaterial input; ensure no single material insider fact is included. (ch04, ch07)

## Fair Allocation and Dissemination
**When to use**: recommendation changes, new issues, block trades.
**How**: distribute simultaneously; restrict advance knowledge; allocate before execution by client with documented policy; prorate oversubscribed offerings (minimum lots allowed); family fee-paying accounts treated like any client. (ch05, ch10)

## IPS-First Suitability
**When to use**: opening or reviewing an advisory account.
**How**: gather experience, objectives, constraints; write IPS; assess each action in total-portfolio context; update at least annually and on life changes; unsolicited unsuitable requests get discussion, acknowledgment, IPS update or unmanaged account. (ch05, ch10)

## Personal Trading Control Program
**When to use**: designing a firm policy for VI(B).
**How**: holdings disclosure at hire and annually, duplicate confirmations, preclearance, blackout periods, IPO and private placement limits, informative client disclosure. **Trade-offs**: blackout acts as a trading ban in large firms. (ch08, ch11)

## Conflict-Disclosure Triage
**When to use**: a benefit, relationship or payment appears.
**How**: gift from a party seeking influence = I(B) prohibited; client gift = disclose to employer (I(B)); outside pay competing with employer = IV(B) written consent from all parties; payment for a recommendation of services = VI(C) disclose before agreement with nature and value; any other objectivity threat = VI(A). (ch03, ch06, ch08, ch10)

## Departure Checklist
**When to use**: leaving an employer.
**How**: prepare off-hours; no solicitation of clients, no copying of firm records, no competing for the same RFP; follow client-notification policy; after leaving use only public information unless a noncompete applies. (ch06, ch10)

## Supervisor Response to a Violation
**When to use**: you learn of a possible violation.
**How**: investigate scope promptly (do not accept assurances), restrict the person or increase monitoring, fix procedures; decline in writing if you lack authority. (ch06, ch10)

## Compliance System Build
**When to use**: firm policy design.
**How**: written tailored manual, empowered compliance officer, training, monitoring, reporting and sanctions, incentives rewarding ethical conduct; separate short code of ethics from procedures. (ch06, ch11)

## Referral Fee Disclosure Package
**When to use**: any paid or received referral.
**How**: tell employer and clients before the agreement, in writing, with nature (flat, percent, continuing, non-cash) and estimated value; employer reviews at least quarterly. (ch08)

## Controlled CFA References
**When to use**: cards, bios, ads and web profiles.
**How**: use approved templates; state facts only ("candidate in the CFA Program", "passed Level I"); no expected dates; keep PCS and dues current; full name with designation online. (ch08, ch10)

## GIPS Composite and Return Workflow
**When to use**: building a performance record.
**How**: define firm and discretion; put every actual fee-paying discretionary portfolio in an ex ante composite; value at fair value monthly and at large flows; TWR with geometric linking; asset-weight at least monthly; present 5 to 10 years with dispersion and 3-year standard deviation; verify firm-wide if desired. **Trade-offs**: Modified Dietz is only an approximation. (ch12)

## Asset Manager Code Gap Audit
**When to use**: judging a firm's policy manual.
**How**: map against sections A-F item by item; one gap voids the compliance claim; claim wording must say not verified by CFA Institute; notify online. (ch11)

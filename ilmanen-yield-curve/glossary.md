# Glossary — Understanding the Yield Curve (Ilmanen)

**Arithmetic vs geometric mean** — AM is the expected value, GM the compound (median-like) outcome; `GM ≈ AM − σ²/2` (ch03, ch05)
**Barbell / bullet** — a barbell is short plus long bonds (often with cash); a bullet is one intermediate bond of equal duration (ch01, ch05, ch06)
**Barbell-bullet break-even** — flattening (bp) or parallel shift (bp) needed to offset the barbell's rolling-yield give-up (ch02, ch05)
**Bond risk premium (BRP)** — expected one-period return of a bond over the riskless short rate; also called term premium; includes liquidity, supply and sentiment effects (ch01, ch03, ch06)
**Break-even yield change** — `Δfₙ₋₁ = f₁,ₙ − sₙ₋₁`, the rate move at which a bond earns the riskless return (ch01, ch02)
**Bootstrapping** — solving par yields recursively for spot rates, then forwards (ch01)
**Career risk** — the danger of unconventional underperformance versus peers; explains why slow, high-odds edges persist (ch03, ch04)
**Cash-flow dispersion** — spread of a portfolio's cash-flow dates around its Macaulay duration; drives convexity beyond a zero's (ch05)
**Constant-maturity forward curve** — the curve of one-year forwards f₀,₁, f₁,₂, f₂,₃…; equals rolling yields. Not the same as the implied spot curve one year forward (ch01, ch02)
**Convexity (Cx)** — `(100/P)·d²P/dy²`; for zeros about Dur²/100 (ch01, ch05)
**Convexity bias (CB)** — negative of the value of convexity, `≈ −½·Cx·σ²`; lowers long yields and humps or inverts the curve (ch01, ch05, ch06)
**Convexity-adjusted expected return** — rolling yield plus `½·Cx·σ²`; the right number for duration-neutral comparisons (ch05, ch06)
**Cushion** — yield and rolldown advantage that absorbs rate rises before a position loses (ch01, ch02)
**Cycle of fear and greed** — risk aversion and required premia rise when wealth is low versus its past, fall near expansion peaks (ch04)
**Data snooping** — spurious findings from repeated searching of one dataset (ch04)
**Duration impact of the view** — `−Dur_h·E(Δy)`, the expected-return term from your rate forecast (ch06)
**Dispersion term (Kogelman)** — extra convexity of a coupon bond over a duration-matched zero (ch05)
**Expanding-window forecast** — re-estimating a model each month with only data then available (ch04)
**Forward rate f_m,n** — rate for a loan from m to n fixed today; `(1+f)ⁿ⁻ᵐ = (1+sₙ)ⁿ/(1+sₘ)ᵐ` (ch01)
**Forward-spot premium (FSP)** — `fₙ₋₁,ₙ − s₁ = term spread + rolldown`; equals `(n−1)·Δfₙ₋₁` (ch01, ch02, ch06)
**Geometric premium** — compound return of a maturity sector minus that of the 1-month bill (ch03)
**Hit rate** — frequency with which a longer maturity beat a shorter one, or forecast sign matched outcome (ch03, ch04)
**Implied spot curve one year forward** — f₁,₂, f₁,₃…; the break-even curve next year (ch01, ch02)
**Implied volatility of a barbell-bullet trade** — volatility at which convexity pick-up equals the rolling-yield give-up; negative on a dished curve (ch05)
**Inverse wealth** — `0.1·Σ0.9ᵏWₜ₋₁₋ₖ/Wₜ`; above 1 after a stock fall; a risk-aversion proxy and filter for the term spread (ch04)
**Inverted volatility term structure** — short yields more volatile than long (1970–94) (ch03, ch05)
**Liquidity premium hypothesis** — BRP positive and rising with duration (ch01, ch03)
**Local expectations hypothesis (LEH)** — equal expected one-period returns for all bonds (ch01, ch02, ch06)
**Local rich/cheap** — financing advantage, spread to fitted curve and expected cheapening of an issue (ch06)
**Market price of convexity** — gap between forward-implied and survey-expected flattening (ch02)
**Momentum dummy** — +1/−1 if long yield is more than 5bp below/above its 6-month average, else 0 (ch04)
**Money-market credit-spread inversion** — private-issuer spreads wider at 1m than 6m because 1m bills are overpriced (ch03)
**Neutral sample period** — window whose start and end yields match, so trend effects wash out (ch03)
**Par yield curve** — yields of hypothetical par bonds; the flattest of par, spot, forward (ch01)
**Persistence factor (PF)** — slope of realized BRP on FSP; 0 under PEH, 1 if the curve persists; 0.8 below 1y, 1.4–2.3 beyond (ch02, ch04)
**Preferred habitat hypothesis** — long-liability holders find long bonds safe, so BRP may fall with duration (ch01, ch03)
**Pure expectations hypothesis (PEH)** — BRP = 0; forwards are expected future rates (ch01, ch02)
**Real yield** — 5y spot minus latest year-on-year CPI; BRP predictor (ch04)
**Risk premium hypothesis** — forwards reflect premia only; E(Δs) = 0; today's curve is the forecast (ch02)
**Rolldown return** — capital gain from aging down an upward-sloping curve (ch01, ch02)
**Rolling yield** — yield plus rolldown; return if the curve is unchanged; equals the one-year forward for a zero (ch01, ch02, ch05)
**Scaled / 1/0 strategies** — dynamic duration: position proportional to forecast, or long 1 unit if forecast > 0 else cash (ch04)
**Scenario analysis with probabilities** — weighted scenario returns from which implied view, volatility and correlations are backed out (ch06)
**Sharpe ratio (bonds)** — excess return over volatility; falls monotonically with maturity in 1970–94 (ch03, ch04)
**Spot rate sₙ** — yield of an n-year zero (ch01)
**Term spread** — sₙ − s₁; noisy BRP proxy (ch01, ch04)
**Treasury Model spread** — rich/cheap vs a fitted curve; persistent (ch01, ch06)
**Unbiased expectations hypothesis (UEH)** — forwards equal expected spots; differs from LEH by convexity bias (ch01, ch02, ch06)
**Value of convexity** — `≈ ½·Cx·σ²` with σ the basis-point yield volatility (ch05, ch06)
**Yield volatility (bp vs relative)** — σ_bp = Vol(Δy/y)·y; option quotes are relative (ch05)
**Yield to maturity** — single discount rate equating PV to price; misleading on a sloped curve (ch01)

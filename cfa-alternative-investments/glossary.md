# Glossary — CFA Alternative Investments (L1 · L2 · L3)

**AFFO (FAD)** — FFO less straight-line rent, recurring capex and leasing costs; best proxy for REIT dividend capacity, no standard definition (ch 02)
**Alternative investments** — non-traditional, often private, leveraged, illiquid or derivative-based vehicles: hedge funds, PE, real estate, commodities, infrastructure, other (ch 01)
**Appraisal lag / smoothing** — appraised values trail transactions, understating volatility and correlation and overstating Sharpe (ch 01, 02, 06)
**Backfill bias** — a fund enters a database with its prior (good) history, overstating returns (ch 01)
**Backwardation** — spot above near futures above deferred futures; positive calendar spread and positive roll return (ch 04)
**Basis (commodity)** — spot price minus futures price (ch 04)
**Beta vs correlation** — beta is response size, correlation is strength of linear link; hedge funds have beta about 0.4 but correlation about 0.8 (ch 06)
**Brownfield / greenfield** — existing infrastructure with operating history vs assets to be built (ch 01)
**Cap rate** — NOI1 / value; equals discount rate minus growth; going-in vs terminal (ch 01, 02)
**Capital call / commitment** — LP promise to fund and the GP's staged draw; undrawn commitments are liabilities excluded from NAV (ch 03, 06)
**Carried interest** — GP share of fund profit, typically 20% after management fees and hurdle (ch 01, 03)
**Clawback** — GP returns excess carry so LPs get capital back plus an agreed share of profit (ch 01, 03)
**Collateral return** — interest on cash/T-bills backing a fully collateralized futures position (ch 04)
**Commitment pacing** — spreading fund commitments over several vintages to reach a target NAV weight (ch 06)
**Commodity** — physical good from a natural resource with no cash flows; six sectors: energy, grains, industrial metals, livestock, precious metals, softs (ch 01, 04)
**Contango** — futures above spot, deferred above near; negative calendar spread and negative roll return (ch 01, 04)
**Convenience yield** — benefit of holding physical stock; high when inventories are low (ch 01, 04)
**Conversion ratio / price / value** — shares per convertible bond; bond price / ratio; stock price x ratio (ch 05)
**CVaR (expected shortfall)** — expected loss beyond the VaR threshold; used in mean-CVaR optimization (ch 06)
**Deal-by-deal (American) waterfall** — carry paid after each exit; favors the GP, needs clawback. Total return (European) pays after capital is returned; favors LPs (ch 03)
**Dedicated short / short bias** — 60%-120% short vs 30%-60% net short; negative equity correlation (ch 05)
**Distressed investing** — buying securities of troubled issuers; fulcrum security becomes new equity (ch 01, 05)
**Drawdown** — fall of NAV below its high-water mark; maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough loss (ch 01, 05)
**DSCR** — NOI / debt service; lenders require about 1.25-1.5x (ch 02)
**DPI / RVPI / TVPI / PIC** — distributions, residual NAV, their sum, all per dollar paid in; PIC is paid-in over committed (ch 03)
**Equity dividend rate** — (NOI - debt service) / equity; first-year levered cash yield, not an IRR (ch 02)
**Equity market neutral (EMN)** — long/short with beta near zero; leverage amplifies a modest edge (ch 05)
**FFO** — net income + real estate depreciation + losses (- gains) on sales + impairments (ch 01, 02)
**Fulcrum security** — partially in-the-money claim that will own the reorganized company (ch 05)
**Fund of funds (FoF)** — pool of hedge funds: access, diversification, diligence, at the cost of a second fee layer and netting risk (ch 01, 05)
**Gate / lock-up / notice / holdback** — redemption limits that let managers unwind in an orderly way (ch 01, 06)
**GP / LP** — general partner manages with unlimited liability; limited partners hold fractional interests (ch 01, 03)
**Hedging pressure hypothesis** — curve shape follows the imbalance between producer and consumer hedging (ch 04)
**Highest and best use** — use with the highest residual land value, not the highest completed value (ch 02)
**High-water mark (HWM)** — peak net-of-fee value that must be recovered before a new incentive fee, per investor (ch 01)
**Hurdle rate (hard/soft)** — minimum return before incentive fee; hard = fee on excess, soft = fee on all once cleared; PE preferred return 7%-10% (ch 01, 03)
**Insurance theory (Keynes)** — producers hedge, speculators demand a discount, so futures < expected spot (ch 04)
**Insurance-seller payoff** — merger arbitrage, on/off-the-run and short-vol collect small premia and lose in the tail (ch 05)
**J-curve** — low early reported returns from fees and cost marks, improving as companies mature (ch 03, 06)
**Layer method** — values contractual and market-rent income layers at separate risk (ch 02)
**LBO model** — solves for the maximum price that meets financiers' required returns; a price ceiling, not fair value (ch 03)
**Leverage (LTV)** — loan / value; rising LTV raises risk for debt and equity (ch 01, 02)
**Liquid alts** — mutual fund/UCITS/ETF wrappers of hedge strategies: daily liquidity, no incentive fee, 100-200 bps below private funds (ch 05)
**Managed futures (CTA)** — systematic trend following, positive skew (ch 05)
**Mean-CVaR optimization** — allocation using tail loss instead of variance; avoids MVO over-allocation to alternatives (ch 06)
**Merger arbitrage** — long target, short acquirer in stock deals; riskless bond plus short binary put (ch 05)
**Mezzanine** — subordinated debt or preferred with equity kicker (ch 03)
**MOIC** — (value + distributions) / invested capital; ignores timing (ch 06)
**NAVPS** — REIT net asset value per share: NOI / cap rate + other assets - liabilities, / shares (ch 02)
**NOI** — rental revenue less operating expenses, before financing, tax, depreciation, capex (ch 01, 02)
**Net / gross / percentage lease** — tenant pays expenses / owner pays / base rent plus share of sales above breakpoint (ch 02)
**Pre-money / post-money** — value before / after new financing; ownership = investment / post-money (ch 03)
**PME** — public market equivalent: invests fund cash flows in an index to compare with IRR (ch 03)
**REIT / REOC / TRS** — tax-transparent distributor (90%-100% payout, 75% tests) / taxable operating company / taxable REIT subsidiary (ch 02)
**Repeat-sales vs hedonic index** — transaction indexes: same asset twice vs regression on characteristics (ch 01, 02)
**Roll return** — (near - far) / near x share rolled; accounting component of futures return (ch 04)
**Rho (serial correlation)** — first-order autocorrelation; high rho flags smoothed returns (ch 05, 06)
**Sharpe / Sortino** — excess return per standard deviation / per downside deviation (ch 01, 05)
**Side pocket** — illiquid assets segregated from redemption terms (ch 06)
**Stub trading** — parent vs subsidiaries weighted by ownership (ch 05)
**Survivorship bias** — only surviving funds remain, overstating index returns (ch 01, 05)
**Takahashi-Alexander model** — contributions, NAV growth and distributions schedule for private funds (ch 06)
**Theory of storage (Kaldor)** — futures = spot + storage costs - convenience yield (ch 04)
**Total return swap (commodity)** — pays index return incl. collateral, net of fee; excess return swap excludes collateral (ch 04)
**Unsmoothing** — r_u = (r - s x r_prev) / (1 - s) to recover true volatility (ch 06)
**VC method** — post-money = exit value / ROI, pre-money = post - investment (ch 03)
**Vintage year** — fund launch year; peers compared within vintage (ch 03, 06)
**Volatility / variance swap** — forward on realized volatility or variance, zero value at fair strike (ch 04, 05)

# Glossary — CFA Economics (Level I R12-R18, Level II R6-R8)

**Absolute advantage** — lower absolute resource cost of producing a good; does not determine trade direction (ch06)
**Absorption approach** — `X − M = Y − absorption`; depreciation helps only if income rises relative to spending (ch07)
**Aggregate demand (AD)** — downward-sloping curve from IS-LM: higher price level lowers real money, raises rates, cuts output (ch03)
**Automatic stabilizer** — tax and transfer feature that moves the deficit with the cycle without legislation (ch03, ch05)
**Average product of labor (APL)** — `Q/L`; the right measure to compare firm efficiency (ch01)
**Bail-in** — shareholders and creditors absorb a failed bank's losses instead of taxpayers (ch10)
**Balance of payments (BOP)** — double-entry ledger: current + capital + financial accounts + discrepancy = 0 (ch06)
**Base / price currency** — in `P/B`, B is the unit priced and P the pricing currency; the base appreciates when the quote rises (ch07, ch08)
**Bid / offer** — dealer buys the base at the bid, sells at the offer; inversion swaps them (ch07, ch08)
**Business cycle** — recurrent, non-periodic swings in activity: trough, expansion, peak, contraction (ch04)
**Capital deepening** — rise in capital per worker; movement along the production function with diminishing returns (ch03, ch09)
**Carry trade** — long high-yield, short low-yield currency, unhedged; earns when UIP fails, crashes in volatility spikes (ch08)
**Cartel** — formal collusion on price or output; unstable because members gain by cheating (ch02)
**Central bank independence** — operational (how) versus target (what) independence (ch05)
**Cobb-Douglas** — `Y = A K^α L^(1−α)`; α is capital's income share (ch09)
**Comparative advantage** — lower opportunity cost; basis for gains from trade (ch06)
**Concentration ratio / HHI** — sum of top-N shares / sum of squared shares; screens that ignore entry and elasticity (ch02)
**Convergence (absolute, conditional, club)** — poor countries catch up unconditionally / given same fundamentals / only within an institutional club (ch09)
**Core inflation** — inflation excluding food and energy (ch04)
**Cournot model** — simultaneous quantity setting; outcome between competition and monopoly (ch02)
**Covered interest parity (CIP)** — `F = S(1 + i_P τ)/(1 + i_B τ)`; enforced by arbitrage; high-rate currency at forward discount (ch07, ch08)
**Cross-price elasticity** — `%ΔQx/%ΔPy`; positive substitutes, negative complements (ch01)
**Crowding out** — government borrowing raises rates and displaces private investment (ch05)
**Currency board** — legal parity with 100% foreign backing of the monetary base (ch07)
**Deadweight loss** — surplus destroyed by a tariff, quota or monopoly restriction (ch02, ch06)
**Deflation / disinflation / hyperinflation** — falling price level / slower positive inflation / extreme money-financed inflation (ch04)
**Demand-pull / cost-push inflation** — demand above capacity / rising input costs, notably wages (ch04)
**Diffusion index** — share of components rising; measures breadth of a move (ch04)
**Diminishing marginal returns** — with a fixed input, each added unit of the variable input adds less (ch01)
**Dornbusch overshooting** — sticky prices make the currency overshoot its long-run level after a monetary shock (ch08)
**Dutch disease** — resource-driven appreciation that hollows out manufacturing (ch09)
**Economic profit** — revenue less all opportunity costs including a normal return (ch01, ch02)
**Economies of scale / minimum efficient scale** — falling LRAC / output at the bottom of LRAC (ch01)
**Endogenous growth** — growth driven by R&D and human capital spillovers; saving can raise the growth rate (ch09)
**Externality** — spillover cost or benefit on third parties; basis for taxes, subsidies and mandates (ch10)
**Fiscal multiplier** — `1/[1 − c(1 − t)]`; balanced-budget multiplier is 1 (ch05)
**Fisher effect** — `i = r + πe`; international version links the interest gap to the inflation gap (ch05, ch08)
**Forward points** — `(F − S) × 10,000` (yen pairs ×100); positive means base at a premium (ch07, ch08)
**Giffen / Veblen good** — inferior staple with perverse income effect / status good whose price adds appeal (ch01)
**GDP deflator** — nominal/real GDP × 100; prices of domestic output (ch03, ch04)
**Growth accounting** — `ΔY/Y = ΔA/A + αΔK/K + (1 − α)ΔL/L`; TFP is the residual (ch03, ch09)
**Heckscher-Ohlin** — comparative advantage from relative factor endowments, same technology (ch06)
**Holdout problem** — counterparty exploits sunk costs to renegotiate; weak contract law discourages long-term investment (ch10)
**Impossible trinity** — fixed rates, free capital flows and independent monetary policy cannot coexist (ch07)
**Income elasticity** — `%ΔQ/%ΔI`; positive normal, negative inferior (ch01)
**Informational friction** — adverse selection and moral hazard; drives disclosure and insider-trading rules (ch10)
**Inventory-sales ratio** — rises near peaks, falls in early recovery; lagging indicator (ch04)
**IOSCO** — body of national securities regulators setting principles; standard setter, not a regulator (ch10)
**IS / LM curves** — goods-market and money-market equilibria in (Y, r) space (ch03)
**J-curve** — trade balance worsens before it improves after depreciation (ch07)
**Kinked demand** — oligopoly model with sticky prices; rivals match cuts, ignore rises (ch02)
**Laspeyres / Paasche / Fisher index** — base basket / current basket / geometric mean of both (ch04)
**Law of one price / PPP** — identical goods cost the same across borders; absolute, relative, ex ante versions (ch07, ch08)
**Lerner markup** — `(P − MC)/P = 1/ε` (ch02)
**Liquidity trap** — extra money is hoarded; monetary policy loses traction (ch03, ch05)
**Marginal revenue** — `ΔTR/ΔQ`; equals price only for price takers; `MR = P(1 − 1/ε)` (ch01, ch02)
**Marshall-Lerner condition** — `ω_X ε_X + ω_M(ε_M − 1) > 0`; depreciation improves the trade balance (ch07)
**Monetary neutrality** — long-run money growth changes prices only (ch05)
**Monopolistic competition** — many sellers, differentiated products; zero long-run profit with excess capacity (ch02)
**Mundell-Fleming** — short-run policy-mix effect on the currency, dependent on capital mobility (ch08)
**NAIRU** — unemployment rate below which wage pressure accelerates; unobservable (ch04)
**Nash equilibrium** — no player gains by deviating unilaterally; usually not joint-profit maximizing (ch02)
**Natural monopoly** — falling LRAC over the market size; regulated by average-cost pricing (ch02, ch10)
**Neutral rate** — real trend growth plus the inflation target (ch05)
**Net regulatory burden** — private costs of regulation less private benefits (ch10)
**Normal / inferior good** — positive / negative income elasticity (ch01)
**Own-price elasticity** — `%ΔQ/%ΔP`; elastic when |E| > 1; revenue peaks at |E| = 1 (ch01)
**Pareto optimal** — no reallocation helps one without hurting another; holds without frictions and externalities (ch10)
**Potential GDP** — maximum sustainable output without inflation pressure; grows with labor force plus productivity (ch03, ch09)
**Prudential supervision** — safety and soundness monitoring of institutions (ch10)
**Public good** — non-rival consumption, usually government-funded (ch10)
**Quantitative easing** — large-scale asset purchases creating reserves (ch05)
**Quantity theory** — `MV = PY` (ch03, ch05)
**Real exchange rate** — `S_(d/f) × P_f/P_d`; relative purchasing power (ch07)
**Regulatory arbitrage / capture / competition** — exploiting regime differences / regulation serving the regulated / regulators bidding for firms (ch10)
**Ricardian equivalence** — households save a tax cut in anticipation of future taxes (ch05)
**Ricardian model** — comparative advantage from technology differences (ch06)
**Self-regulating organization (SRO)** — private body recognized by government with enforcement power (ch10)
**Shutdown / breakeven point** — `P < AVC` short run; `P = ATC` zero economic profit (ch01)
**Solow model** — neoclassical growth with diminishing returns; steady-state growth `θ/(1 − α)` (ch09)
**Stagflation** — falling output with rising prices, an SRAS shock (ch03, ch04)
**Structural (cyclically adjusted) deficit** — deficit at full employment; the measure of fiscal stance (ch05, ch09)
**Substitution / income effect** — price-change decomposition; defines normal, inferior, Giffen (ch01)
**Terms of trade** — export price index / import price index (ch06)
**Total factor productivity (TFP)** — scale factor A; residual from growth accounting (ch03, ch09)
**Trade creation / diversion** — customs-union shifts of purchases to a lower-cost member / to a higher-cost member (ch06)
**Triangular arbitrage** — profit from a dealer cross that departs from the implied cross (ch07, ch08)
**Uncovered interest parity (UIP)** — expected depreciation equals the interest gap; not arbitrage-enforced (ch08)
**Unit labor cost** — compensation per hour over output per hour (ch03, ch04)

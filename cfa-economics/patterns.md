# Patterns — CFA Economics

## Elasticity from a Linear Demand Function
**When to use**: an estimated `Q = a − bPx + cI + dPy` is given. **How**: 1) substitute the stated Px, I, Py to get Q; 2) elasticity = coefficient × (variable/Q); 3) classify by |E| versus 1 and sign. **Trade-offs**: local approximation; elasticity changes along the curve. (ch01)

## Operate / Shut Down / Exit Decision
**When to use**: a firm earning a loss. **How**: compare P with AVC (short-run operate) and ATC (long-run stay); loss when shut = TFC. **Trade-offs**: ignores sunk-cost reversals and price outlook. (ch01)

## Monopoly and Duopoly Solving
**When to use**: linear demand, given costs. **How**: monopoly: `MR = α − 2βQ = MC`, price off demand, check `ε > 1`. Cournot `q = (a − c)/[b(N + 1)]`; Stackelberg leader `(a − c)/2b`, follower half of that. Compare with the cartel. **Trade-offs**: assumes constant MC and homogeneous goods. (ch02)

## Market Structure Screen
**When to use**: judging profit durability. **How**: score sellers, differentiation, barriers (incl. exit costs), pricing power; add contestability, HHI and Porter's forces; check the markup `1/ε`. **Trade-offs**: concentration ratios ignore entry. (ch02)

## GDP Reconciliation and Sectoral Balances
**When to use**: national accounts data. **How**: expenditure `C + I + G + X − M` against income components; disposable income = GDP − CFC − transfers/taxes; `S = I + (G − T) + (X − M)`. **Trade-offs**: statistical discrepancy absorbs timing and source gaps. (ch03, ch06)

## AD/AS Shock Diagnosis and Asset Tilt
**When to use**: reading a macro move. **How**: output and prices same direction = AD; opposite = SRAS; map to recessionary gap, inflationary gap, stagflation; tilt defensives/long bonds, cyclicals/commodities, or commodities. **Trade-offs**: policy lags and self-correction speed are uncertain. (ch03)

## Locating the Cycle
**When to use**: judging recovery or peak. **How**: read overtime, temps, claims, orders, inventory-sales, then leading/coincident/lagging composites and diffusion; markets lead by 3-6 months. **Trade-offs**: composites revise and relationships break. (ch04)

## Price Index Computation
**When to use**: inflation questions. **How**: Laspeyres `ΣP_tQ_0/ΣP_0Q_0`, Paasche with current quantities, Fisher = geometric mean; headline vs core; deflate GDP with the GDP deflator. **Trade-offs**: Laspeyres biases from substitution, quality, new goods. (ch04)

## Monetary and Fiscal Stance Check
**When to use**: classify policy. **How**: monetary: policy rate versus neutral (trend growth + target); fiscal: change in the structural balance; multiplier `1/[1 − c(1 − t)]`; debt drift `(r − g)D/Y − primary balance`; mix table for rates and output. **Trade-offs**: neutral rate and potential GDP are unobserved. (ch05)

## Trade Welfare Analysis
**When to use**: tariffs, quotas, VERs. **How**: small country: ΔCS = −(A+B+C+D), ΔPS = +A, revenue C, DWL = B + D; quota with foreign rents loses B+C+D; test opportunity costs for comparative advantage with terms of trade between autarky costs. **Trade-offs**: large-country terms-of-trade gains need no retaliation. (ch06)

## FX Cross, Forward and CIP Arbitrage
**When to use**: pricing and checking quotes. **How**: write `price/base`, cancel units for crosses (bids×bids, offers×offers; invert swaps sides); `F = S(1 + i_P τ)/(1 + i_B τ)` with Actual/360; points ÷ 10,000 (÷ 100 yen); compare dealer quotes to implied values and trade the gap. **Trade-offs**: stress and basis breaks CIP briefly. (ch07, ch08)

## Forward Mark-to-Market
**When to use**: valuing an open forward. **How**: 1) offset position; 2) new all-in rate (bid if selling base, offer if buying); 3) cash flow `N × (F_orig − F_new)` signed; 4) discount at price-currency rate over the remaining days. **Trade-offs**: currency of the cash flow follows the exposure view. (ch08)

## Carry Trade Return and Risk
**When to use**: unhedged yield-spread trades. **How**: `(1 + i_inv) × S_0/S_1 − (1 + i_fund)` in the investor's currency; assess crash risk with leverage, volatility and positioning. **Trade-offs**: skewed, fat-tailed payoff. (ch08)

## Marshall-Lerner and Absorption Test
**When to use**: judging whether depreciation fixes a deficit. **How**: check `ω_X ε_X + ω_M(ε_M − 1) > 0` with trade shares, allow a J-curve, then ask whether national saving rises relative to investment. **Trade-offs**: elasticities are low early and unstable. (ch07)

## Growth Accounting and Potential GDP
**When to use**: estimating sustainable growth. **How**: TFP = `g_Y − αg_K − (1 − α)g_L`; capital deepening = productivity growth − TFP growth; potential = labor growth + productivity growth; compare with actual growth for inflation pressure. **Trade-offs**: window choice distorts averages. (ch03, ch09)

## Solow Steady-State and Level-Effect Calculation
**When to use**: saving-rate or policy questions. **How**: steady growth `θ/(1 − α)`; `Ψ = (1/s)[θ/(1 − α) + δ + n]`; transition `θ/(1 − α) + αs(y/k − Ψ)`; level change `(Ψ ratio)^(1/(α−1))`. **Trade-offs**: exogenous TFP; endogenous growth swaps in `Δy/y = sc − δ − n`. (ch09)

## Regulatory Impact Assessment
**When to use**: a proposed rule hits a company or sector. **How**: 1) probability of adoption (intent, CBA, lobbying, politics); 2) effect on revenue (caps, bans, allowed return), costs (opex, capex, one-off or recurring, pass-through) and business risk (fines, bans); 3) put into forecasts, multiples or the discount rate. **Trade-offs**: unintended consequences and regulatory uncertainty are hard to price. (ch10)

## Regulator and Rule Classification
**When to use**: exam or analyst scoping. **How**: ask (a) who funds it and who gives authority (state, statute, members); (b) statute, administrative rule or judicial law; (c) capture, competition, arbitrage risk; (d) intended versus unintended costs. **Trade-offs**: ambiguous bodies exist (ACRA, PCAOB, EU instruments). (ch10)

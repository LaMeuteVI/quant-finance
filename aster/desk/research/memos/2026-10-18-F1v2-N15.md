## 8. Why the cushion of 2 bps/fixing did not test the carry hypothesis

This is the explicit R1 lesson the queue asked for.

The carry hypothesis says: *funding is persistent; when the predicted
funding exceeds the round-trip amortised cost, enter; otherwise stay
out.* The 2 bps/fixing cushion in F1v2 was a **fixed signal threshold
on the funding rate itself**, not on the net-of-costs expected carry:

1. Funding averages ≈1.23 bps/fixing on the BTC panel, so the cushion
   sat *above* the unconditional mean almost everywhere. The signal
   "predict funding > 2 bps/fixing?" was **always true** when measured
   over a long lookback and never true when measured bar-by-bar.
2. F1v2 entered **inconditionally every bar** (or on the unconditional
   mean), paying a round-trip on every entry/exit regardless of whether
   funding was actually paying. With 18.26×/y turnover, the entry fee
   alone (10 bps spot + 5 bps perp = 15 bps each side ≈ 30 bps per
   round-trip) overwhelms any mean funding edge.
3. **The test was of funding persistence, not of carry net of
   amortised costs.** It asked "is funding > 2 bps?", not "is
   (funding − amortised_cost) > 0?" with hysteresis around the
   amortisation rate.
4. F1v3 must therefore: (a) use a signal = funding − amortised_round_trip_cost,
   (b) enter only when the signal clears a *positive* threshold (e.g.
   2 bps/fixing *after* the round-trip is paid), (c) exit when funding
   flips sign or the signal goes negative (hysteresis), (d) measure
   net Sharpe ex-ante on the panel, not just CAGR.

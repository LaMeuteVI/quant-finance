## 3 — Interpretation (honnest)

- **Intra-bar order flow has strong positive autocorrelation** (lag1=0.17) → flow
  is **persistent** at the 50-BTC bar horizon. A buy imbalance tends to be
  followed by another buy imbalance. This is a *microstructure* fact, not yet
  a tradable signal.
- **Top-5% buy / sell flow predict next-bar returns** with the right sign and
  near-symmetric magnitude (+0.014% / -0.014%). The *median* is slightly
  negative (-5e-5) which is a tiny mean-reversion overlay on top of the
  continuation.
- **Gross expected return** (one round-trip / 2 bars): 0.014% × 2 = 0.028%
  per signal. With 1 trade/day on a single symbol, that is roughly 10%/year
  pre-cost, but the 2.8 bps gross round-trip signal is **below** the
  conservative 8 bps/side (16 bps round-trip) hurdle in `costs.md`. This is
  therefore not economically validated and no capacity claim is made.
- **Kyle λ** is small in absolute terms (the flow bar is in BTC units, the
  return is in log price, so λ mixes scales), but the sign is right and
  matches the rest of the picture.

- **No ETH flow stats** (Q4 aggTrades ETH is not in `data/` — only BTC was

  rule, no speculative mass download here.)
- **One partition window** (2022-Q1+Q2). A wider window (2023-Q3, 2024-H1)
  would confirm the persistence is not a 2022-specific regime.

## 5 — Edge implications (forward-looking, not a backtest)

- A **next-bar continuation** signal (long if top-5% buy flow, short if
  top-5% sell) is a candidate. It can be cast as a Q-family hypothesis
  with strict pre-registration, in-sample validation (walk-forward), and
  a holdout read.
- The signal needs to be **normalized by bar size** (a 100-BTC bar is
  more informative than a 50-BTC bar) and **time-decayed** (old flow
  should matter less).

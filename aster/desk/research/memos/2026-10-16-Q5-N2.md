## Falsification verdict

1. *1m and 1d returns have the same sign of autocorr*: cannot test (1m absent from V1) but the pattern is **consistent** — both 5m and 1d show negative lag-1 autocorr, so the horizon-dependence claim is **not refuted**, just not pinpointed.
2. *VR fails to reject RW at all horizons*: not triggered. Most VR(2) sit in [0.92, 1.02] — the random-walk null is not rejected, but the **direction is mean-reverting on most pairs** (VR < 1, autocorr < 0), which is a mild deviation from a strict random walk. The signal is small, not absent.
3. *Hurst = 0.5 ± 0.05 at every horizon*: **partially falsified**. All 25 Hurst values lie in [0.595, 0.649], well above 0.55 — the scaling story is one of **long-memory positive trend** at every horizon, not 0.5 random-walk. The brief's "momentum or mean-reverting, at which scale?" is answered: **mildly trending on 5m–1d, mean-reverting on the lag-1 return, strong volatility clustering at every horizon**.

## Cross-symbol cross-TF observations

* **Return autocorr at lag 1** is negative on 22/25 cells. The exception is SOL 1d (ret_lag5 = -0.025) and XRP 4h (ret_lag1 = +0.006). Crypto is **mean-reverting at 1-bar** on liquid pairs.
* **|r| autocorr at lag 1** is between +0.16 (BTC 1d) and +0.44 (BNB 15m, XRP 5m). Strongest on 15m and 5m; weakest on 1d (still solidly positive). The "GARCH effect" is the **single most robust** empirical feature across this grid.
* **VR(2)** is in [0.92, 1.02]. The strongest mean-reversion signal is BNB 1d (0.888) and BNB 1h (0.952) — but these are also small samples (2500 obs / 58k obs). On the 5m grid, the VR is essentially 1.0 for all symbols.
* **Hurst** is uniform across symbols per TF: 0.596-0.610 on 5m/15m/1h, 0.609-0.621 on 4h, 0.597-0.649 on 1d. SOL has the highest 1d Hurst (0.649) — its small sample amplifies the trend.

## What this means for the next desk actions

* **The microstructure mean-reversion at 5m (autocorr -0.05, VR 0.95)** is large enough to be a potential signal if cost is low enough. **Q14 (cross-sectional / pairs)** is the natural home, not Q5.
* **Volatility clustering** is the desk's strongest empirical fact across every (symbol, TF) — confirms that **vol-targeting** and **HAR-RV** (Q10) are worth pursuing. The brief's mention of EGARCH/GJR is on point.
* **Hurst > 0.5 at every horizon** is **informative against a pure mean-reversion strategy** and **mildly supportive of trend** at long horizons. The desk's prior "trend on daily, mean-revert on 1m" is only half-right: it's "trend on every horizon via Hurst, but mean-revert at 1-bar via autocorr". A trend strategy has a small edge here, but a mean-reversion strategy on the 1-bar autocorr is at war with the long-memory Hurst.
* **Q5 V2** = seasonality (hour UTC, weekday, funding fixings 00/08/16, session opens, month-end, weekends) and frozen-minute exclusion. Q5 V3 = jumps + CUSUM/SADF. Each is its own memo.

## Limits and caveats

* No frozen-minute exclusion in V1. A handful of flat bars at 0:00 UTC on the 1m series (from a small exchange data outage) could pull autocorr toward 0. The 1m data is excluded from V1 (not materialised) precisely because of this risk.
* VR(2) and Hurst are point estimates without confidence intervals. V2 should add block-bootstrap CIs.
* The sample is 5-7 years per symbol — long enough to span multiple regimes, but the 2022 crypto winter and 2023-2024 ETF flows are not separately identified. Regime decomposition is Q11.

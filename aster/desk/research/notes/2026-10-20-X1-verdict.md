## 3. The mechanism is real; the trade is not

This is the part worth keeping. The signal works exactly as
preregistered:

- mean funding on the **long** leg (cheapest tercile): **0.068 bps/fixing**
- mean funding on the **short** leg (dearest tercile): **1.458 bps/fixing**
- spread: **−1.39 bps/fixing** in the book's favour

Sign convention verified against the engine: on Binance `rate > 0` means
the **long pays**. Longing the cheap leg and shorting the dear leg
therefore *receives* the spread, and the idealised book earns
**+2.47 %/yr**. The engine's reported `funding_drag_ann` of −1.64 %
(negative drag = gain) is the same effect net of the book's actual,
sometimes-empty positions.

**So the funding edge exists and is captured correctly.** What fails is
the cost of harvesting it:

- the mean C1b perp round trip across the 12 symbols is **42.9 bps**;
- at 20.96×/yr turnover that is **9.01 %/yr**;
- the preregistered break-even holding period on this universe is
  **72 days**. The prescribed **7-day** rebalance is a factor of ten
  too fast.

This is precisely the zero-net bar written into the preregistration
*before* the run: cost 7.27 bps/fixing > spread 2.11 bps/fixing. The
prediction held.

`expected_max_sr_pp = 0.0`. The Deflated Sharpe carries **no**
multiple-testing penalty here — the family is untouched. The result is
rejected on its own economics, which is the strongest form of
rejection available: no amount of extra trials changes it.

`min_trl_bars = inf`, because the edge itself is negative.

## 6. Universe-subset diagnostics — reported, explicitly NOT gated

Recorded here because the preregistration committed to publishing them.
They are **not** gates and **not** an alternative universe: the
prescribed universe is the 12 symbols with both spot OHLCV and perp
funding on disk.

| Universe | Gross spread | Mean RT cost | Break-even hold | Net at weekly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 (prescribed) | 2.11 bps/fixing | 42.9 bps | 72.0 d | **−56 %/yr** |
| 5 majors | 2.88 bps/fixing | 35.6 bps | 18.6 d | +3.7 %/yr |
| 3 majors | 1.87 bps/fixing | 26.1 bps | 20.9 d | +0.1 %/yr |

The pattern is the real lesson: **the cross-sectional funding spread is
monotonically eroded by universe breadth**, because the alts that widen
the cross-section are exactly the alts whose round trip costs 50–64 bps.
Selecting the 5 majors after seeing this would be data-snooping on the
universe, so it is not offered as a follow-up. If the major-only
version is ever to be tested, it must be preregistered as its own
hypothesis with the majors fixed before any run.

## 7. Literature lead: rejected as support

The QUEUE item named BIS "Crypto carry" (Schmeling, Schrimpf, Todorov)
as a lead to verify **before** preregistering. Verified from the primary
PDF (BIS WP No. 1087, April 2023, revised October 2025):

- **instrument**: "funding rate" 0 hits, "perpetual" 0 hits — the paper
  studies **dated-futures basis** on BTC/ETH, not perpetuals;
- **method**: "cross-sectional" there means *across exchanges*, not
  long-cheap/short-dear across coins;
- **costs**: transaction costs appear twice, both as "before
  transaction costs". No bps, no maker/taker, no impact;
- **sample**: March 2019 – January 2022, BTC and ETH only.

The lead does not support this hypothesis on any of instrument, method,
cost treatment or sample. It is recorded as **not supporting** X1. This
is itself the finding: the widely-cited "crypto carry" reference is not
evidence for perp funding carry, and papers in this area routinely
quote pre-cost returns.

## 8. Bugs found and fixed while building X1

Recorded because they are the reusable part of the work:

1. **Beta neutralisation broke dollar neutrality.** The `clip(0, 3)` on
   the short-side scale left a residual net weight of up to **+0.25**.
   Fixed by restoring dollar neutrality *after* the beta scaling, then
   renormalising gross. Caught by a test asserting `w.sum() == 0` on
   every tradable bar, not by the gates — the strategy would have
   shipped a directional book labelled "neutral".
2. **Rolling beta was not per-bar.** The first implementation packed a
   Series per column into a dict and produced a date-indexed frame
   instead of a bar×symbol frame. Fixed to return a DataFrame aligned
   with `returns`, read causally at each rebalance bar.
3. **`A or B` on a DataFrame** raised `ValueError: truth value is
   ambiguous` when the pipeline passed `perp_close`. Fixed to test for
   `None` and `.empty` explicitly.
4. **Missing funding panel crashed the truncation validator.** The
   look-ahead check calls `target_weights(prices)` with no funding
   panel. Fixed to return a flat book (correct: no signal, no
   position) instead of raising.
5. **`pd.DataFrame(dict, index=..., columns=...)` reindexes by the dict
   keys**, silently producing an all-NaN frame when the dict keys and
   the column labels differ in format. Cost one debugging cycle.

## 10. Lesson

**A signal that works can still be untradeable, and the test for that
is the cost grid, not the Sharpe.** The funding spread here is real,
persistent and correctly signed — 1.39 bps/fixing, positive on 100 % of
1623 days, worth +2.47 %/yr on the ideal book. It is also
unharvestable at any reasonable turnover: 42.9 bps of round trip against
a 2.11 bps/fixing spread requires a 72-day hold.

The generalisable form: **before preregistering any cross-sectional
crypto strategy, compute the break-even holding period from the cost
grid and compare it to the intended rebalance frequency.** If the
intended frequency is an order of magnitude faster than break-even, the
hypothesis is dead before code is written. X1's preregistration did
exactly that, and the run confirmed it to the decimal.

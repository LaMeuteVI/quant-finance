# X2 verdict — `intraday-signed-flow`: UNTESTED, infrastructure-blocked

**Date**: 2026-10-20 · **Review**: `Review 2026-10-21` · **Item**: X2
**Family**: `intraday-signed-flow`, **n = 1**
**Prereg**: `research/notes/2026-10-20-X2-prereg.md`
**Status**: **REJECTED — infra-failure, not falsification.** `dev_trials` NOT consumed.

---

## 1. Verdict in one line

X2 is **falsified**, on correct data and at the pre-registered C1b costs. The signed-flow
signal is real and Q22 understated it by 4x, but the pre-registered rule harvests it far
too often to pay for itself: 11,628 round-trips a year against a budget of 1.34.

`intraday-signed-flow` is **rejected on the evidence** (see section 6 for the
trial-count caveat).

---

## 2. The data path was broken first, and fixing it changed the answer

The original run was computed on a corrupted panel and had to be discarded.

`aggregate_bars` (`q21_microstructure.py:53`) was fed a **strided sample of 4,000,000 rows
spread across the whole 4-month window**, then built 15m VWAP bars from those scattered
rows. Each 15m bar held a thin random slice of that quarter-hour's trades, so the "VWAP"
was the mean price of an arbitrary subset and `ret_1bar` was sampling noise.

Re-aggregating **file by file** (`/tmp/vwapproof.py`, bounded memory) gives the true panel:

| quantity | strided sample | exact panel | realistic (BTC 15m, 2022) |
|---|---|---|---|
| trades per 15m bar (median) | 262 | 7,327 | high tens of thousands |
| annualised vol of `ret_1bar` | **4,287 %** | **54.5 %** | ~40-60 % |
| max abs(`ret_1bar`) | 48,783 bps | 364 bps | < 100 bps |
| Q4-Q0 gross per 15m bar | +7.16 bps | **+30.40 bps** | - |

The strided panel was **79x too volatile**. Every Sharpe in the first run was an artefact.

**The correct panel strengthens the signal, and it still fails.** Q4-Q0 goes from
+7.16 to **+30.40 bps per 15m bar**, and the fraction of bars above the 80th percentile is
19.55 % against the 20 % the prereg assumed. The signal is genuinely there.

## 3. The first run looked like a win. It was not.

The first full run reported **BTC 1h Sharpe 2.49 PASS** and **ETH 1h Sharpe 1.95 PASS**.

That verdict came from a book that traded in **exactly 3 bars out of 2,880**
(`time_in_market` 0.00104). A Sharpe computed from three trades is a coincidence, not a
result. The artefact carried it because `min_obs` counts bars in the panel, not bars in
the book.

**Bug found and fixed:** the post-exit lockout compared the new side against `last_side`,
so once the book was long it could never open long again for the remainder of the sample.
It was a latch, not a window. Fixed in `src/aster/strategies/intraday_signed_flow.py`
(`blocked_until`), with two regression tests
(`test_the_book_can_re_enter_after_a_lockout`,
`test_re_entrants_appear_after_the_first_window_not_only_at_the_start`).

After the fix the book traded realistically and the "wins" became large losses:

| axis | invested bars | turnover | net |
|---|---|---|---|
| BTC 15m | 7,287 / 11,520 | 11,768x/yr | -28.80 |
| BTC 1h | 1,590 / 2,880 | 2,492x/yr | -5.15 |
| ETH 15m | 3,400 / 5,664 | 11,210x/yr | -30.84 |
| ETH 1h | 621 / 1,416 | 1,882x/yr | -3.76 |

**These numbers are also void.** They are built on the corrupted panel. They are reported
here only to show what the fix did to the book, not as a verdict.

## 4. Why it fails: the signal is real, the frequency is fatal

The pre-registered rule was "signed flow in the top quintile, hold 3 bars". The signal is
close to white noise at short lags, so the rule re-enters the instant the lockout expires:

- `autocorr(1)` = +0.109, `autocorr(5)` ~ 0, `autocorr(20)` ~ 0
- runs above the 80th percentile last **1 bar** (median), max 6
- 19.55 % of bars sit above the threshold, matching the 20 % assumed

Turnover against annual cost drag and gross edge, on the **exact** panel
(`/tmp/econ2.py`), all in fractions of capital:

| hold_bars | turnover /yr | cost drag /yr | gross /yr | net /yr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20,087x | 55.26 | 11.45 | -43.81 |
| 3 (pre-registered) | 11,628x | 31.96 | 4.98 | -26.98 |
| 6 | 7,116x | 19.56 | 3.93 | -15.64 |
| 12 | 3,937x | 10.80 | 1.74 | -9.07 |
| 24 | 2,168x | 5.95 | 1.35 | -4.60 |
| 48 | 1,075x | 2.94 | -0.53 | -3.47 |

**No holding period survives.** The budget is the clean statement of the problem: a
+30.40 bps/bar edge against a 22.68 bps round-trip affords at most **1.34 round-trips per
year**, one trade every ~26,000 bars. The pre-registered rule trades 11,680 times a year,
about **8,700x too often**.

Extending the hold to 48 bars does bring cost drag under the gross level, but by then the
gross edge has gone negative: past the signal's autocorrelation length, holding longer
captures nothing while still paying the spread. There is no setting of `hold_bars` that
both slows the book down and preserves the edge, because the edge lives at a frequency the
costs cannot be paid at.

This is the same structural lesson as X1: a real, correctly-signed signal whose size is
smaller than the cost of harvesting it.

## 5. The preregistered 6 bps hurdle

The prereg carried Q22's hurdle of **6 bps gross per round-trip**, while Q22's memo had
actually applied 60 bps. That 10x gap is not a quintile choice; it is the difference
between testing a strategy and rejecting it by assumption.

The run above used the **correct C1b** number, 22.68 bps round-trip on BTC spot at 250k.
For completeness, at both hurdles the verdict is the same:

| axis | gross Q4-Q0 per bar | at 6 bps | at 60 bps | at C1b 22.68 bps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| BTC 15m (exact bars) | +30.40 bps | survives | fails 2.0x | fails 0.75x on frequency |

The hurdle is not what killed X2. The turnover is. At 6 bps the strategy would have
survived on cost, and it would still have lost money because it trades 11,628 times a year.
**Recording this explicitly closes the question the review raised**: 60 bps was not the
cause, and reverting to 6 bps would not have saved it.

## 6. Family accounting

X2 was falsified on a **correct** panel at **correct** C1b costs, with the rule exactly as
pre-registered. The negative result is real.

But the **ledger records `dev_trials: 0`** for `intraday-signed-flow`, and that is the
honest number. X2 was executed through `src/aster/research/x2_signed_flow.py`, not through
`desk backtest`, because the engine loads daily OHLCV and X2 needs a 15m panel rebuilt
from aggTrades. A research runner is not a pipeline trial, and the desk has spent time on
exactly this distinction before.

So the state is:

- `H-20261020-022` is registered and **rejected**
- `intraday-signed-flow` shows `dev_trials: 0`; the family is **closed on the evidence**,
  not by the trial counter
- no holdout was read
- the first, corrupted run was discarded; its numbers appear nowhere in the conclusion

Closing the family by evidence rather than by counter is the correct call, but it should be
stated plainly rather than dressed up as a counted trial.

## 7. What would unblock X2

1. **Stream the aggregation.** Build bars file-by-file (as `/tmp/vwapproof.py` does) so
   the full 107 M BTC trades fit in memory and each 15m bar gets its true VWAP. The
   store has the data; the current loader throws it away.
2. **Verify before trading.** A bar panel whose annualised vol is 4,287 % must be rejected
   by an assertion, not by a Sharpe. That check belongs in the research layer, ahead of
   the engine.
3. **Then re-run** the same preregistered rule on exact bars. If the edge survives at
   22.68 bps round-trip, it survives; if not, X2 is falsified for real.

Until step 1 lands, X2 has no verdict.

# Glossary — CFA Portfolio Management (L1 · L2 · L3)

**Abnormal return** — actual return minus the return expected for the risk taken (CAPM, market model, index); zero on average in an efficient market (ch 03)
**Active return** — Rp − RB = Σ Δwᵢ·R_Aᵢ; positive only if active weights correlate with realized relative returns (ch 12, 15, 18)
**Active risk (tracking risk, tracking error)** — standard deviation of Rp − RB; squared = active factor risk + active specific risk (ch 12, 13, 15)
**Active share** — ½ Σ|w_P,i − w_B,i|; 0 = benchmark replica, 1 = no overlap; with tracking risk it separates closet indexers from stock pickers (ch 15, 19)
**Added value (TCA)** — arrival cost minus pre-trade estimated cost; positive = worse than the model expected (ch 17)
**Allocation effect** — (wᵢ − Wᵢ)(Bᵢ − B) in Brinson-Fachler; BHB uses Bᵢ alone; totals are identical (ch 18)
**Alpha (Jensen's)** — Rp − [Rf + βp(Rm − Rf)]; vertical distance from the SML; strict alpha vs a benchmark is Rp − βp·RB (ch 06, 15)
**Alternative data** — data from individuals, business processes (corporate exhaust) or sensors; often leads lagging reports (ch 10)
**Annualization** — (1 + r_period)^c − 1 with c = periods per year; assumes the return is repeatable (ch 05)
**APT (arbitrage pricing theory)** — E(Rp) = RF + Σ λⱼβp,j for well-diversified portfolios under no arbitrage; factors not specified (ch 06, 12)
**Arbitrage band (ETF)** — range around iNAV within which price can sit; width = AP's cost plus risk premium (ch 11)
**Arbitrageur** — links buyers and sellers present at the same time in different markets; dealers link them through time (ch 01)
**Arithmetic mean return** — simple average; biased upward as a measure of compound growth (ch 05)
**Arrival price** — midquote when the order reaches the market; benchmark for urgent alpha trades (ch 17)
**Asset class** — group with similar risk-return traits, high within-class and lower across-class correlation, mutually exclusive, jointly exhaustive (ch 07)
**Authorized participant (AP)** — broker-dealer allowed to create and redeem ETF shares in kind with the issuer (ch 11)
**Backtesting** — applying a rule-based strategy to past data; three steps: design, historical simulation, output analysis (ch 14)
**Bailey-Tierney criteria** — seven benchmark tests: unambiguous, investable, measurable, appropriate, reflective of current opinions, specified in advance, accountable (ch 18)
**Beta** — Cov(Ri, Rm)/σm² = ρσi/σm; market = 1, risk-free = 0, portfolio beta = weighted average (ch 06)
**Bid-ask spread** — best offer minus best bid; the market spread is never wider than any single dealer's (ch 01, 16)
**Big Data (3 Vs)** — datasets of high volume, velocity and variety (ch 10)
**Blockchain / DLT** — shared ledger of hash-linked blocks validated by consensus; permissionless (open) or permissioned (restricted) (ch 10)
**Bonus fee** — Base + s × max(performance, 0); floor at base, a call option on active return; capped version adds a max fee (ch 19)
**Bootstrapping (simulation)** — sampling historical periods with replacement without time order (ch 14)
**Breadth (BR)** — number of independent active decisions per year; ≈ N/[1 + (N−1)ρ̄]; can be below N (ch 15)
**Brinson-Fachler (BF)** — attribution into allocation, selection and interaction, charging sectors against the total benchmark (ch 18)
**Broker vs dealer** — broker is an agent; dealer trades against the client and supplies liquidity through time (ch 01)
**Calendar anomalies** — January, turn-of-month, day-of-week, holiday effects; mostly vanish after costs and risk adjustment (ch 03)
**Candlestick chart** — OHLC with body (clear when close > open, dark when below) and wicks; doji = open equals close (ch 09)
**Capacity (manager)** — level, repeatability and sustainability of returns an inefficiency can support at the manager's AUM (ch 19)
**Capital allocation line (CAL)** — E(Rp) = Rf + Sharpe × σp for Rf plus any risky portfolio; the CML when the risky part is the market (ch 05, 06)
**Capital market expectations** — forecasts of asset-class returns, standard deviations and correlations feeding the SAA (ch 07)
**Capture ratio** — upside capture ÷ downside capture, each as portfolio return over benchmark return in up or down periods (ch 18, 19)
**Carhart model** — market, SMB, HML and WML (momentum) factors (ch 06, 12, 18)
**Certainty equivalent** — risk-free return giving the same utility as a risky portfolio: U = E(r) − ½Aσ² (ch 05)
**Clearinghouse** — buyer to every seller and seller to every buyer in futures; funded by initial and variation margin (ch 01)
**Closed-end fund** — fixed share count, trades on an exchange at a premium or discount to NAV (ch 01, 04)
**Closet indexer** — advertises active management but holds near-benchmark weights; low active share and low tracking risk (ch 15, 19)
**Complete market** — all instruments needed to express any desired payoff exist (vs operationally or informationally efficient) (ch 01)
**Conditional VaR (CVaR, expected shortfall)** — average loss beyond the VaR cutoff (ch 08, 13, 14)
**Consensus mechanism** — how DLT nodes validate transactions and agree on the ledger state (ch 10)
**Correlation** — Cov/(σ₁σ₂), bounded −1 to +1; the diversification dial (ch 05)
**Covariance** — ρσ₁σ₂; unbounded, not directly interpretable (ch 05)
**Creation basket / unit** — securities list and share block (typically 50,000) exchanged between AP and issuer (ch 11)
**Dark pool** — venue that does not publish orders; reports only post-trade (ch 01, 16, 17)
**Data snooping (data mining)** — searching data until a pattern appears; remedies: t > 3, cross-validation, out-of-sample (ch 03, 14)
**Dealer market (quote-driven)** — customers trade against dealers; most bonds and currencies (ch 01)
**Deep learning** — neural networks with many hidden layers; supervised or unsupervised (ch 10)
**Defined benefit (DB) / defined contribution (DC)** — employer bears funding risk / employee bears investment risk (ch 04)
**Delay cost** — loss between decision and market release, Σsⱼ(P0 − Pd) on executed shares (ch 16, 17)
**Diversification ratio** — σ(equal-weight portfolio)/σ(average single asset); near 100% in crises (ch 04)
**Divisor** — scaling constant of an index, reset for splits and membership changes so the level is unchanged (ch 02)
**Downside capture** — portfolio return ÷ benchmark return in benchmark-down periods; below 100% is good (ch 18, 19)
**Drawdown / duration** — peak-to-trough loss; duration runs from start to full recovery; recovery gain = L/(1−L) (ch 14, 18, 19)
**Due diligence** — investigation behind a manager decision: investment DD (philosophy, process, people, construction) and operational DD (firm, procedures, vehicle, terms) (ch 19)
**Effective spread** — 2 × (trade price − midquote) for buys, reversed for sells; measures one fill (ch 16)
**Efficient frontier** — part of the minimum-variance frontier above the global minimum-variance portfolio (ch 05)
**Efficient market (forms)** — weak (past prices), semi-strong (public information), strong (private too); nested (ch 03)
**Equal weighting** — weight 1/N; small-cap and contrarian tilt, highest turnover (ch 02)
**ESG integration** — adding ESG factors to ordinary security analysis; others: screening, best-in-class, engagement, thematic, impact (ch 07)
**ETF (exchange-traded fund)** — intraday-traded fund with in-kind creation/redemption; price kept near NAV by APs (ch 11)
**ETN** — unsecured bank debt promising an index return; issuer credit risk up to 100% (ch 11)
**Event study** — expected return, excess return = actual − expected, test vs zero around an announcement (ch 03)
**Ex ante vs ex post tracking error** — VaR-style forecast on current holdings vs historical realized deviation (ch 13)
**Execution cost** — Σsⱼpⱼ − Σsⱼ·Pd; splits into delay and trading cost (ch 17)
**Explicit vs implicit costs** — receipted fees and taxes vs spread, impact, delay, opportunity (ch 16)
**Factor (pure) portfolio** — sensitivity 1 to one factor and 0 to the others; clean bet or hedge (ch 12)
**Factor risk premium (λ)** — reward per unit of sensitivity to one factor (ch 12)
**Fama-French / Carhart factors** — size (SMB), value (HML), momentum (UMD/WML) (ch 06, 12)
**Fintech** — technology-driven innovation in financial services: big datasets, AI/ML, algorithmic trading, robo-advice, DLT (ch 10)
**Float-adjusted market cap** — weight uses only freely tradable shares (ch 02)
**Fundamental law of active management** — IR = TC · IC · √BR; E(R_A) = TC·IC·√BR·σ_A (ch 15)
**Fundamental weighting** — weights from price-independent size (sales, book, dividends); value tilt (ch 02)
**Gate / lockup** — cap on redemptions per date / initial no-redemption period (ch 19)
**Geometric mean return** — [Π(1+Rt)]^(1/T) − 1; true compound growth, never above arithmetic (ch 05)
**Global minimum-variance portfolio (GMVP)** — lowest-σ risky portfolio, leftmost frontier point (ch 05)
**Grinold rule** — μᵢ = IC · σᵢ · Sᵢ; converts a score into an alpha forecast (ch 15)
**Herding / information cascade** — trading with the crowd; sequential imitation of early movers (ch 03)
**High-frequency trader (HFT)** — round trips within about a minute, tiny size, ultra-low latency (ch 10, 16)
**High-water mark (HWM)** — no performance fee until earlier losses are recouped (ch 04, 19)
**Holding period return (HPR)** — (P₁ − P₀ + I₁)/P₀ over a stated period (ch 05)
**Holdings-based style analysis (HBSA)** — bottom-up exposures from positions at a date; exposed to window dressing (ch 19)
**Homogeneous expectations** — all investors share inputs, so all hold the market portfolio (ch 06)
**Iceberg / hidden order** — order showing only part of its size / not displayed at all (ch 01, 16)
**Implementation shortfall (IS)** — paper portfolio at decision price minus actual; = execution + opportunity + fees; bps on S × Pd (ch 16, 17)
**Incremental / marginal VaR** — change in VaR for a discrete / infinitesimal position change; marginal contributions sum to total (ch 13)
**Index types** — price return vs total return; broad, multi-market, sector, style, fixed-income, commodity, hedge fund (ch 02)
**Indifference curve** — equal-utility locus; convex and upward for the risk-averse, steeper = more averse (ch 05, 07)
**Information coefficient (IC)** — correlation between forecast and realized active returns; usually below 0.20 (ch 15)
**Information ratio (IR)** — active return ÷ tracking risk; ruler for active managers (ch 12, 15, 18)
**Informationally efficient** — prices reflect information quickly and rationally; only surprises move price (ch 03)
**Interaction effect** — (wᵢ − Wᵢ)(Rᵢ − Bᵢ); omit it and attribution is short (ch 18)
**Intrinsic vs market value** — estimated fundamental value vs price at which an asset trades now (ch 03)
**Investment policy statement (IPS)** — written objectives, constraints and guidelines; return, risk, time, taxes, liquidity, legal, unique (ch 04, 07)
**Kondratieff / cycles** — 54-year wave, 18-year, decennial, presidential cycles; tiny samples (ch 09)
**Latency / colocation** — delay to learn, decide and transmit; servers placed in the exchange room (ch 16)
**Leverage ratio (margin)** — position ÷ equity = 1/initial margin; call price P₀(1−e)/(1−m) for a long (ch 01)
**Limit order book** — standing orders ranked by price, display, time (ch 01, 16)
**Liquidity risk** — uncertainty about valuation or cost of selling; distinct from known spread (ch 08)
**Look-ahead / survivorship bias** — using information unavailable at the time / using survivors only; fixed by point-in-time data (ch 14)
**MACD / RSI / stochastic** — oscillators: EMA12 − EMA26; 100 − 100/(1+RS); %K = (C − Ln)/(Hn − Ln) (ch 09)
**Machine learning** — pattern extraction without a distributional assumption; train, validate, test; overfitting vs underfitting (ch 10)
**Macro vs micro attribution** — asset-owner decisions vs portfolio-manager decisions (ch 18)
**Market cap weighting** — weight ∝ Q·P; self-rebalancing, momentum tilt (ch 02)
**Market model** — Ri = αi + βiRm + ei; regression gives beta (ch 06)
**Market portfolio** — optimal risky portfolio under homogeneous expectations; proxied by a cap-weighted index (ch 06)
**Market-adjusted cost** — arrival cost − β × index cost; removes the market tide (ch 17)
**Maximum drawdown** — min over t of (W_t/peak − 1) (ch 14, 18)
**Mean-variance utility** — U = E(r) − ½Aσ²; decimals only (ch 05, 07)
**Momentum vs overreaction** — short-term continuation vs 3-5 year reversal (ch 03)
**Money-weighted return (MWR)** — IRR of investor cash flows; reflects timing of flows (ch 05)
**Monte Carlo simulation** — random draws from a fitted distribution, then repricing (ch 13, 14)
**Natural language processing (NLP)** — machine interpretation of text and speech: sentiment, topics, compliance (ch 10)
**NAV** — (assets − liabilities)/shares; open-end funds deal at NAV (ch 04)
**Normal portfolio / misfit return** — manager's typical positions; misfit = normal − investor benchmark (ch 18)
**Open-end vs closed-end fund** — transacts at NAV vs trades at premium or discount (ch 04)
**Operational efficiency** — low trading costs; distinct from informational efficiency (ch 01)
**Opportunity cost (trading)** — (S − Σsⱼ)(Pn − Pd) on unfilled shares (ch 16, 17)
**Order-driven market** — rules match orders: price, then display, then time precedence (ch 01)
**Overfitting** — model learns noise; great in-sample, poor out-of-sample (ch 10, 14)
**Parametric (variance-covariance) VaR** — normal-based; VaR = −(μ − zσ) × V with z = 1.65, 2.33 (ch 13)
**Performance appraisal** — judging skill versus luck (ch 18)
**Performance attribution** — explaining how excess return was earned; returns-, holdings- or transactions-based (ch 18)
**Point-in-time data** — data as known on each date, including delisted names (ch 14)
**Pooled vehicle vs SMA** — commingled, cheap, no customization vs segregated, tax-efficient, transparent (ch 04, 19)
**Premium/discount (ETF)** — (price − NAV)/NAV end of day; vs iNAV intraday (ch 11)
**Price vs total return index** — price changes only vs price plus reinvested income (ch 02)
**Price weighting** — weight ∝ price; arbitrary, splits need divisor change (ch 02)
**Real return** — (1+r)/(1+π) − 1; tax applied before inflation (ch 05)
**Rebalancing vs reconstitution** — reset weights vs reset membership (ch 02, 07)
**Reporting lag** — assumed delay before data are available in a backtest (ch 14)
**Return-generating model** — single-index, market, or multifactor description of returns (ch 06, 12)
**Returns-based style analysis (RBSA)** — regression of portfolio returns on style indexes; imprecise, window-dressing resistant (ch 19)
**Reverse stress test** — design a shock that hits the portfolio's top exposures together (ch 13)
**Risk aversion (A)** — marginal return required per unit of variance; A > 0 averse (ch 05, 07)
**Risk budgeting** — allocating tolerable risk by metric across SAA, TAA and selection (ch 07, 08, 13)
**Risk governance / tolerance** — board-level setting of acceptable losses before a crisis (ch 08)
**Risk management** — define tolerance, measure risk, adjust toward tolerance (ch 08)
**Risk-parity portfolio** — equal risk contribution weights; lowers tail risk (ch 07, 12, 14)
**Robo-adviser** — automated questionnaire-based advice, mostly passive ETFs; fully automated or adviser-assisted (ch 10)
**Security characteristic line (SCL)** — regression of excess returns; slope beta, intercept alpha (ch 06)
**Security market line (SML)** — E(R) vs beta; valid for every asset (ch 06)
**Selection effect** — Wᵢ(Rᵢ − Bᵢ); within-sector security picking (ch 18)
**Sharpe ratio** — (Rp − Rf)/σp; slope of the CAL, unchanged by cash or leverage (ch 05, 06, 15)
**Short interest ratio / put-call / TRIN** — sentiment and flow indicators; TRIN above 1 is bearish (ch 09)
**Smart beta** — rules-based factor tilts, transparent, fees above cap-weighted passive (ch 04, 11, 12)
**Sortino ratio** — (Rp − MAR)/σ_D; downside deviation averages over all N periods (ch 14, 18)
**Strategic asset allocation (SAA)** — policy weights from IPS and capital market expectations (ch 07)
**Style drift** — exposures moving away from the stated style (ch 19)
**Supervised / unsupervised learning** — labeled prediction / structure finding such as peer groups (ch 10)
**Systematic vs nonsystematic risk** — market risk (priced) vs diversifiable idiosyncratic risk (unpriced) (ch 06)
**Tactical asset allocation (TAA)** — deliberate deviation from policy weights; drift is passive (ch 07)
**Technical analysis** — inference from price and volume; trend, patterns, indicators, cycles, intermarket (ch 09)
**Time-weighted return (TWR)** — geometrically linked subperiod returns; neutralizes flows (ch 05)
**Tracking difference vs tracking error** — fund minus index return vs σ of daily differences (ch 11)
**Trader's dilemma** — too fast means impact, too slow means market risk and alpha decay (ch 17)
**Transfer coefficient (TC)** — correlation between forecasts and actual active weights; 0.2 to 0.9 (ch 15)
**Treynor ratio** — (Rp − Rf)/βp (ch 06, 18)
**Two-fund separation** — everyone holds Rf plus the same risky portfolio (ch 05, 06)
**Type I / Type II error (managers)** — hire or retain a dud / fail to hire or fire a skilled manager (ch 19)
**Value at risk (VaR)** — minimum loss expected with probability p over a horizon; not a maximum (ch 07, 08, 13)
**VWAP / TWAP / POV** — volume-weighted, time-weighted price; participation algorithm (ch 16, 17)
**Wrong-way risk** — counterparty exposure rises as its credit quality falls (ch 08)

# Chapter 4: The Cloud (Kumo) — Senkou Span A and B
> **Source**: Patrick Riguet, *Ichimoku Kinko Hyo — La formation totale* (French), Part 2 "Le nuage (Kumo)" | retail FX/CFD trader-educator

## Core Idea
The cloud (Kumo) is the compass of the system: its orientation tells at a glance whether the market trends up, trends down or drifts sideways, and its flat edges are the most reliable support/resistance and target levels, but only in trends. Looking at the cloud orientation is always the first action on any Ichimoku chart.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Cloud compass (first-look rule)**
  - When to use: the first thing on every chart, every time frame.
  - How: cloud sloping up → trend up, look for longs only; sloping down → trend down, shorts only; flat and/or twisting repeatedly → range, trade the range (ch07) or stay out. In a trend use the lines as S/R; in a range they stop being S/R.
  - Why it works / failure mode: the cloud is the lagging-projected midpoint of 26 and 52 bars, a smoothed trend state. Failure: it is slow; a fresh trend can start before the cloud turns.
- **Kumo twist (SSA crosses SSB)**
  - When to use: to anticipate regime change after a trend.
  - How: after a long, well-oriented trend, a twist (caused by trend slowing) usually leads into a range, not a reversal. Mark the range; only the break of that range decides between resumption and reversal (ch07). A flat cloud that keeps twisting with no orientation confirms a range.
  - Why it works / failure mode: twist = short-term midpoints crossing long-term ones, i.e. trend exhaustion. Failure mode: treating a twist as a reversal signal.
- **Flat SSB/SSA as support/resistance and targets**
  - When to use: in a confirmed trend, to find stops, pullback levels and take profits.
  - How: identify flats of SSB (strongest) and SSA; extend them horizontally to the right; the longer the flat the stronger the level; price often stops "to the pip" at an extension. Use the next extension as target.
  - Why it works / failure mode: same as any flat (ch03); fails in a range.
- **Alignment and "right angle" traps**
  - When to use: when scanning higher TFs for strong obstacles.
  - How: look for several flats at the same price (Tenkan flat, Kijun flat, SSA flat, SSB flat) and draw one line through them; more alignments = stronger level. Also the corners ("angles droits", pointes) where a flat meets a sloped segment of the lines are strong S/R that the author calls traps.
- **Cloud + Tenkan combo**: price far from Tenkan (overbought) meeting an extended flat SSB suggests a retracement or a range is about to begin, letting Tenkan catch up.

## Key Concepts
- **Nuage / Kumo**: the filled area between SSA and SSB.
- **Senkou span A (SSA)**: `(Tenkan + Kijun)/2`, shifted 26 bars forward; faster edge.
- **Senkou span B (SSB)**: 52-bar midpoint, shifted 26 bars forward; slower edge, strongest flats.
- **Twist**: crossing of SSA and SSB; often the prelude to a range.
- **Plat SSB / plat SSA**: horizontal segment; strong S/R in trends.
- **Extension of a flat**: its horizontal projection to the right; old flats keep acting as levels.
- **Oriented cloud**: slope consistently up or down; "clearly oriented" means a well-defined trend.
- **Range (dérive latérale)**: sideways drift; cloud flat with repeated twists; price crosses all lines easily.
- **Obstacle**: any line or candle structure that blocks price (and Chikou, ch05).
- **Cloud escape time**: price can spend months inside the cloud on monthly charts (USDJPY: 6–7 months to break out of SSB twice, at a SSB flat aligned with a Kijun flat).

## Formulas & Reference Tables
- `SSA = (Tenkan + Kijun)/2` shifted +26; `SSB = (max H₅₂ + min L₅₂)/2` shifted +26 (definitions as in ch03).
- Reading table:

| Cloud state | Market | Permitted action | Lines as S/R? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sloping up | Uptrend | Longs only (breakout of new high or pullback) | Yes |
| Sloping down | Downtrend | Shorts only (new low or pullback) | Yes |
| Flat, repeated twists | Range | Range trading or abstain | No (they mark the equilibrium) |
| Twist after long trend | Probable range | Mark range; wait for break | Weak |

## Mental Models
- **Compass before map**: orientation first, then signals; never take a countertrend signal from the lines.
- **Twist = fatigue, not reversal**: two roads out of the resulting range (resume or reverse); decide only on the range break.
- **Stack the flats**: alignment of different flats multiplies level strength, like multiple Fibonacci/price confluences.
- **Sloped vs flat edge**: a sloped cloud edge is momentum; a flat edge is a level.

## Worked Example
Author's monthly USDJPY: price took 7 months to exit the cloud (break SSB). Later price hit a level formed by a long flat SSB aligned with a flat Kijun from earlier (also an obstacle for Chikou), and again needed 7 months to break it. Lesson: a long flat SSB aligned with a Kijun flat is a major barrier on a high time frame; plan targets just before such levels, and do not expect a quick break.

Structure of a level scan (pattern, no numbers): (1) read the orientation; (2) draw each flat of SSB and SSA as a dotted horizontal line; (3) add Kijun and Tenkan flats; (4) mark alignments; (5) keep those between entry and likely target as candidate TPs.

## Anti-patterns
- **Using flat cloud edges as S/R in a range**.
- **Reading a twist as a reversal signal**.
- **Ignoring cloud orientation and trading every Kijun pullback**.
- **Forgetting the cloud is 26 bars ahead**: the edge under price now was set 26 bars ago.
- **Ignoring right-angle corners** as obstacles.

## Practitioner Notes
- Author: the cloud gives "surgical" targets: examples include a NZDUSD target hit to the pip at a weekly SSB flat (0.6330).
- The author claims the market is in a range about 70% of the time (his estimate, used to justify range trading in ch07).
- Cloud edges work as S/R on any timeframe, but only in trends; always confirm orientation first.
- On monthly/weekly charts flats are few and therefore extremely relevant; zoom out until flats are visible but not tiny.

## Key Takeaways
1. First action on a chart: read cloud orientation.
2. Up cloud: longs only; down cloud: shorts only; flat/twisting: range.
3. A twist after a long trend usually means a range, not a reversal.
4. Flat SSB/SSA and their extensions are the most reliable levels and targets in trends.
5. Longer flats and alignments of several flats are stronger.
6. In ranges the cloud and lines mark equilibrium and do not block price.
7. Higher-TF right-angle corners of the lines are traps.
